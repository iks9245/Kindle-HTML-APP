‘Stunning’ Percolation Proof Solves Decades-Old Puzzle About Phase Transitions

Benjamini and Schramm suspected that a version of the sharpness conjecture was true on all infinite transitive graphs. That conjecture could be broken down into two separate problems. The “subcritical” half — addressing what happens below the critical point — was completed in 2007, by Tonći Antunović and Ivan Veselić. Their work showed that here, pools of fluid are tiny and far apart. Even a hair below the critical point, the system looks more like Arizona than Minnesota.

The “supercritical” half of the conjecture — which deals with probabilities above the critical threshold — seemed harder. Here, the landscape should be made up of possibly many seas, each infinitely large. In this scenario, large pools that are not connected to the infinite seas become exceedingly rare. That’s because a large, isolated pool can only stay separate if there is a lot of dry land — or closed edges — around it.
