In praise of the dilettante

Dilettantism rejects this quasi-capitalist mindset. The petty territorialism common to social, academic and cultural spaces (think record stores, cocktail bars, art galleries, philosophy classes, etc) is unnecessary but, more importantly, at odds with a genuine interest in the thing itself. A crucial part of the switch from deficiency to abundance is learning to see the dilettante as an asset to a given practice rather than a threat to it. Even Adorno, snob par excellence, allows a version of this, arguing that the expert critic needs the dilettante around ‘in order to prove to himself that he isn’t one’.

More than simply escaping sickness, the dilettante’s movement is a headlong rush at vitality and vigour
