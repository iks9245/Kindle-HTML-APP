Paternity is poetical

There are other ways to narrate poetic breakthroughs, of course. Coleridge’s development as a poet is often recounted – most powerfully by Adam Nicolson, in The Making of Poetry (2019) – as bound up with his friendship with Wordsworth, so that the two men together pioneered what we now think of as Romanticism. A pattern is not a recipe, and each of these examples has its own complications in the granular details. But the pattern is substantial enough – and counterintuitive enough – to be worth remarking upon. It is also supported by recent developments in neuroscience. As a field, neuroscience has been transformed by neuroimaging technologies: particularly functional MRIs, developed in the early 1990s, which permit researchers to track brain activity by measuring changes in blood flow. This has led to notable insights in two research fields: into creativity, and into fatherhood. Strikingly, the two may be linked.
