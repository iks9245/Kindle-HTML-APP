Some of those who returned to the red zone found buildings reduced to charred, empty shells; in other cases, homes had been looted. The scale of destruction in some of these places, Bohackova said, is "breathtaking".

Others were rushing back for reasons beyond material concerns. Angela Dobrovolska's husband Serhiy was killed two years ago by a grenade dropped by a piloted Russian drone - an instance of a "human safari" attack of the kind that Kherson has become sadly infamous for.

On Thursday, Angela headed back from Kyiv to Kherson to visit his grave for the first time in months. "They say that all the people have rushed to Kherson," she said. "And I'll be going to the cemetery to my husband."
