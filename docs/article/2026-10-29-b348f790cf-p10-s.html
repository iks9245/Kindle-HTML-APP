Johnson observed that after she added phosphate to the liquid silk dope to start the condensation process, side groups on two amino acids (arginine and tyrosine) in the intrinsically disordered regions of the spidroin proteins started to interact weakly with one another. This interaction displaced water, organizing the disordered proteins into a denser, gel-like material — a condensate. The interaction persisted in the solid fiber, suggesting that the amino acids serve as “molecular stickers” that guide the disordered proteins into condensates and ultimately into fibers.

Dennis Kunkel Microscopy/Science Photo Library

Others on the team used AlphaFold, an artificial intelligence tool that models protein structures, and molecular dynamics software to predict this process computationally. Researchers know that spider silk’s strength comes from the sturdy beta-sheet protein structures in its final fiber. The team’s simulations showed that these amino acids line up right up at the bends in the beta sheets, suggesting that they are helping to guide the spindroins into their final structure.
