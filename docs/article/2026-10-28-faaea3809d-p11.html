How to build real peace

A form of self-determination oriented towards the autonomy of all peoples, particularly women, democratic confederalism proposes a structure of governance organised from the bottom up. In Öcalan’s paradigm, cooperatives are the means through which an economy in service of these principles – communal, ecological, feminist – might be built. The cooperatives that interest me, therefore, have three defining characteristics: they are part of broader social movements and territorial struggles; they prioritise meeting collective needs over the accumulation of capital; and they challenge not just economic exploitation but also the patriarchy, racism, colonialism and statism entwined with capitalism. Such cooperatives are the building blocks of what I call an ‘anticapitalist peace’ – a peace not satisfied with the silencing of arms, nor with positive peace that doesn’t challenge capitalist logic, but one that transforms the economic relations at the root of violence. I have encountered two cases that demonstrate this concretely, in two different parts of the world.
