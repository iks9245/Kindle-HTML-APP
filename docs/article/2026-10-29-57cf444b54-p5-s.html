High-speed train crash kills at least 68 people in Spain

Spain's Civil Guard said it would need DNA samples from relatives to identify some victims.

"The whole of Spain is grieving", Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said during a visit to the crash site. "Lives have been shattered, families have been torn apart, the pain is immense."

Most political parties in Spain cancelled their campaign events scheduled for Wednesday.

The exception was the far-right party Vox. Its leader, Santiago Abascal, said his party would not take part in what he described as attempts to "use mourning to silence criticism" of the country's rail infrastructure.

Spain was already reeling from a high-speed rail disaster near Córdoba in January that killed 46 people. It happened on the same rail line, but much further south.

The prime minister said it was "unacceptable" for Spain to have two serious train crashes in one year, and vowed to "get to the bottom of this" and determine if there was any negligence.
