Using friction as a tool has come to us only as we’ve evolved to understand the depths of friction, including its behaviours and where it may be lurking. We know it is involved in dynamic processes, so let’s use it to control such processes. Yes, we’ve done so for a while with mechanisms like brakes and dampeners, but expanding this beyond machines has really taken hold in recent decades. Now, we’re looking at how friction may be a tool in perhaps the most impressive machine there is: the human body. It’s possible friction may be a lever to help us better model and understand biological processes and, in turn, help in the development of therapeutics. The rate at which a virus ejects from its protein shell or capsid can dictate whether it results in an active infection or instead remains dormant in the cell. If we want to use viruses to intentionally infect and defeat other viruses or bacteria, then controlling the virus’s ejection rate is crucial. Friction is the key to unlocking this possibility. In 2023, researchers found that, by packing the coils of virus DNA in certain ways, they could increase friction during ejection and slow down its rate of ejection.
