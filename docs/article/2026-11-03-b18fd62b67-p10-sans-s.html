The main difference in these exotic atoms is that the electron substitutes are much heavier. A muon is 207 times as massive as an electron; an antiproton, 1,800 times. According to quantum mechanics, every object is ultimately wavelike, and the greater its mass, the shorter its wavelength. Consequently, a muonic atom is as much as 207 times smaller than its vanilla counterpart, depending on the chemical isotope; an antiprotonic atom, as much as 1,800 times smaller. When physicists want to get across the scale of atoms, they often compare the nucleus to a marble and the whole atom to a football stadium around that marble. With antiprotonic atoms, however, the atom is not a football stadium, but just a football.

“The whole system is collapsed down,” said Nancy Paul, a physicist at the Kastler Brossel Laboratory in Paris. She is exploring using even heavier negatively charged particles to shrink atoms still further. “It’s so rare in physics, especially nowadays, where you get something where nothing has been done.”
