The ghetto on the lagoon

It was not the first time that Jews had ever been confined to a separate quarter, of course. By the early 16th century, Jews in France and in German lands had already been living in designated neighbourhoods, physically distinct from the surrounding areas, for close on 300 years. Nor was it even the first time Venice itself had toyed with the idea of Jewish confinement. Back in the 13th century, it had already obliged Jews in its Cretan colony’s capital Candia (now Heraklion) to live in a separate area.
