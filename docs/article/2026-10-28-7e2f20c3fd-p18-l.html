Love with nowhere to go

Those whose grief is disenfranchised because their losses are ambiguous don’t just lack scripts or rituals or traditions. They lack the interpretative resources to understand and share significant aspects of their experience. When we lack the concepts to name what it is like to be us, what it is we are going through, we suffer what the philosopher Miranda Fricker calls a ‘hermeneutical injustice’ – an inability to make our own experience intelligible to ourselves and to others. This is not because we are confused, but because the shared language or conceptual apparatus – the frameworks we use to make sense of ourselves – hasn’t caught up. As two of Ratcliffe and Richardson’s respondents put it: ‘It took me a while to understand that what I was feeling was grief’; ‘I never realised this was grief until I found others in a similar situation.’
