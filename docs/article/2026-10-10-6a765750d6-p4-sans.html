Ward’s diagnosis of Ghana’s predicament emerged from an intellectual trajectory that predated decolonisation itself. She was born in 1914 to a British Quaker-Catholic family that put a strong emphasis on education, encouraging her to attend a French lycée in Paris and later Somerville College, Oxford, where she was among the first women to graduate in politics, philosophy and economics in 1935. She continued to lecture at Oxford and Cambridge, as well as in Catholic circles, until 1939, when she launched her journalistic career as a contributor to The Economist on foreign affairs.

‘For 30 years, it has scarcely been possible to discuss development anywhere in the world without bringing in Barbara Ward’
