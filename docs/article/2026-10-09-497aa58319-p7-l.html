A comprehensive systematic review found robust evidence for evening chronotype predominance in ADHD (). Approximately three-quarters of adults who developed ADHD in childhood show objective evidence of phase-delayed circadian rhythms. Biological markers such as dim-light melatonin onset in saliva, core body temperature rhythms, and actigraphically-recorded sleep patterns are typically shifted later by roughly 90 minutes compared to neurotypical adults ().
