小中大切換為無襯線

Is everything conscious?

Instead of maintaining that different things merely have different properties, perhaps we ought to say that they are radically different in their essential attributes. What are ‘essential attributes’? Recall that, according to dualism, reality is bifurcated into mental substance with the essential attribute of thought, and physical substance with the essential attribute of extension. In other words, the mental and the physical have radically different essential attributes. But this raises a new question: why is mental substance not extended, and why is physical substance not thinking? The PSR demands an explanation. One might think that the nature of mental substance excludes being extended, and vice versa. But Spinoza objects that this violates the independence of substance, since it entails a mutual dependence of mental and physical substance to explain the fact that they exclude one another. Ultimately, Spinoza thinks there is no reason why substances would differ from each other and, since there is no reason, they do not differ. There is, then, just one substance, one thing, and this thing has all properties, and is both extended and thinking, because there is no reason that it should lack any properties or essential attributes.
