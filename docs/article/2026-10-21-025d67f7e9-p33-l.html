How intuitions work

Belief-formation is less like drawing a conclusion and more like yielding to a disclosure. When the apprehension is accurate, this default response reliably produces true belief; when it is not, the response is still intelligible and reasonable. Presentational states are epistemically respectable because they are where the world shows up for us: the point at which reality makes its first claim on belief. Intuitions inherit this status. They are simply the presentational events through which we apprehend the conceptual structure of a situation as actual, and in doing so provide a rational basis for belief without the need for any further epistemic mechanism.
