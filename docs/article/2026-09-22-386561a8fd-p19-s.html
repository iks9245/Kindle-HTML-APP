He was probably right

First, science – contrary to what you may have heard – does not run on certainty. Scientific knowledge is not a collection of established facts, it is a provisional consensus of the best-tested theories, each held subject to revision. Thomas Kuhn showed that scientific paradigms shift over history, and Imre Lakatos argued that scientists rightly persist with research programmes despite anomalies they cannot explain, revising peripheral hypotheses before abandoning a theory’s core. What science practises is structurally identical to Cicero’s probabilia: the best current views, held with proportionate confidence, open to revision. Modern Bayesian probability theory (elaborating the theorem Thomas Bayes set out in the 18th century) formalises the same insight: priors updated by evidence, never certainty, always proportionality. The mathematics is new, but the wisdom is not.
