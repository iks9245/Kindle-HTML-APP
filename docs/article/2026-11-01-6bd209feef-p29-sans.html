Exploitation status. Anthropic says Claude wrote the exploit "after initial prompting," that it runs "arbitrary commands as root, without any credentials or user interaction" on servers with a multipath TCP listener, and that in its proof of concept "it takes 48 minutes to leak a kernel pointer and another five minutes to reach the shell command execution." Anthropic hasn't published the exploit. Its disclosure policy gives developers 90 days to patch and a further 45 days before Anthropic discusses details beyond a public advisory. Red Hat's bulletin now says the bug could be exploited "to trigger remote code execution." By 5:15 PM CDT on October 31, 36 GitHub repositories referenced the CVE. Most describe themselves as audit or mitigation tooling, among them Ansible roles and a livepatch for AlmaLinux and Rocky Linux. At least six say they include a race trigger or a proof of concept for the use-after-free; the first was created at 7:22 PM on October 29, and the latest at 11:04 AM on October 31. The CVE isn't in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog as of 5:15 PM CDT. We haven't tested any public proof of concept, and we aren't linking them.
