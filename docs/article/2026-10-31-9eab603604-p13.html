In praise of the dilettante

But due to their idealisation of the ‘true artist’ as a manifester of Truth and Beauty, Goethe and Schiller cannot help but view the dilettante as lacking in both aim and ability. To be fair, no practice is a total free-for-all, and some practices are less permissive of dilettantism than others (who wants to live in a house built by a dilettante architect?). But the notion that the dilettante tries and fails to follow the ‘true objective rules’ of art and beauty the way legit artists do rests on outdated and Eurocentric assumptions about what both artists and dilettantes are actually up to. In other words, take away the neo-classicist presumption, and suddenly the dilettante appears less like an imitator and more like a liberated creator. It’s not that the dilettante falls short in any given practice but, rather, has a different orientation toward it. Indeed, an overly goal-oriented attitude, especially in the realm of pastimes and pleasures, cuts against the very spirit of dilettantism. Part of its appeal is its ethos of open-endedness, its willingness to do something for the sake of delight rather than achievement. The dilettante is comfortable in the garb of an amateur. For the dilettante, every night is karaoke night.
