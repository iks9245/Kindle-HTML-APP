This difficult proximity, where we witness and are witnessed without our protective, polished personas is an immensely powerful change agent. You can see it in action in group therapy, the recovery movement and some short-term intensive self-development programmes, but this powerful ‘technology of the self’, to use Michel Foucault’s phrase, is vanishingly rare. This kind of witnessing (during my housemate’s labour, for example, I witnessed parts of her that only her partner otherwise sees) is effective because change requires us to be honest about who we currently are. Living in community is where illusions about the self go to die!

One of the phrases we use a lot in our household is ‘We are made for each other and we are made by each other.’ Marxists such as Silvia Federici call this ‘social reproduction’. Neurologically, this makes sense: human interaction shapes our brains from our very earliest moments. Every human comes into the world in search of a gaze.
