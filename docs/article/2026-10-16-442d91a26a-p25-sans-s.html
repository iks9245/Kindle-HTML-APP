What is nice about studying these trade patterns is that it allows organisms access to very different materials that they couldn’t get themselves. But there’s a tension, and you used that word, and I think the tension here is quite interesting because when we take an example like plants and their fungi, it’s expensive for plants to photosynthesize and to feed that carbon, and it’s expensive for the fungi to go out and collect these resources.

And so we study it as sort of an underground economy and trying to understand under what conditions do certain trade behaviors evolve? What makes fungi collect resources and give them to a certain plant and vice versa? What makes a plant give more carbon to one fungal network than the other? And we set up these kinds of experiments in the lab, these very manipulative experiments, and then we watch to see what both partners do.
