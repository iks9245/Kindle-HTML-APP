Chasing la dolce vita

In today’s world of hardening borders and changing alliances, challenges lie ahead for diaspora Italians within Italy itself. Rules for claiming Italian citizenship through ancestry or ius sanguinis (‘right of blood’) have been tightened for descendants of emigrants. This route to citizenship is now granted only to people who have a parent or a grandparent with exclusive Italian nationality, or a parent who was a citizen for two years before their birth. A 160-year history of citizenship by descent has been undermined. The question, officially at least, of who is Italian or not will be thrown into further disarray. I know from experience how hard it will be to extricate Italy from the bodies, imaginations and feelings of these figli d’italiani, these sons and daughters dispersed and far away – people who often feel they have earned the right to call themselves Italian, or at least proudly Italo-something, not least because of the stigma and rejection they or their ancestors once encountered.
