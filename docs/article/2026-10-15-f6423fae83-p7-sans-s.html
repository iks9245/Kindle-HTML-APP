Dark matter is only known to act with the gravitational force; there’s no compelling evidence yet that it interacts with other fundamental forces, like electromagnetism. If two typical galaxies met in a head-on collision, the dark matter would experience very little friction, or drag, and would keep on rocketing by. But ordinary matter, which interacts using all four fundamental forces, would get all tangled up.

“An analogy would be two knights jousting, where the knights and their lances collide and may be thrown to the ground in a heap, while their horses keep on running at full speed,” said Aaron Romanowsky, an astronomer at San José State University.
