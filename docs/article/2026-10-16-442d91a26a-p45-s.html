What if they just stopped fixing nitrogen? And so we did this very, I still think it clever, it was in the early 2000s, but to me it’s still incredibly clever, where we forced the bacteria to cheat by growing them in an argon atmosphere. So all of a sudden, the poor bacteria were trying so hard to make nitrogen, but you can’t make nitrogen out of argon, and argon’s just an inert gas, so it didn’t hurt the nodules. And then we watched very patiently to see what was the response of the plant to this cheat. And like you said, that’s exactly what happened, was that the soybean actually suffocated the bacteria that failed to provide nitrogen. It could sense at the level of an individual nodule, how much nitrogen was being provided, and it cut off oxygen. And since then, there have been many other mechanisms that have been discovered, by which how the plants really keep a check on that bacteria from overgrowing the nodule.
