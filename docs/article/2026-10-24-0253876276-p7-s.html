小中大切換為無襯線

Malaria Parasite Caught in the Act of Breaking and Entering

Based on previous research, scientists knew the broad strokes of this process. The parasite approaches a host red blood cell and inserts a ring of protein anchors into the cell’s membrane. Collectively these anchors are known as the moving junction. Plasmodium locks into them with proteins expressed on its surface — and then pushes forward. The moving junction forms a continuous portal in the host cell membrane for the parasite to travel through. Once the parasite is fully inside, the cell membrane seals up behind it. The whole process takes less than a minute.
