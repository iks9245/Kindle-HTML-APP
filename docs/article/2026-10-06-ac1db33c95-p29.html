I posted this as a cautionary anecdote, but some of my readers declined to let it stay one. One refused the heroic frame: I was not brave, Mats said, but rather ‘naive and insensitive’; I could have asked the same legitimate question in a way that sought an answer rather than an audience. I conceded: ‘Yes, it was provocative and foolhardy.’ An American colleague, Richard, turned the story over and read its underside – call it instead ‘How I began my career in Denmark’ – and I had to agree that leaving the Swedish history-of-science milieu was my intellectual salvation. My Danish colleague Jes moved the lens onto vanity itself: gracious answers existed that would have honoured the question without diminishing the questioner, but they require the one quality rarest in academic life, ‘the absence of a dominating vanity’ – and then he caught his own pretension in the act of saying so.
