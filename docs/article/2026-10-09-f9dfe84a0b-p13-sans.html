It worked like a dream. Little by little, the Venetian economy began to recover. The government was so happy, in fact, that just three years later it voted to offer the ebrei Tedeschi a new condotta, allowing them to go on living in Venice for another decade, after the last arrangement expired. And that wasn’t all. The Venetians were so keen to attract more Jewish bankers to the city that the government even decided to set aside a quarter especially for Jews. In the event, nothing came of this – but the intention was clearly to give Jews a more settled status, and to make Venice a more attractive home for Jewish migrants. A bright future seemed to lie ahead.
