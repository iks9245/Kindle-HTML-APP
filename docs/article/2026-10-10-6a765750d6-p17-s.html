Barbara Ward’s vision

Thus, in the mid-1960s, Ward uncritically sought to persuade her audience that only the Christian West could provide the world with the moral foundations of a planetary community. The ‘West’ had a special responsibility, she argued, to provide moral guidance for a new world order based on justice, equality and solidarity. Thus, the Christian West was self-servingly represented as both fraternal and paternal, both equal and superior, and a cultivator of a universal creed that could motivate planetary action. Imagining a ‘planetary community’ that could foster humanity’s freedom and equality was, for Ward, an act of faith.

Alongside economic inequality and planetary politics, the third pillar of Ward’s thought regards humanity’s relations with the natural environment. In the 1970s, she used the term ‘sustainable development’ to argue that economic growth, though essential to human wellbeing, must not come at the cost of natural degradation. She outlined a vision for sustainable growth that reflected her optimism about ending poverty while avoiding a planetary economic and environmental crisis.
