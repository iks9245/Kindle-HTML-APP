And that’s really what drove the field of radio interferometry. We needed to make a picture that was, let’s say, 10,000 times better than anything that we had made before. And the magic of interferometry is that you can detect radio waves from a black hole, let’s say in Chile, and you can time tag them with an atomic clock so you see all the crests and the troughs of these radio waves as they’re coming in. And you can do the same thing in, let’s say Hawai‘i. And then you can bring those waveforms together and compare them as though they had been received by one huge telescope, and they will be coherent. You can play them back against each other, and you can line up the troughs and the crests. Now this is the secret that allows you to turn the Earth into a telescope.
