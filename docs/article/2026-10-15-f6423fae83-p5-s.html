Triplet of ‘Ghostly’ Galaxies Hints at What Dark Matter Is — and Isn’t

Dark matter, which makes up 27% of the universe — and 85% of all matter —interacts with gravity. If it were down to the gravitational pull of ordinary matter alone, stars on the edges of rapidly spinning galaxies would escape into space. Ordinary matter alone would struggle to clump together tightly enough to form stars. Dark matter also seems to warp space itself, bending light around objects in a process known as gravitational lensing.

“You wouldn’t get galaxies without dark matter,” said Chamkaur Ghag, a physicist at University College London. “Is there a way of evolving the galaxies without dark matter in the first place? Our understanding says no.”
