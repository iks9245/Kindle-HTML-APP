LEVIN: Right. I also wanna return to the idea of the atomic clock, because these atomic clocks are so precise that, what you lose a few seconds over the age of the universe?

DOELEMAN: Uh, well, no, they’re not, they’re not that good. And I’ve been around for most of the age of the universe, so I know. So yeah, so the key thing is that when you record the radio waves at any of the Event Horizon Telescope sites, you need for the signal not to jitter.

It has to be stable so that when you have another one, they line up in just the right way. And if the signal is jittering back and forth because your clock is imperfect, you’ll never be able to line up these waveforms from different parts of the Earth.

So you need a super stable clock. We use hydrogen maser clocks, and these lose about one second every 10 million years or so, and that’s the stability that we need in order to keep everything stable.
