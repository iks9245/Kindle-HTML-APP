We also practised rupture and repair, realising that, even as we saw the most ridiculous, fragile, defensive parts of each other, we were not running away. In his play The Real Thing (1982), Tom Stoppard aptly defined love as ‘Knowledge of each other … knowledge of self, the real him, the real her, in extremis, the mask slipped from the face.’ In the course of our building trust, it did feel a lot like love. Not love based on surface similarities or romance, but on a mutual commitment to each other. It felt like growing up. The existential emptiness, the unsteady sense of vertigo eased.
