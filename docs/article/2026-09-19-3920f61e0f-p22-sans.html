This brings us back to black holes. Wheeler’s student Jacob Bekenstein knew that entropy should always increase, but identified a puzzle. If you take a hot gas cloud full of entropy – molecules in disordered motion, like the perfume – and drop it into a black hole, its entropy appears to simply vanish. What was chaotic becomes simple. That seemed to be a violation of a fundamental law: the entropy of the Universe decreasing. However, Bekenstein realised that a black hole’s area always increases, just like entropy. He concluded that there must be a relationship between entropy and black hole size.
