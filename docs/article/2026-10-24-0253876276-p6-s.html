To this end, the Plasmodium parasite assumes a variety of shapes and sizes as it moves through its life stages: Sometimes it’s shaped like an egg, sometimes a spindly thread, sometimes a gangly starfish. It can move through this cycle in rapid succession or hang out in one stage (the hypnozoite phase in the human liver) for years. When it enters its tiny, round merozoite stage, the parasite is ready to invade a red blood cell.

Plasmodium pulls up to the scene of the crime with all the proteins and other biological equipment it needs. The entry procedure is more delicate than simply punching a hole in the host cell. “If you’re trying to push an orange into a balloon, the balloon mustn’t pop, right?” Baum said.
