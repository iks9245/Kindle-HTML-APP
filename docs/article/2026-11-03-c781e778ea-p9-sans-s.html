It also would mean that an ever-larger share of the fallout from a potentially devastating AI crash would fall on ordinary people. As a private company, Anthropic’s shares are overwhelmingly owned by corporate insiders, tech giants, and venture capitalists. Once the company goes public and shares start getting funneled into 401(k)s, retirement accounts, and index funds, it will be workers, retirees, and retail investors that are increasingly left holding the bag when the forces squeezing Anthropic’s margins start to bite.

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) has the power to stop that from happening. The agency can delay or stop companies from going public if it believes a company’s registration statement is “on its face incomplete or inaccurate in any material respect.” That legislative language might as well have been written with Anthropic in mind, considering that the company’s prospectus includes only unaudited financial results for the period where it claims the greatest revenue growth, relegates its most significant future obligations to the footnotes of its registration statement, and has continued to announce new 11-digit financial commitments as its IPO approaches.
