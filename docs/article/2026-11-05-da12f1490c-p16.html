These results implied either that the models were engaged in two unrelated forms of introspection, or that one of the processes wasn’t introspection after all. Since steering alone could make models start talking about the injected concept, Lederman and Mahowald felt that the latter option was more likely. That is, the descriptions the models had given in response to Lindsey’s second question probably hadn’t been the result of introspection.

But then how could the models detect the injections in the first place? Lederman and Mahowald suspected that the answers to Lindsey’s first question might have a more mundane origin as well. But after subjecting the models to a battery of tests, the researchers found that these simpler explanations couldn’t fully account for the models’ behavior. They’d come in as skeptics but encountered an effect they couldn’t explain away.
