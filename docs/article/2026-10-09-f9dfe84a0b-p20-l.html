This was easier said than done. As Venice had already recognised, most towns depended so much on Jewish credit that it was difficult to imagine doing without them. The friars therefore proposed a solution. With the papacy’s approval, they proposed that towns should set up monti di pietà (‘banks of compassion’). Funded by voluntary donations, these would offer small loans to the working poor on a charitable basis – that is to say, either without asking for any interest at all, or charging rates no higher than they needed to cover their running costs. In principle, this would then relieve Italian towns of the need for Jewish moneylenders, while still respecting the prohibition on Christians lending money.
