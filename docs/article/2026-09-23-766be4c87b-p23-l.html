- OIDC assumes HTTP, whereas SAML is transport independent. Sure, SAML HTTP bindings exist and are most commonly used, but they are not required. This affords SAML a certain degree of flexibility, but also means that flexibility must be well defined, must be implemented somewhere, and can contain bugs. SAML came about at a time when HTTP + TLS was not yet the dominant backbone of web service communication, and it has never reconciled with this modern landscape. HTTPS allows OIDC to punt encrypted, trusted communication to the transport layer.
