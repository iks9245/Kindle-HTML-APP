US and Denmark reach deal over Greenland after Trump annexation threats

Chairman of the Greenland Government Jens-Frederik Nielsen said in a statement announcing the deal that it "is gratifying that we are about to enter into an agreement that ensures and strengthens the security" of Greenland, Denmark and the US.

"The agreement recognises Greenland's interests and our place in international co-operation. It is for the benefit of us all," he said.

Trump had tried for months to purchase or annex the mineral-rich Danish territory, arguing that it is vulnerable to adversaries, including Russia and China. He even threatened military action in Greenland, prompting an outcry from Danes and other Nato countries.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it a "historic deal" and a "huge win for the United States and the American people".

"This deal permanently and completely addresses our national security concerns in Greenland," he said in a statement.

No text of the agreement has been released. The BBC has reached out to the Danish Embassy and the White House.
