小中大切換為襯線

The self-making world

Not only did living beings create and transform themselves, according to Lamarck’s picture of things, they also made and re-made the inanimate world. Plants played the primary role in this process, he thought, because they could invent themselves out of just sunlight, air and water. He described experiments to confirm this miraculous capacity. For instance, carve a hollow into the side of a turnip and suspend the vegetable in a window by its root, Houdini-style: nothing up its sleeves! Keep the hollow filled with water, and you’ll see that the turnip will grow a whole flowering plant. It has created itself literally from thin air. Plants, by bringing together the primitive elements into combinations, originated the cosmic relay chain, then handed off their work to animals. By eating, digesting and engaging in their other functions and behaviours, animals produced more elaborate compounds, and so they shared with plants the defining ability of all living beings to build, compose and create.
