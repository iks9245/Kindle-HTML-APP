It didn’t take long for Venice to change its mind, though. After a devastating war with Genoa, the Venetian economy was in ruins. Trade had dropped, food prices had soared, and poverty was rising fast. With more people borrowing than ever before, interest rates skyrocketed. If Venice was to stand any chance of getting back on its feet, it needed cheap credit – and fast. Therefore, it put pragmatism ahead of prejudice. In February 1382, the Venetian government offered any banker who would agree to lower their interest rates the chance to live in the city for a period of five years. Theoretically, anyone could apply; but, with only one exception, all those who signed up were Jewish.
