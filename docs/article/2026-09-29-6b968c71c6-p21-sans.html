Paternity is poetical

Again and again, the research points to intensified cerebral connectivity, and suggests that such changes take place regardless of biological relationship. A fascinating 2014 study conducted by Ruth Feldman and Eyal Abraham and their colleagues in Israel found the familiar patterns of response in homosexual male couples raising infants who had either been adopted or born through surrogacy. Summarising the study, Saxbe wrote that the homosexual fathers performing the primary caregiver role ‘showed amygdala activation similar to the primary caregiver moms and mentalising network activation similar to the secondary caregiver dads, as well as stronger connectivity between those two parts of the brain.’ The process of becoming a father – quite literally, thinking your way into it – is itself a creative act, driven by time, proximity and a sense of responsibility.
