So, of course, the fungi are living, so they respire, so they lose some of that carbon, but they also create these very sticky, beautiful compounds that are very hard to break down, and so that helps keep carbon underground. You can think of them as like a scaffolding that sort of holds the soil together. If they weren’t there, the soil would just erode and disappear.

So, not only are they important in regulating our climate, but they also help with our ability to grow food. So, as I was saying, the plants really depend on these networks for their phosphorus uptake, for their nitrogen, for their water. Up to 80% of a plant’s phosphorus first passes through a fungal network. So, what that means is even the DNA, backbone, the phosphorus backbone of our own DNA first passed through a fungal network. Like we are made up of phosphorus that passed through fungi.
