Barbara Ward’s vision

Ward’s vision achieved its clearest expression in 1972, at the UN Conference on the Human Environment, which was the international organisation’s first attempt to outline a common environmental strategy for the world. Ward was invited to take a leading role in preparation for the conference in Stockholm and wrote (with the scientist René Dubos) the report Only One Earth: The Care and Maintenance of a Small Planet, which was circulated to all participants before the event to present the organisers’ vision of economic development with respect for nature.

Drawing on her vast international network of experts, Ward approached 152 scientists, diplomats, industry leaders and stakeholders from 58 countries, and solicited their views about the prospects of economic development and its impact on the planet. She wanted to give space to the multiple, sometimes contradictory, opinions about the ecological impact of economic-growth strategies. She edited the collected ideas into a comprehensive document outlining possible approaches to ensure that economic growth wouldn’t come at the expense of the biosphere by limiting (though not banning) pollution, consumption and extraction.
