How Spiders Spin ‘Silk Dope’ Into Nature’s Toughest Material

The strand is tougher than Kevlar, the synthetic fiber in bike tires and bulletproof vests, and stronger than steel. It’s flexible and biocompatible with human bodies. Scientists dream of making spider silk into high-performing materials for seat belts, helmets, naval materials, and even scaffolds to help repair brain cells. But attempts to make spider silk in the lab have fallen short because researchers don’t know enough about how a spider spins “silk dope,” a liquid blend of proteins, water, and salt, from its silk glands into a remarkable solid.
