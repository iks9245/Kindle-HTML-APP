Deskilling the body

The boundary of the body also creates a distinct inner environment, one that is physically and chemically different from the surrounding world. Through this difference, the body acts as a translator, turning external changes into useful internal physical responses. This allows a life-form to carry out specific reactions in controlled conditions that support their survival and flourishing. Internal regulation also gives organisms a unique logic of their own. They can then act according to the current state of their bodies and the world as they find it. Our bodies detect changes that have consequence for us, evaluate them against our needs, and reorganise into useful action. Thus, living beings might be understood as a remarkable kind of organisation of matter: a bounded, metabolically open, self-maintaining form that gives rise to purposeful, intelligent action. This is key to understanding both the skills of our flesh, and why we can think of ourselves as being ‘deskilled’ today. When we recognise that our bodies are intrinsically intelligent in their organisation, we don’t need a conscious agent beyond the flesh. The body is the agent from which intelligence emerges and also, in the fullest sense, who we are.
