Right after the lecture, Shelest emailed Bialy, asking to join his research group. In an act of kismet, Bialy had recently been looking for a student to explore the ISM through three-dimensional mapping, and so a research partnership was born.

Shelest already had the map of the ISM’s dust. The plan was to combine that map with a map of the ultraviolet radiation that heats the ISM up.

By chance, Bialy located just such a map by striking up a conversation at a conference with the astrophysicist Troy Porter of Stanford University, who had made a map but had yet to publish it.
