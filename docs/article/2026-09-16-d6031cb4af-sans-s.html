小中大切換為襯線

Chasing la dolce vita

When he was a child growing up in Emilia-Romagna, my great-grandfather Marco once found an eagle fledgling that had fallen from its nest. He kept it warm in the folds of his shirt and ran home, only to discover as he undressed that the bird had eaten strips of his flesh, its beak tinged red with blood. The rescued eagle thrived in my great-grandfather’s care, its wingspan soon a remarkable length. And all the while, Marco himself grew immensely tall. Then, one day, without warning, the eagle flew away.

The moment my father told me this story when I was a boy, it lodged itself deep in my imagination. I pictured young Marco as a giant striding through the forests of north-central Italy, calling to the bird of prey, waiting in vain for it to land on his outstretched hand. Marco merged with the heroes of my illustrated classical civilisation books, earning his place between the busts of Roman emperors and the chapters on Greek mythology.
