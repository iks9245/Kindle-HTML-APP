Chasing la dolce vita

My children and I have now visited most of the spots across London where my family lived and worked. Walking further from the former Italian Hospital in Queen Square, Bloomsbury, it doesn’t take us long to reach Clerkenwell’s once-vibrant Little Italy. Though many of the delis and ice-cream parlours have been changed by new waves of immigration and redevelopment, the plaque to Giuseppe Mazzini, a champion of Italian unification, on Laystall Street, reminds passersby of the area’s historic importance to Italianness. Mass is still celebrated in Italian at St Peter’s Church, and near the back of the church is a monument to the dead of the SS Arandora Star. We sit and read the list of surnames as they mingle with the priest’s prayers to form one long lament. I picture the churches of Emilia-Romagna, and the cemeteries where my ancestors are buried, and I see the roads that lead from there to London and back, worn down by constant travelling in both directions, a straddling between nations, where ghosts are as substantial as the living.
