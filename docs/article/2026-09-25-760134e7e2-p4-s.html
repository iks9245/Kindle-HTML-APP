小中大切換為無襯線

Where a land ethic blooms

No time to waste – the weather is expected to hold for another day, and I need to cover the field before the light fails this afternoon. Tomorrow, David will rake this chewed-up detritus into windrows and then come back for another round to bale them up. While it is good to leave some of this thatch on the field – to keep a roof over the soil and guard against erosion by wind and rain – we can afford to borrow some of this, which also makes good straw. Wherever livestock are gathered in close quarters, their waste can produce concentrations of ammonia and other gases that can be harmful to animals and people alike. Adding straw slows the breakdown of manure and urine, reducing the production of noxious gases and suspending valuable nutrients in a semi-solid mixture. The straw and excrement will come back to this field loaded with nitrogen, phosphorous and other nutrients.
