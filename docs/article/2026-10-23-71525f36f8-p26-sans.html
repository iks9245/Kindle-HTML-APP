Other people

Each day that I wake up in this house, I am choosing to enact a different script, one in which other people’s concerns are more real to me. My housemate’s labour pains, this baby’s cries, are my business. Conversely, my occasional existential terror or my children’s school struggles are tangible to a wider group of people than they would otherwise be. We have a stake in each other. There is a fierce tenderness in it that often brings me to tears. Iris Murdoch describes something like it in ‘The Sublime and The Good’ (1959) when she writes:

Love is the extremely difficult realisation that something other than oneself is real. Love … is the discovery of reality … The exercise of overcoming one’s self, of the expulsion of fantasy and convention … is indeed exhilarating. It is also, if we perform it properly which we hardly ever do, painful.
