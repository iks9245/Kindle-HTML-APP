The textual enthusiasm of both Cellini and the duke of Urbino finds a material counterpart in a growing culture of luxury weapons. The aesthetics of these high-end guns helped firearms fit into a court culture that valued beauty and craftsmanship. They were decorated with classical gods, Christian figures and mottoes, and images of the hunt, while the novelty value of combination weapons such as spears with miniature guns attached appealed to the Renaissance sense of fun.
