It was 2014. The 23-year-old Belarusian, who had been coding competitively since he was a kid but hadn’t done academic research before, was asked to help figure out how to get a computer to translate sentences from English to French. Bahdanau was working with artificial neural networks, which had already shown promise in pattern-recognition tasks such as deciphering handwriting and identifying objects in photos. The networks learn to recognize patterns by strengthening and weakening connections between nodes in response to training data. Bahdanau quietly doubted their potential to achieve anything like human-level intelligence, but his adviser, the computer scientist Yoshua Bengio, and the postdoc on the project, Kyunghyun Cho, were believers. And translation was widely seen as a formidable and interesting problem for neural networks to try to solve.
