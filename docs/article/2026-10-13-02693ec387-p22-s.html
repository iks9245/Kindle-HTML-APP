Is everything conscious?

Other properties, such as negative charge, could, for all we know, have disposed things to behave very differently or in no systematic way at all. But it is inconceivable that pain disposed us to do anything besides avoid the source of pain. ‘It does not,’ Mørch writes, ‘seem conceivable that pain makes subjects try to do anything else than avoid it in virtue of its intrinsic character alone.’ This raises a fascinating prospect. In the interest of rendering the behaviour of matter intelligible, we should take those properties responsible for that behaviour to be conscious mental properties. That is to say: all paradigm physical properties such as charge, mass and quantum spin are conscious mental properties. This move provides an answer to the question of what matter fundamentally is, which, in turn, explains why matter does what it does. The fundamental properties of matter are properties of consciousness. By their very nature, they dispose the matter of which they are properties towards certain behaviours. This plugs the explanatory gap left by the scientific method alone. Again, the primary motive in this argument is the desire for intelligible explanations of the world, as per the PSR.
