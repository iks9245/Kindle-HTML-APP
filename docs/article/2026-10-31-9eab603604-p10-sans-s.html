In praise of the dilettante

Though rooted in classicist aestheticism, some of Goethe and Schiller’s criticism of the dilettante still resonates today. They worry, for example, that the dilettante ‘jumps over the steps’, gliding too glibly through the creative process and claiming victory too early. There’s something to this. Similar to Oscar Wilde’s concern about the ‘sentimentalist’ in his 1897 epistolary essay, De Profundis, who ‘desires to have the luxury of an emotion without paying for it’, the authors voice a reasonable objection to those willing to cut the line in chasing laurels. Progress is earned through practice, and those of us who have put in the work are right to protect it, as any trained therapist, frustrated with the quackery that passes for therapy on YouTube and TikTok, will gladly testify.
