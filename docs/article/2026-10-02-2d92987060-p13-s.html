Reason is more than a tool

Hugging Face and similar incidents certainly challenge any reassuring picture of AI as an obedient and easily-controlled instrument. But recall what ‘servant’ meant for Hume. Reason serves the passions, not necessarily another person’s commands. And it can serve an ambition by breaking rules just as readily as by following them. Kant’s alternative to Hume was not simply the freedom to choose one’s own destination, but the capacity to ask whether that destination was worth choosing. Having purposes of one’s own is not yet the same as governing them by reason. That stronger capacity involves judging and revising our purposes according to principles we can justify to other rational beings understood as equals, recognising that they, too, are persons rather than instruments of our will. The question is not just whether a system can act independently, but whether it can judge and take responsibility for the ends it pursues.
