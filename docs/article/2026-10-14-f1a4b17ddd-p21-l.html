小中大切換為無襯線

Deskilling the body

Something similar may be at work in our increasing reliance on tools like ChatGPT. Like finding our way, articulating our thoughts is a dynamic, embodied act, whether writing or speaking. Learning a new speech movement, for instance, changes how we perceive speech sounds, as well as how we generate them. We develop these abilities by repeatedly finding a word, uttering it, listening to it, and correcting it as necessary – tasks that draw on multiple regions within our bodies. This is just as relevant for writing. But when an AI writing app automatically provides structure, it also diminishes the skills of selection and self-correction from our repertoire. In one study, passively copying AI-generated writing, unlike drafting first and then using AI for revision, was found to lessen an individual’s confidence in subsequently performing the task unaided. It is execution – physically doing it – that builds our embodied skills. That is why using AI to revise a draft we have written is fundamentally different from asking it to produce the draft for us. It’s not that computational tools are inherently bad or unhelpful – far from it – but rather that when they replace the physical effort of our bodies, our own agency and skill take a hit.
