A New Map of ‘Empty’ Space Challenges a Tranquil Picture of the Galaxy

Introduction

You might imagine space to be empty. But the seeming void contains a barely perceptible fog of hydrogen and other neutral atoms. In 2020, NASA’s New Horizons spacecraft, having passed the orbit of Pluto, measured a proxy for the amount of hydrogen around it. In the area between stars in our galaxy — the “interstellar medium,” or ISM — New Horizons found around 130 hydrogen atoms per liter of space.

This hydrogen, along with some helium and other molecules, is mostly left over from the Big Bang, and it’s constantly supplemented by infusions from cosmic phenomena such as stars ejecting jets of gas and dust as they are born and shedding their outer layers as they die.
