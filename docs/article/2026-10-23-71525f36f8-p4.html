More space, more privacy, more control – all are sold as natural, unquestioned goods to be sought in the unconscious script that most of us carry. For the nuclear family, they are tokens of success and maturity, whereas forgoing these ‘goods’ and living with people you are neither shagging nor parenting has an association with low-status, studenty seasons. Not for proper grownups. My husband and I began to feel the strengthening drag of this story. I sometimes saw it in my mind’s eye as a tide, washing young adults from their family homes into temporarily intertwined clusters (largely in cities), then out again, to the suburbs and beyond, in pairs or alone, in search of a garden, a bigger kitchen, storage space for the mountains of stuff we inevitably accumulate. Swept away from each other, everyone further and further apart.
