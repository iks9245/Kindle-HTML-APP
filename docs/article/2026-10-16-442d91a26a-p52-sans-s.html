KIERS: Yeah, exactly, and almost the opposite, because I’m actually doing quite a lot of work in the field as well, which we haven’t talked about. I mean, so I spend about half my time in the lab and about half my time out in the field collecting soils to understand fungal communities and dynamics at the global scale.

I actually got very frustrated with academia because it felt like the work that we were publishing on the importance of mycorrhizal networks wasn’t really being absorbed by decision-makers. That there’s a big leap, this chasm, between publication and how the world actually works and how decisions are made. The importance of these mycorrhizal networks, it just wasn’t getting past the literature.

And so what I wanted to do was start an organization, a nonprofit, that was able to draw attention to all of this incredible peer-reviewed research that had been being published, honestly, for decades showing the importance of mycorrhizal networks, but that just wasn’t being paid attention to.
