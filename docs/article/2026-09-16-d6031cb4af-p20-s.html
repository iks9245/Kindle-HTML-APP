Chasing la dolce vita

I know that, for every idealised image of the country, there’s a darker insinuation that Italians have had to face abroad. Late 19th-century and early 20th-century migrants from the peninsula frequently encountered discrimination – accompanied by a range of slurs, from ‘wop’ and ‘eyetie’ in Britain and the US, to ‘rital’ in France. Italian Americans were at times deemed ethnically ambiguous, their whiteness questioned, particularly when they were from the south of the peninsula. Hate-mongers, such as the Ku Klux Klan, alleged that the dregs of Italy were being sent abroad to cleanse the peninsula of its undesirables. In Protestant and English-speaking countries, Italian immigrants became easy targets because of their Catholicism, or their associations with anarchism and organised crime.
