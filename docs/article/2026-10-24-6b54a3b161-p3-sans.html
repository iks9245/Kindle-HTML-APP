In order to appreciate all that an understanding of friction has helped us to accomplish, it’s necessary to start at square one and state what friction actually is. We define friction as the force that resists motion, and that means friction is rather ubiquitous. If things are moving, whether they’re sliding around or rolling, there’s friction. Solids, liquids, gases? Doesn’t matter. If there’s movement, friction is there to resist it. Unfortunately, because friction’s job is to resist, we’ve developed an aversion to it as a nuisance. But it’s a very important force, and we’ve thrived by expanding our understanding of it. By the 1960s, after millennia of evolving with friction, we finally accepted that friction is such a critical part of our world that a scientific field of study needed to be devoted to it: tribology.
