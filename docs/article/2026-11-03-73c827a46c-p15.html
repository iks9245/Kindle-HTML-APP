小中大切換為無襯線

The self-making world

The incessant creative activity of each organism was far from solitary. Living beings collaborated as they worked to form the world and fend off destruction, and Lamarck took a great interest in this collaborative action. For instance, one spring day in 1798, having brought his family to a country house he briefly owned outside Paris, he noticed a swallow’s nest in one of the windows. Shortly afterwards, a local child destroyed the nest, leaving the pair of swallows homeless just as the female was about to lay her eggs. Immediately, 10 or 12 other swallows gathered around the wreckage. The birds were so agitated and noisy that at first Lamarck assumed they were quarrelling, but he soon realised it was just the opposite. They had organised themselves to rebuild the nest collaboratively. He watched in amazement as some remained by the construction site while others came and went with twigs. Swallows generally take eight to 12 days to build their nest, Lamarck observed, but these worked so diligently that they finished it the following morning.
