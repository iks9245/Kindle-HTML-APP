Chasing la dolce vita

Every summer, on car journeys to Italy we’d listen and re-listen to Italian songs and fairytales to drum the language into us. Once we’d crossed the Alps, any stopover became an opportunity for my father to immerse us in further knowledge. He kept a torch in his pocket, and in the stillness of churches he’d let the beam scurry over frescoes until it hovered, halo-like, above a painted face or a feature in the background. ‘Such bellezza,’ he’d whisper, ‘look at the beauty of that.’ In explaining Masaccio’s brushstrokes, or playing Verdi and Puccini arias at car-shaking volume, we sensed my father was offering us, his sons, yardsticks by which to measure life. He needed us to see ourselves as the inheritors of a civilisation unlike any other. Italianness was his gift to us.
