Is There Anything Left in the Dream of Cold Fusion?

Shrinking atoms intensifies the electric field within them. According to Coulomb’s law, which is the electric counterpart of Newton’s law of gravity, the electric field strengthens as the inverse square of distance. For a kaonic or antiprotonic atom, then, it scales up a millionfold. That provides a stress test of a central pillar of modern physics, quantum electrodynamics (QED), or how electromagnetism behaves at the quantum level. “It is the strongest Coulomb field that you can create in the lab to probe QED,” Paul said.

In fact, it breaches the so-called Schwinger limit, the threshold at which quantum effects go berserk and empty space becomes unstable. Particles begin to materialize spontaneously out of nothingness. “You’re boiling the vacuum with the stronger field,” she said. Physicists predicted this particle effervescence in the 1930s and have yet to observe it directly, but they have discerned a closely related phenomenon using exotic atoms. In spring 2026, Curceanu and her colleagues did so using kaonic fluorine that was experiencing an electric field above the Schwinger limit.
