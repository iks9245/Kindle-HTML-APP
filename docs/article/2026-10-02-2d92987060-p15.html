This distinction brings us back to the claim I heard in the auditorium. The speaker was not merely describing what machines can do; he was offering optimisation as a definition of intelligence itself. Accept that definition, and Hume’s side of the old dispute begins to look like common sense: our reason, too, becomes a tool for getting what we want, rather than a power to judge what is worth wanting.

If intelligence is really nothing more than optimisation, then the hardest questions can be ignored
