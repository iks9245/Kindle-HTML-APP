How to be a (Roman) man

Cicero was criticised by his contemporaries (and by later commentators) for his emotional reaction to exile. Should he have adopted a Stoic stiff upper lip, perhaps? To have been unaffected by this experience would have been inhuman rather than manly, he asserts in a speech given after his return in 57 BCE:

I could not now describe my action as praiseworthy or say that I was the source of any benefit to the republic, if I had but abandoned, for the republic’s sake, what I felt no pang at losing; I should hold that such mental apathy, like the physical apathy which does not feel the sting of fire, was brute insensibility rather than bravery [virtus].

In his own way, he claimed, even his retreat into exile, and even his frequent complaints, had shown bravery – manliness.
