Barbara Ward’s vision

Ward’s bestselling books and prestigious public lectures made her a well-known figure, and she developed a public profile as a political commentator and advisor to world leaders, including Nkrumah, Adlai Stevenson, John F Kennedy, Robert McNamara and Lyndon B Johnson. By the late 1950s, she divided her time between Harvard, where she held a visiting professorship, and Ghana, where she continued to promote the Volta Dam project, sharing Nkrumah’s hope that it might foster Ghana’s economic independence. Later, she was appointed to a prestigious professorship in development economics at Columbia University in New York, and in the 1970s she was made a Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire and a life peer. Upon her death in 1981, The Washington Post acknowledged her influence by stating that: ‘For 30 years, it has scarcely been possible to discuss development anywhere in the world without bringing in Barbara Ward.’
