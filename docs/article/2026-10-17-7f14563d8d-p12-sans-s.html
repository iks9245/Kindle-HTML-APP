The rows $\tilde k_i\transpose$ of $(KK\transpose)\inverse K$ are known as the dual basis for the rows of $K$: by construction, $(KK\transpose)\inverse KK\transpose = I$, so $\tilde k_i\transpose k_j = \delta_{ij}$. Then $f(k_j) = \sum_i v_i\, \tilde k_i\transpose k_j = v_j$, so there is no interference and we have perfect recall.

When $M > d_k$. Setting the gradient of the objective to zero gives $W K\transpose K = V\transpose K$, so $W = V\transpose K\,(K\transpose K)\inverse$. Both solutions are $W = V\transpose (K\transpose)\pinv$, where $A\pinv$ denotes the pseudoinverse of $A$: $(K\transpose)\pinv = (KK\transpose)\inverse K$ when the keys are linearly independent, and $(K\transpose)\pinv = K(K\transpose K)\inverse$ when they span $\reals^{d_k}$.
