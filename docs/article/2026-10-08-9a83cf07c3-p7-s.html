How to assemble science

Glass set out to do a better job. He and his wife, a psychologist named Mary Lee Smith, systematically hunted down every scrap of research they could find that compared the effect of psychotherapy with a control group or another therapy – ending up with more than 370 studies.

Then Glass and Smith worked out a way to extract and combine the measurements of psychotherapy’s effect in each study. Even though one study measured the effects of the therapy on anxiety and another its impact on blood pressure, they devised a way to convert these results into one standard measure known as ‘effect size’ and then average them across all the studies. (This is somewhat like converting various currencies into dollars, so they can all be combined.) Glass called this new statistical method a meta-analysis – an analysis of analyses, just as metadata is data about data.
