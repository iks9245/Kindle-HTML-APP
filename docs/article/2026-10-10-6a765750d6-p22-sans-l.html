Barbara Ward’s vision

Ward recognised the value of humanity’s pluralism yet, as a devout Catholic, she found inspiration in Christianity. She criticised the ecclesiastical institutions, although she was one of the few women in 1964 invited to contribute her expertise to the Second Vatican Council, and admitted that, historically, faith had ‘divided the world so bitterly’. But she was persuaded that the notion of ‘Christian brotherhood’ could help rediscover ‘what unites rather than divides the communities of the world’. Quoting Pope Paul VI, she argued that the newly found unity of her era emerged from ‘one of the fundamental moral insights of the Western culture which has now swept over the whole globe’, namely that ‘mankind is not a group of warring tribes, but a single, equal and fraternal community’. Equality and justice thus relied on the ‘Western sense that men, as souls of infinite metaphysical value, stand equal before the throne of God’.
