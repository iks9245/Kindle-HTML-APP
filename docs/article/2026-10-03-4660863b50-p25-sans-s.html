Finally, participants learned to distinguish what they had done from who they were. Jaysean, 22, had taken the bad things he had done as proof that he was a bad person. Abandoned by kin while still a boy, he had sought out older robbers to teach him how to survive. He said: ‘A lot of things that I did bad, I did it for survival. Cause I didn’t really know no other way to get no money.’ Jaysean explained: ‘I was looking for the wrong help. I didn’t know no better. You a product of your environment to a certain extent.’ The reframing allowed him to imagine a self no longer defined by his past: ‘I’m a good person within… I’m still falling down but I’m picking myself back up.’ What Jaysean had once believed was a sign of his character, he could now see as a result of surviving tough circumstances.
