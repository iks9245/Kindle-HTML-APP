DOELEMAN: Yeah. Well, so, so this idea of turning the Earth into a telescope had been around for a long time, since the ‘60s. And the key is that the angular resolution of a telescope or the ability to magnify things on the sky, it scales as the wavelength of light you’re observing, lambda, divided by the size of your telescope.

So if you wanna see things that are really small in the sky, you either reduce your wavelength of light or you expand the size of your telescope. So you’ll often see these huge blocks of glass on top of mountaintops. These huge optical dishes, and they’re big for a reason. They collect a lot of light, but also the bigger they are, the smaller the objects they can see.
