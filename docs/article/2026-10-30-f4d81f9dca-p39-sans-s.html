STROGATZ: It’s very inspiring to listen to the story of how much science goes in to make the stunning image that a billion people or whatever got to see. How much fundamental science between the atomic clocks, the idea of interferometers, all the general relativity…

LEVIN: I totally agree that this remarkable precision of the clock that they needed, and the industriousness of being on the edge of observability, I mean, it really was almost impossible. You could totally have imagined that somebody would’ve said, “This is ridiculous. Give this up. You need a telescope the size of the Earth, so move on to something else. We’ll never see a picture of any black hole ever.”

And instead, they thought, “Well, how would I make a telescope the size of the Earth,” and calling on observatories that had been in use for other purposes, as opposed to building a fundamentally new instrument.
