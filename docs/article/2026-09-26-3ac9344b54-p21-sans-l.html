For Habermas, there are two forces at play in this widening. Platforms drop the editorial filtering that newspapers and broadcasters used to perform, and they turn every user into someone who publishes. For Habermas the deeper problem isn’t the resulting noise; it’s that heavy platform use produces something smaller than a public: semi-public spheres, self-enclosed, silos whose members can no longer perceive the wider public sphere as inclusive at all – claims to universality beyond their own horizon become, in his words, ‘suspect of hypocrisy’. That perception is itself the damage, on his account, as much as anything actually happening out in the general public sphere. He ends by insisting that ‘maintaining a media structure that ensures the inclusive character of the public sphere and the deliberative character of the formation of public opinion and political will is not a matter of political preference but a constitutional imperative.’
