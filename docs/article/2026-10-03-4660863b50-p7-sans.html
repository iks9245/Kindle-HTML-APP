Life on a hair trigger

In 2022, I was part of a research team that interviewed 99 men from Chicago’s highest-violence neighbourhoods. All were former or current participants in the Rapid Employment and Development Initiative, or READI Chicago, a community violence intervention run by a partnership of Heartland Alliance, the University of Chicago and seven nonprofit organisations on the city’s south and west sides. READI served adult men at greatest risk of shooting someone or being shot. By the time they entered the programme, 98 per cent had been arrested, averaging more than 17 times each, and 35 per cent had been shot. For 18 months, participants were paid to work and attend group cognitive behavioural intervention (CBI) sessions. Outreach workers helped them navigate housing, legal, safety and substance-use crises that might otherwise have made steady participation difficult.
