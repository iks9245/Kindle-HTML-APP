Russell had been wrestling with the foundations of mathematics for some years before his letter. It was only after meeting the Italian mathematician Giuseppe Peano at the International Congress of Philosophy in 1900 that he acquired the tools to attempt the problem. In place of Frege’s edifice, Russell set out to build his own, together with his colleague Alfred North Whitehead. Principia Mathematica appeared in three massive volumes between 1910 and 1913: all of mathematics, grounded in pure logic.

Mathematics carries what’s already there without adding anything, as plumbing moves water without supplying any
