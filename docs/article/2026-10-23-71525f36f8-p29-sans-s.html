Other people

Helpfully, Jesus had a lot of other less cryptic things to say about learning to bear each other and be together. The New Testament takes it as a given that humans are messy, dishonest, selfish, misguided, that we will hurt and anger each other. We may be made for and by each other, but it assumes there will be days when we wish we were not. This tendency to disconnect, this almost gravitational pulling away from each other led Martin Luther, building on Augustine, to call us ‘Homo incurvatus in se’: humanity turned in on itself. I find it one of the more useful definitions of sin.

The New Testament is largely a set of letters between what we might see as communitarian experiments, and reckoning with our inward-turning tendencies. The petty squabbles between early believers trying to share their lives and possessions are there on the page for all to see. The difficulty was part of the point, not a reason to avoid it.
