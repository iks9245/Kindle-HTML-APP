What Habermas never spelled out is what this does to moral and political claims once people stop being able to agree at all. Truth, on his revised view, still has somewhere to go even if everyone stops talking: the world doesn’t care whether anyone’s listening. His philosophy of Rightness never had that option. So, when the people who’d need to agree on something splinter into groups that no longer talk to each other, a factual claim can still be true or false regardless. A moral claim has nothing left holding it up, once nobody’s left in the room to agree on it.
