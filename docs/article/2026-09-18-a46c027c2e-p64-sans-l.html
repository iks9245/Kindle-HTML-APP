HALLIWELL: That’s an excellent question. In quantum physics, you have the possibility of a so-called superposition state, where a particle can, for instance, be in two places at the same time. So position, we would say, is not definite. But it’s a little bit more nuanced than that. I mean, it could be a probabilistic distribution of being here or here.

But quantum physics, you can do a measurement that shows it definitely is in two places at the same time, and then you can measure it in each place and find it’s definitely there and definitely there. So sometimes people say like misty states or indefinite states.
