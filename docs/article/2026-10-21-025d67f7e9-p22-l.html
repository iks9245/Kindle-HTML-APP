It is at this point that intuitions re-enter the story. According to John Bengson’s concept of quasi-perceptualism, we should model the epistemic behaviour of intuitions on the epistemic behaviour of perceptual experiences: if perceptual seemings justify because they possess a presentational phenomenology, and if intuitions are to justify in the same basic way, then they must share that same kind of presentational phenomenology. When we have an intuition, it justifies a corresponding belief by virtue of exhibiting a felt quality analogous to the what-it’s-likeness of perception, a kind of intellectual seeming that presents its content as actual. For instance, think again of the stranger who gives you a seriously creepy vibe. Just as the tree visually seems green, so the stranger may simply seem untrustworthy. Something about him presents itself to you that way without you having worked out what exactly is making you uncomfortable.
