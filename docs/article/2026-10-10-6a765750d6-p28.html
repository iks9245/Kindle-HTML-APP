It is worth noticing, however, that in her later writings about environmental justice, Ward downplayed her earlier emphasis on Christianity as the moral foundation for a planetary community, suggesting instead that public awareness of the planet’s physical unity could also generate political union. Economic development of postcolonial countries should, she argued in the 1970s, take local preferences into account rather than follow a Western model. The emergence of ecological concerns marked a significant shift in Ward’s political thought, transforming her understanding of the relationship between economic justice, political unity and postcolonial world order.
