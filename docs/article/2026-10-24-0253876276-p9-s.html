Once inside, Plasmodium approaches its remodeling work more comprehensively. Red blood cells are essentially bags of hemoglobin; they have no nucleus or organelles to speak of. So Plasmodium builds from the ground up. It secretes proteins that allow it to feed on hemoglobin, remove waste, and stretch the cell’s cytoplasm. Ho compared it to the conversion of an empty warehouse floor into a stylish lofted apartment.

“It’s putting in plumbing,” she said. “It’s putting in the floors. It’s literally building everything for itself.”
