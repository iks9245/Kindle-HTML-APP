In his 2026 book The Emerging Science of Machine Learning Benchmarks, Hardt makes the provocative case that these measuring sticks have been broken for decades already: Benchmarks, he writes, are “the mistake that made machine learning.” Notably, he argues, instead of creating a replicability crisis, they drove legitimate progress anyway — “mostly by sheer luck,” in his view. By understanding why, we can explain how benchmarks have since lost this accidental credibility in the age of LLMs, and how they might regain it.

Quanta spoke to Hardt about what went wrong, and right, with benchmarks, and where to take them next. The interview has been condensed and edited for clarity.
