It took me a long time to understand why. In the family in which I grew up, we didn’t talk about money. When we had it, nobody mentioned it. Then we lost our house, my parents divorced, and money was a more frequently discussed issue. It wasn’t until years later, while having conversations about finances with my husband that stirred negative responses in me, that I recognised how I automatically logged a conversation about money as the beginning of something going wrong. With help from a cognitive behavioural therapist and my very patient husband, I learned what the men in READI learned: to notice a reactive feeling in my body, to have compassion for myself about where it came from, and to recognise when it no longer served me.
