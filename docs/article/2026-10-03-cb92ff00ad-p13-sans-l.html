When Si and Fang calculated how the shrimp’s flailing bodies disrupted the moving water, they saw “something very, very strange,” Fang said: The shrimp weren’t just contributing to the chaos of the system; they also seemed to be shifting the direction in which energy flowed.

A turbulent system can be described in terms of how much it stretches and compresses the fluids within, due to the interactions of fluids flowing at different velocities. These dynamics show up, for example, when you pour confetti into a river and watch it organize into sweeping lines as it races on competing currents downstream.
