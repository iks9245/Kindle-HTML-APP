Don’t use the ‘C-word’

Cancer is fundamentally different: although ultimately it also causes system failures, its origin lies in cells in a corner of a given organ thriving more than they should, growing too quickly, and eventually exploring and colonising other parts of the body. A marauding virus or bacterium is clearly a malign invader that can be understood and labelled as foreign and hostile. A cancer is a person’s own cells. They are behaving badly but are still intrinsic to the self. Chemotherapy for cancer is so much more toxic than antibiotics for bacterial infection precisely because it is harder for treatments to consistently distinguish ‘normal’ from ‘cancer’ among a body’s cells.
