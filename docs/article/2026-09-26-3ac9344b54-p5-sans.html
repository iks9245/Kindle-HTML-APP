After the seminars, the class would sometimes go out for drinks. It was on one of these occasions that I understood where the joke had come from. I’d asked him what it had been like arriving in the US as a visiting professor for the first time, at the New School for Social Research in the autumn of 1967, which decades earlier had served as a refuge for scholars fleeing the same Germany in which he’d grown up. It was the start of a long habit of crossing the Atlantic: Princeton four years later, for the Gauss Lectures where he first tried out the turn toward language that would eventually become his theory of communicative action; then, decades on, Northwestern, where I got to sit in the room.
