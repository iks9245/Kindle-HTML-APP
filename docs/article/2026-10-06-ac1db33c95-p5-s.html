A life in episodes

I first tried my hand at full-scale life-writing in 1986, shortly after I finished my first book, on the history of Swedish ecology. Almost accidentally, I met with Niels Kaj Jerne, the Danish immunologist who had been awarded the Nobel Prize in physiology or medicine just two years earlier. ‘I have saved some papers,’ he casually remarked as we sat talking over a glass of wine in the kitchen of his large villa in southern France. We went up to the spacious attic room, where he parted the shutters and gestured towards the ancient Roman aqueduct, the Pont du Gard. Yet, I had no interest in antiquity; my attention was entirely arrested by the hundreds of grocery bags from the local supermarché lined up across the stone floor. They were stuffed to the brim with intimate letters, private diaries, raw laboratory protocols, rough article drafts, and decades of correspondence with colleagues around the world. He had kept virtually every scrap of paper that had passed through his hands from his youth onwards.
