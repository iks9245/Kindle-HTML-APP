Malaria Parasite Caught in the Act of Breaking and Entering

What malaria was and how it infected humans was deeply mysterious until barely 150 years ago. In 1880, the French physician Charles Louis Alphonse Laveran peered at a sample of a soldier’s blood through a crude microscope and discovered the true cause of the disease: not miasma, but a genus of shape-shifting single-celled protists called Plasmodium. Since then, scientists and doctors have described in precise detail the parasites’ life cycle, hosts, and disease progression, and even the nuances of various Plasmodium species, which present slightly different forms of the disease. But some crucial aspects have remained a mystery.
