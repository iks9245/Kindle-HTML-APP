But there was of course a paradox in all this effort focused on generating an impression of authentic masculinity, a paradox apparent in the advice in To Herennius to ‘make everything seem straight from the heart’. The artifice required to generate authentic-seeming masculinity was all too evident. We can see this as a symptom of the ongoing challenge of justifying male supremacy, through assertions of the ‘natural’ superiority of men to women (and indeed of free men to enslaved ones). What exactly was ‘masculine’ rhetorical style? Opinions could differ significantly. But masculinity itself was always a positive term. And giving effective speeches in Rome’s public spaces was an ideal way to communicate one’s specifically masculine virtue.
