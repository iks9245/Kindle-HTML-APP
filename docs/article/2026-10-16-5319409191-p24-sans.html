The ladder of knowledge

Dummett died in 2011, having been knighted in 1999 for ‘services to philosophy and to racial justice’. He is remembered as one of the most significant British philosophers of the previous century. Yet for all its power, his programme remained a prospectus: he set out what a theory of language, logic and reality must achieve, but he never completed one.

In 2005, Tor Sandqvist, a doctoral student at Uppsala University, submitted a thesis that took up Dummett’s challenge: to justify the ordinary practices of mathematicians without any appeal to truth. Where Dummett had given the conditions a solution must satisfy, Sandqvist gave a solution. He produced a precise mathematical justification of deduction as mathematicians actually use it, grounded entirely in practices of assertion. The boxer’s fists were untied.
