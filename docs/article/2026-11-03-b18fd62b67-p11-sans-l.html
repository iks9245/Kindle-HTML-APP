Is There Anything Left in the Dream of Cold Fusion?

The core idea that builds to muon-catalyzed fusion is simple. Ordinary atoms consist of a positively charged nucleus orbited by negatively charged electrons. But there’s no reason they have to be electrons. Other negatively charged particles serve just as well. In the Standard Model of particle physics, the electron has a sibling called the muon that can stand in for it. Physicists have also swapped in particles from other families, such as kaons, pions, and Xi baryons. They have even created hybrids of matter and antimatter, in which a nucleus containing protons is orbited by negatively charged antiprotons. In short, you can have regular hydrogen, muonic hydrogen, kaonic hydrogen, antiprotonic hydrogen, and so on.
