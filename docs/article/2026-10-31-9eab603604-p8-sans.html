In praise of the dilettante

One reason to defend the dilettante, then, is for lexicological justice. The term has been unfairly burdened with an overly negative connotation. It wasn’t always this way. From the Baroque-era Italian dilettante, the term originally referred to someone who pursues an interest, particularly an artistic one, for the sake of pleasure, or ‘delight’. Even as the term evolved, it stayed largely neutral, distinguishing enthusiasts from professionals in a given practice. The pejorative connotation familiar to us today is more recent, shaped by what I’ll call a ‘deficiency’ model, in which amateurism is viewed negatively as a lack of something: skill, seriousness, focus, expertise. This is the meaning ascribed by cultural gatekeepers like Theodor Adorno, who in 1929 dismissed the dilettante as one ‘unable to establish any standards’.
