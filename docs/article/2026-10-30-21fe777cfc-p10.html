In the first instance, billionaires and political operatives make for awkward ambassadors; Cummings, Musk and Munger might be brilliant, but they’re also extremely divisive. But more than who they are, what interests me is how they’re putting physics to work – whether they’re truly thinking with the discipline, or just brandishing it for rhetorical effect. Is ‘physics’ simply a shorthand for cutting through complexity, for stripping down problems to their most basic parts? Or does it genuinely offer something distinctive that other sciences and ways of thinking cannot?

Philosophers call the first approach ‘reductionism’: the idea that if you break any system into small enough components and trace them far enough, you eventually arrive at physics. That view can be powerful, and explains why so many people find it attractive.
