How intuitions work

Why should apprehending a content as actual have any epistemic significance? The answer, I think, is simple. Presentational states are the cognitive events that place us in contact with the world or, more precisely, that seem to place us in contact with the world. When something presents itself to us as being the case, our default cognitive response is to form the corresponding belief. When I turn the corner of a building and my eyes fall on a tree, the tree is simply there before me. Believing that there is a tree is not normally a conclusion I draw; rather I simply accept what my experience presents to me. This is not a matter of inference or deliberation; it is the natural way a rational mind responds to what it takes to be reality.
