The bombarding of childhood

This is why the familiar adult language of ‘engagement’ is so poor for children. A child can look intensely engaged while being given very little time to think. In the Children’s Slow Media Project, we have therefore tried to make these differences visible. We evaluate children’s media by looking at how these media organise time. We ask whether the narrative is coherent enough to follow; whether the sensory field overwhelms meaning-making at specific age junctures; whether the design builds in stopping points, where the story can be digested before it goes on. Does a piece of media allow learning to develop through meaningful repetition? And does a story or piece create openings for a child and adult, or two children, to notice something together?
