I am 42, and I did not expect to end up here. Like most of my peers, I was handed a vision of a home worth striving for with far, far fewer people in it and, to begin with, I followed that script. A middle-class 1980s child, I did well at school, moved away from my home county dormitory town for university, and never went back. London, jobs at the BBC, running a think tank all followed. I lived with good friends, and juggled seeing others between our clashing personal schedules. Then people began to pair up and procreate. I met someone, followed suit, and we clawed our way onto the very bottom of the housing ladder. It was a lovely life. A bit relentless, perhaps, a bit lonely around the edges, too, as the schedules got ever harder to tessellate, the work and care burdens heavier. But we had done it. Our little dyad, with dependants, was standing on its own feet, even if something felt decidedly unstable underneath.
