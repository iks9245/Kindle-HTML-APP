There is something about our house I have not mentioned much. You may have caught that throwaway line about praying together. One of the arguments made by Charles Taylor and others about the isolating tendencies of modernity is that it is linked to secularity. He argues that pre-moderns had a much stronger sense of how porous they were, how bound up with each other, with nature, and with divine or magical presences. He links our increasingly boundaried or buffered sense of self to the loss of religion: we moderns are not made by and for god(s), by and for each other, but by and for ourselves. I have some sympathy with the argument, because without a robust, collective wisdom tradition, it is challenging to keep choosing to move towards each other. Despite believing in this way of life for policy reasons, emotional reasons, neurobiological reasons, even goddamn survival reasons, I am not sure I would be able to do it without a powerful, and in our case, religious, reason why.
