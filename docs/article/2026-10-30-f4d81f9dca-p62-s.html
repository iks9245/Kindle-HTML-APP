So, even now, the EHT, Event Horizon Telescope, is a bit democratic, so other researchers who are not in the collaboration can still apply for time on it, and there’s been like one or two cases I think where that’s happened. But also more to your point about what else can we look at, we train the Event Horizon Telescope not just on Sag A*and M87, but on many radio loud quasars. So these are supermassive black holes that produce these jets of oppositely directed matter, and we’re able to get the best angular resolution that you can get on those sources. So we’re studying the propagation of jets, the collimation of those jets.

They’re very far away, but we can get the best most detailed images of them using the EHT. So it’s advanced our understanding of how jets are produced and how they maintain their shape very far from the black hole. And then there’s also a couple of other objects we can look at.
