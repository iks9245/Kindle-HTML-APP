小中大切換為襯線

The bombarding of childhood

Research has told us many useful things about children’s media. We know that some educational television (like Sesame Street) can support learning. We know that some apps are better designed than others. We know that interactive features in digital books can help when they serve the story, and distract when they do not. We know that characters and narratives shape children’s ideas about identity, gender and social life. Pace cuts across all of these questions as the condition under which content becomes experience.

Screen time can run fast or slow. This is partly why merely clocking ‘screen time’ is too crude a measure. Two children may each watch 20 minutes of media and have entirely different encounters with time. In one case, the child is moved through a coherent story with pauses, repetitions and recognisable emotional beats. In another, she is pulled through 10 clips, 30 characters, flashing colours, sudden songs and rewards that arrive before thought has had time to form. The clock records the same duration but the child does not live the same time.
