Love with nowhere to go

But despite the depth of our bond, she left, abruptly, as if she had never been there. She had fallen in love with another man. I was left behind, dazed and disoriented, and although my heart was broken, this kind of loss did not have a name that matched how shattered I felt. It was ‘just a breakup’, and it didn’t even deserve that name: we had been dating, but it never technically became a relationship. It was also a romantic connection that ran alongside another one, a long partnership that was open by mutual agreement and conscious design. Much about it wasn’t legible to most people around me. I faced the grief largely by myself. No scripts, no rituals, no recognised form, no legitimacy, no shared language, no way to make sense of what had happened to me.
