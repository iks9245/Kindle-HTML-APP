Cognitive scientists distinguish between what are popularly known as Type-1 and Type-2 mental processes. The first are quick, unconscious, associative thoughts, while the second are slower, deliberate and conscious, mediated by the frontal lobe and executive control network. A 2020 study put EEG beanies on the heads of 32 jazz guitarists and asked them to improvise riffs while their brain activity was scanned. ‘Higher-quality improvisations were associated with predominantly posterior left-hemisphere activity,’ the study found, while ‘lower-quality improvisations were associated with right temporo-parietal and fronto-polar activity.’ This suggests that the creative process takes place – or takes place best – in the automatic and unconscious left posterior part of the brain, sometimes described as the ‘default’ system, rather than the more conscious, deliberative ‘control’ system.
