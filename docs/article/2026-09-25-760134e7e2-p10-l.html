小中大切換為無襯線

Where a land ethic blooms

I crest and ease on over oak-dappled ridges – glacial moraines – that attest to the furthest advance of the recent Ice Age glaciers and the fire-centric economy of the people who covered this land with effigy mounds and who later began to grow corn and other crops in fields they weeded using the shoulder blades of bison. Further west, a narrow, rusty-girdered bridge threads over the Mississippi River, carrying water that fell as rain and snow on north country forests, coulee pastures in the tri-state Driftless Area, and the broad, flat corn-and-soybean lands of central and western Minnesota. Each fall, the skies above fill with white pelicans, bald eagles and a paradise of waterfowl. Against this pattern of gathering and flow, I’d continue west, west: chopping through the dried stalks of compass plant and tall tassels of big bluestem at Hayden Prairie State Preserve in Iowa and fording the Big Sioux beneath the watch of yucca and side oats grama.
