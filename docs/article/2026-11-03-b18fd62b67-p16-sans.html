Shrunken atoms open a window into other aspects of fundamental physics, too. The muons, kaons, or other electron substitutes orbit the atomic nucleus so tightly that they become sensitive to its interior structure. This was how the radius of the proton was measured in 2019. Like a miniature planet, the nucleus can be layered, with an outer crust or “skin” of neutrons. “You have a really beautiful microscope of seeing what’s happening at these neutron skins,” Paul said. Shrunken atoms might allow researchers to pick up on wholly new physics, such as deviations from Newtonian gravity or the existence of particles beyond the Standard Model. “We are looking into unknown physics at the extreme precision limit,” Curceanu said. “It’s complementary to extreme high energy.”
