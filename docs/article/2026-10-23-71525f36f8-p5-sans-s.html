Every policy issue we researched at the think tank led back inexorably to disconnection. It was labelled different things (overconsumption, fraying trust, the corrosion of democracy, crises of loneliness, mental health, care, climate, housing). But a thread linked them all: our inability to bear each other, to share (our stuff, space, skills, and deepest selves) was actively making the world worse. I came across thinkers who shared my intuition that something largely invisible but all-encompassing was getting in the way of our being together as healthier cultures remembered how to do. Some called it individualism or modernity. Others blamed ‘the machine’ of late-stage capitalism. I never settled on a name I liked, but I felt the disquiet of it. Still, all the intelligent analysis and critique never quite seemed to lead to progressive action, but instead to a conservative turn that half-appealed in its communitarianism and rooted localism, but not in its approach to women and ‘outsiders’.
