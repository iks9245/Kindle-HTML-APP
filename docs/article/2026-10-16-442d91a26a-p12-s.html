And the type that we really concentrate on are called arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi, and we can get into where that name comes from. But these particular networks are like an open-pipe system. They’re literally like a tube. And in some senses, people think they’re the largest cells on Earth because they don’t have the septa that break it up into individual compartments.

And so a lot of what we do in the lab is growing these networks across sort of a transparent soil almost, like a way that you can see the networks and use microscopy to follow these networks and understand how they navigate space. We have developed together with biophysicists at the Biophysics Institute called AMOLF here in Amsterdam, these imaging robots that allow us to track individual nodes across these networks. So in any given experiment, we’re tracking about half a million of these nodes, these fungal decisions whether to go left or right or make a bridge when they fuse together.
