Chasing la dolce vita

Lina Stores deli in Soho, London, has been a pillar of Italian life in England for decades. It’s where we’d sometimes meet our paternal grandparents when visiting London from our home in France. My brothers and I would line up in front of the refrigerated counter and ask for our favourite ravioli, doing our utmost to avoid any slip-ups in Italian that would make the staff wince: ‘ricotta e spinaci’, ‘zucca’, ‘funghi porcini’. Back at our grandparents’ flat, Nonna served the ravioli on an elaborately laid table, and my grandfather appeared with freshly grated Parmigiano. He’d sprinkle the cheese evenly over our pasta; never too much, nor too little. How he did this, and with such speed, we didn’t know. If we had spaghetti, Nonna would wrap her right hand around ours and twirl the fork with us so we held just the right amount, the bespattered tablecloth damning proof of our slapdash habits at home.
