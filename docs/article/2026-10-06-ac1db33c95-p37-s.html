I do not see this as a pessimistic conclusion, but as a liberating one. Strawson’s critique helps me calibrate my ambitions: to aim for fidelity to the fragments rather than the false comfort of a whole. If one thinks – as many narrativists do – that the essential task is to shape a unified self-narrative, the temptation is always there to smooth over ruptures, play down contradictions, and tidy away whatever doesn’t fit. The drive for narrative risks crowding out the unpredictable, the messy and the recalcitrant that make up much of our lived experience.

What I resist is not coherence but the imperative to impose it

The process matters far more to me than the result; even so, I have not ruled out publishing the final text as a book. But that intention sits uneasily with everything I have just argued: to edit the stream of episodes into a single volume risks imposing a coherence that was never there. Perhaps a narrative will emerge in the editing; perhaps it will not.
