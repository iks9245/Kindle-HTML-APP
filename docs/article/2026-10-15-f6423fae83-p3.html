It’s a convincing explanation for many experts — one that, crucially, may tell us something about the mystery of dark matter.

Some scientists have suggested that dark matter, which has yet to be directly detected, may not exist. Instead, they propose that a tweak to our understanding of the gravitational force might explain various features of the universe.

These bizarre galaxies pour cold water on that idea. “What this is showing is that dark matter is … not just a modification of gravity,” said Keim, who is an author of the new study. If you segregate ordinary matter from dark matter, you’d expect that the dark matter–lacking galaxies would struggle to form stars — and that’s what DF2, DF4, and DF9 show.
