小中大切換為襯線

Love with nowhere to go

Over the past few years I have accumulated many such losses. There is the psychological loss of my father as I knew and loved him: a brutal stroke rendered him incapable of walking and cycling, and cycling was the only thing that could ‘make his thoughts liquid’, as he used to say. There is the loss of old friendships. First you are no longer part of each other’s lives – you just update one another about your life – and then, one day, it becomes awkward to reach out at all. There is the gradual softening of an academic identity I’d spent the most vital years of my life constructing. It was a genuine vocation, a life of the mind that taught me to think slowly and carefully. But it was also, as I came to realise, a way of living safely at one remove from where actual life takes place. What is slipping away is a future in which being an academic philosopher would be the whole answer to the question of who I am.
