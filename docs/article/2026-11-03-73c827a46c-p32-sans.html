The self-making world

German evolutionary geneticists have found that wild guinea pigs subjected to heat-stress show heritable alterations in their livers, which play a crucial role in metabolic regulation; and biologists in the United States and Kenya have shown that the metabolisms of baboons also heritably change in response to differences in their food supply. In fact, it seems increasingly apparent that all kinds of living beings respond to their changing environments with heritable epigenetic transformations. These might provide means of rapid adaptation that precedes, and perhaps shapes, slower genetic changes.

Bird nests, beaver dams, termite mounds – the evidence of organisms creating their own environments is ubiquitous
