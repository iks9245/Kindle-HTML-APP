At that point, inserting these particles into atoms is easy. Atoms are surprisingly klutzy when it comes to their electrons. They are apt to lose them for all sorts of reasons, and exotic-atom formation exploits this tendency, with the novelty that the lost electron is replaced with an incoming particle. If the atom was originally in its lowest possible energy state, the new arrival immediately defines a new set of energy levels, like a fire-escape ladder that suddenly descends. The atom drops through the newly established energy levels. In the process it ejects all its remaining electrons and emits a series of X-rays.
