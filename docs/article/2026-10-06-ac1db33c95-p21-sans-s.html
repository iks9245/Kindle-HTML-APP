A life in episodes

What the post did not give me, the readers did in their comments. An old neighbour, Eva, who had known Lone, supplied a fuller portrait than I had been able to manage on my own; I had been writing, I admitted to her in the comments, ‘under the influence of my memory of the bad aspects of the relationship’. Another reader, Barbro, caught me in a single line: people would describe her own personality much as I had described Lone’s, she wrote – and she had never had diabetes. I had inadvertently conflated Lone’s difficult personality with her chronic illness in a way that exonerated my decision to leave her. A third reader, Kristina, asked what I thought the empty envelope had meant. I tried to deflect: I had been blank then and was blank now. She refused the deflection – she had not asked what I thought back then, she had asked what I think now. When I still had no answer, she offered one: a beautiful container with nothing inside, a friendship promised for a lifetime that turned out to be air. The process was forcing the container open, allowing a small, late act of atonement when another reader, Carolina, suggested I plant the daisies anywhere beautiful, since I had no grave to find. But I have still not planted them.
