This simile – shocking in multiple ways to a modern reader – aligns the recommended mode of speech with the embodiment characteristic of the free, adult, bodily intact male. Indeed, the right kind of speech makes clear the laudable moral character and manliness of the speaker: ‘I decline to regard as eloquence in any sense something which shows no trace at all of a normal male, let alone of a man of weight and integrity,’ Quintilian continues.
