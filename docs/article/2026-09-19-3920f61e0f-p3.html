小中大切換為無襯線

Cosmic amnesia

As I sit down with a coffee, I open my laptop to a wavy signal that gradually fades to a flat line. Two black holes have finished merging – a frenzied process of coalescence – leaving an extremely brief wiggle originating from the remnant black hole.

Much like the struck bell, this leftover black hole is ringing in a process called ringdown, and it’s consistent across every merger I investigate. Ringdown allows physicists to test their theories of gravity: did Albert Einstein really have the last word? Just as an off-sounding note from a bell might indicate a design flaw, a discrepancy in the frequencies of a ringing black hole could be the first crack in our understanding of gravity.
