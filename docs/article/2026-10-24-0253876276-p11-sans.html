In this process, the parasite also redecorates the cell’s exterior. It is equipped with genes that allow it to make a series of distinct but closely related adhesive proteins that it can express on the red blood cell’s surface. These proteins cause the cells to stick to the walls of the blood vessels around them — one of the main causes of malaria’s painful symptoms. In 2025, research from Deitsch’s lab found that by periodically switching between proteins, the parasite can avoid tipping off the immune system to its presence for days or weeks at a time. Such extensive exterior cellular remodeling serves a purpose: It helps Plasmodium avoid a trip to the spleen, an organ that filters out dead, defective, or broken red blood cells as well as any undercover parasites.

Rick Fairhurst and Jordan Zuspann, National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases, National Institutes of Health
