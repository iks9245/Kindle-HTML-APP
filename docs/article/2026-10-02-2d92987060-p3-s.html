Reason is more than a tool

In the 18th century, philosophers were concerned not only with the question of what reason is, but with what it is good for. Is reason essentially a servant, a clever instrument for achieving ends supplied from elsewhere? Or is it sovereign, capable of judging, revising and even legislating ends for itself? Can it ultimately shape the direction of human life, or is it merely along for the ride? Everything – from science to social progress to the viability of the French Revolution – seemed to hang in the balance.
