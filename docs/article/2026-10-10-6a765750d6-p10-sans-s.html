Barbara Ward’s vision

Ward outlined various measures to end economic inequality on a planetary scale: training local experts, transferring food surpluses to poorer countries, providing capital assistance in the form of a 1 per cent tax on national income, and advancing trade policies favourable to non-Western economies. Wealthy states, for Ward, should voluntarily embrace a more ambitious form of global fiscal redistribution because narrowing the world’s economic gap would benefit humanity as a whole. She maintained that encouraging economic growth in the ‘underdeveloped’ postcolonial world was in the West’s material interest. Yet, more importantly, it was its moral duty. Her proposals, pushing Cold War modernisation projects beyond strategic investment and reconstruction, struggled to generate political support at the time, but opened up discussions about the appropriate means to promote equality in the global economic order.
