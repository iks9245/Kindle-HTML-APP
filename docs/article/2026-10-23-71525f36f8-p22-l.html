The day the baby was born, while their mother was being stitched up and I was moving bins for the paramedics, my primary-school aged son was one of the first people to hold the tiny form. They’d wrapped the baby in a yellow foil survival bag, and he gazed down for long minutes with as much attention as if he was indeed holding a giant Quality Street toffee. This baby had moved from deep bodily entanglement, hidden in just one of us, to a less visible but no less profound interdependence. They have now been held by more arms than your average Western infant, and whether it’s mum, dad, us housemates, family, neighbours or church friends, we all adopt the same posture as my son. Face turned towards face. Eyes meeting eyes. The baby makes noises, and we make noises back. And as we gaze and gurgle, the mycelia of their brain are forming.
