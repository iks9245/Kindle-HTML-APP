Cuvier believed in extinctions and was a leading figure in establishing their reality. But he assumed that creatures went extinct because catastrophes, like floods and fires, eliminated them, and then those creatures were replaced by newly created ones. This idea supported the doctrine of special creation: that God had created each species for its place in nature. Cuvier’s science was perfectly suited to Napoleon’s purposes. It was a scientific doctrine of revolutionary change that was also profoundly conservative, each revolution imposed from the outside, each a return to the same ultimate authority, and the whole thing guaranteed by Napoleon’s own scientific experts rather than the Church. Cuvier’s science was cutting-edge, but at the same time, it bolstered an authoritarian order. It was emphatically not the science of the materialist, atheist revolutionaries; but nor was it the older science of the Catholic Church. Cuvier translated the authority of religion into science – a powerful modern concoction.
