I heard about them from Shinji Okada, a physicist at Chubu University in Japan. In spring 2026, his team used Swetz’s detectors and the Japan Proton Accelerator Research Complex to measure the structure of muonic hydrogen molecules, with the hydrogen taking the form of the isotope deuterium — the first direct measurement of X-rays emitted by such molecules. When they were smashed into molecules consisting of two ordinary hydrogen atoms, the muonic atoms implanted themselves within those molecules and assimilated one of their hydrogens. This created what Okada called a “matryoshka molecule” — an exotic molecule embedded inside a larger ordinary molecule.
