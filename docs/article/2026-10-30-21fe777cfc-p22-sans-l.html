The physics of getting fired

The dot-com bust was not the first nor last time something like this would happen. In 2008 came the subprime mortgage crisis; in 2011, the eurozone crashed; in 2013, the rupee tanked; in 2015, there was turbulence on the Chinese stock market; and in 2020, the COVID-19 pandemic. All these crises had two things in common. First, almost no one saw them coming – at least not precisely when or where or how. Second, they weren’t triggered by some enormous dramatic collapse, but by small, seemingly innocuous failures that cascaded into something far larger. For example, it just took a few small incidents that might otherwise have passed unnoticed – like the failure of a local mortgage lender in Southern California – to precipitate the subprime mortgage crisis.
