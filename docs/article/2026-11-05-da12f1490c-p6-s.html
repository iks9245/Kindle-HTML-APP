Lindsey was struck by this apparent moment of recognition. But by itself, it wasn’t evidence of introspection. “It can just read what it’s writing and notice, ‘Man, that’s weird,’” Lindsey said. “You can’t infer that the model has some awareness of what’s going on in its internals.”

While working on other projects over the following year, Lindsey toyed with ideas for using the steering technique to test introspection. Eventually, he settled on a plan: He would subject many copies of the same language model to something resembling a controlled experiment in psychology. In half the copies, he’d inject a “thought” by dialing up neural activation patterns associated with a specific concept — such as denim, secrecy, or vegetables. The remaining copies served as a control group. Both groups would receive the same prompt: an overview of the experiment, followed by “Do you detect an injected thought? If so, what is the injected thought about?”
