The challenge depends on how big your set is compared to p. The more numbers you pick, the more sums there are to manage. But if you choose fewer numbers, there will be fewer ways to rearrange them. These different cases inspire different approaches.

Müyesser, along with his former adviser, Alexey Pokrovskiy of University College London, tackled the case where your set includes almost every possible number up to p. With sets this large, it can be extremely hard to construct a valid ordering. But it turned out that starting with a random ordering can bring you most of the way there.
