This has made developing new malaria treatments notoriously difficult. The former workhorse antimalarial drugs, such as chloroquine, sulfadoxine, and pyrimethamine, are now known to target the parasites in the bloodstream. But the Plasmodium species that infect humans are rapidly evolving resistance to these compounds.

Invasion offers a tantalizing choke point for new medicines. “If you block invasion, then you stop the parasite in its tracks,” Ho said. To make her point, her team used an AI-powered protein-design platform to craft an inhibitory protein that could block the moving junction. In test tubes, it prevented Plasmodium from entering red blood cells. But its safety and efficacy haven’t been tested in animal models.
