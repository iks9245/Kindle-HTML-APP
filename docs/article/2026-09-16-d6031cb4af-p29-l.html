小中大切換為無襯線

Chasing la dolce vita

I’d like to think that there exists a worldwide collective remembrance of all things Italian, even from the days before the Risorgimento, or the movement for unification. I want to believe the countless stories of regions, cities and villages, of injustices and glories, are being kept safe by millions of descendants of emigrants who have become fervent storytellers. They are the unsung chroniclers of the peninsula, the guardians of a rich repository of traditions and customs, precisely because they have had to work harder to preserve this heritage than natal Italians. In families everywhere, from Argentina, Brazil, Canada, the US, Uruguay and Australia to France and the UK, traces of Italy remain in photographs, books and art, in buildings and the rituals of food. It is one of the paradoxes of diasporas that it is those who leave who want their country of origin to be fixed in time and preserved a certain way.
