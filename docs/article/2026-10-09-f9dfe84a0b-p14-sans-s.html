However, the Venetian government quickly realised that it had made a mistake. Although it had seemed easy enough to get rid of the Jews, it proved almost impossible to do without them. Even at a time of economic recovery, ordinary people still needed Jewish loans – and, despite everything, the Jewish moneylenders still wanted their business. It did not take the ebrei Tedeschi long to find a way round the ban. After their 15 days in Venice were up, they simply took a boat to Mestre, stopped for as short a time as was needed to reset the clock, and then went straight back to the city. In this way, they were able to maintain an almost permanent presence in Venice – without ever ‘officially’ residing there.
