Love with nowhere to go

Arthur Schopenhauer, unlike many before him, did not despise grief. In The World as Will and Representation (1818), he values sorrow for one reason only: great personal suffering breaks the will and leads to self-renunciation (Selbstverneinung). Grief is worth having, then, because it has the power to cure us of wanting the world at all, which means that, in the end, he is not so far from the thinkers who came before him. A good life, for all of them, is a matter of loosening our attachments to what is impermanent and fragile.
