Other scientists have also begun experimenting with manipulating energy flux. De Serio, at the Polytechnic University of Bari, has been firing strong jets into large tanks of turbulent, rotating fluids with high viscosity. “I was attracted by this idea that these [manipulations] could be applied in a real context,” she said. In March 2026, she reported results from experiments conducted on a large rotating vortex manipulated with small, horizontal jets firing into the middle. She observed patches of inverse energy flux that started out as intermittent bursts but grew steady with increasing rotation. Those findings, she says, may point to practical strategies for controlling energy pathways in real-world situations.
