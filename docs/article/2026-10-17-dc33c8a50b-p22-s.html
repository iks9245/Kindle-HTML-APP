With these speeches, Cicero managed to rally his fellow senators, at least for a time, but, in the end, the power of those who commanded the legions was unstoppable. In 43 BCE, Antony eventually joined forces with Julius Caesar’s great-nephew and heir, Octavian. They rapidly gained control of the Roman Empire – and engineered the deaths of prominent opponents, among them Cicero. When a posse of legionaries finally caught up with him, Cicero is said to have offered them his neck, meeting his end with great bravery.
