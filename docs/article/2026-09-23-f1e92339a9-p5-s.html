"Seventy-five years ago we entered into an agreement about the defence of Greenland. Today, we're signing an even bigger and better agreement - a deal that can last forever."

The Danish prime minister stressed that the agreement "respects sovereignty and territorial integrity of the Kingdom of Denmark, as well as Greenland's right to self-determination".

Jens-Frederik Nielsen said that "for Greenland, it is not only an agreement about us, it is [an] agreement with us.

"It takes into account the people of Greenland, our rights, our livelihoods and the development of our country.

"Greenland matters for North American security and for transatlantic security," he said, adding that the Nato had a key role to play in the North Atlantic in a changing security situation.

The agreement also confirmed Greenland would remain in Nato if it were to gain independence from Denmark.
