Scores of intrepid adventure motorists were electrified by the possibility of such a road, and they believed the best way to support the proposal was to drive it, even before contractors had broken any ground. Oliveira, short in stature but ruggedly muscular, sought a little personal glory, he confessed, and none could blame him for that. But the notion that most resonated with his irrepressible idealism was the raw concept of unrestricted mobility, the agency and facility to go anywhere in the world a person cared to go. The last Western nation to abolish slavery, Brazil codified in its 1891 constitution every citizen’s right to move about the landscape without legal or physical restraint, and to reside, work and form relationships wherever they chose. For Oliveira, the highway and the car represented the medium and the means that could make free movement an achievable reality on a new scale. His long, goodwill drive was launched to celebrate these ideals, and to make them concrete.
