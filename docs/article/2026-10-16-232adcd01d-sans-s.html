The secrets of Hohle Fels cave

Hohle Fels cave in southern Germany has been a place of great interest to archaeologists since it was first excavated in 1870. Discoveries at the site date back as far as 43,000 years and include some of the earliest figurative art and oldest musical instruments ever unearthed. As the Hohle Fels excavation leader Nicholas Conard discusses in this short video, such discoveries point to complex Ice Age cultures built on myths, beliefs and rituals – that is to say, societies that were in many ways not so different from our own. In particular, Conard turns to the recent discovery of two tiny ivory birds at the site to explore why he believes a sophisticated symbolic culture may have given humans an evolutionary advantage over other hominins, including Neanderthals.
