By 1999, Habermas gave up a position he’d held since 1973, the year he’d argued that truth is simply whatever people would agree on if they argued it out under fair-enough conditions. By the end of the century, he’d decided this was a mistake: it confused truth with justification. A claim can meet every test we have for it, survive every objection anyone raises, be about as justified as a claim can be, and still turn out false. That’s because justification answers only to the standards we happen to hold right now, and those standards can later be shown wrong. Truth doesn’t move when our standards do. Moral claims were different, he still thought: what’s right can only ever be what a fair conversation would settle on. There’s nothing else for rightness to answer to. But facts about the world answer to the world itself, not just to our best current agreement about it.
