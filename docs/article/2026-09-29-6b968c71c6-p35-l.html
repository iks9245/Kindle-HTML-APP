Paternity is poetical

Lowell knew that Life Studies was different. He explained to an acquaintance: ‘It’s all quite a different style, uses a lot of free verse, is intelligible to anyone on the surface, and heavily and directly autobiographical.’ This is a good definition of what soon became known as ‘confessional poetry’, arguably the dominant mode of US poetry of the second half of the 20th century, and which was first named in a review of Life Studies. Lowell’s volume won the National Book Award and has been celebrated ever since. Lowell himself saw the breakthrough in a humbler register. As he went on in his letter: it was only now, in ‘the years of my second marriage, and the birth of my daughter, that I have written to my mind what I really have had to say.’
