The mathematician Edward Lorenz discovered something similar in 1961 when running weather simulations. His first attempt entered starting values rounded to six decimal places. His second try was rounded only to three. Yet the results diverged so dramatically that he initially suspected it was a computer fault. In fact, tiny differences in initial conditions had cascaded into entirely different weather systems. The atmosphere, like the economy, like a career, was deterministic in principle and unpredictable in practice.
