Jürgen Habermas died on 14 March 2026, in the German town of Starnberg, aged 96. Every fall, from the mid-1990s into the late-2000s, he would come to Northwestern University in Evanston, Illinois to teach and give talks, a visit that a dedicated group of students looked forward to every fall term. The year that has stayed with me is 2004, when he taught what he’d spelled out in Between Facts and Norms (1992), his opus applying his theory to democratic politics. Law, philosophy, history, political science: students spanned many disciplines to come watch the famous German scholar every week, dressed in Wrangler jeans and a tweed jacket, expose his theory of deliberative politics in his unmistakable nasal voice, thick German accent, and impeccable English. Max Weber and the Frankfurt School, which were intimate to him, since he’d been Theodor Adorno’s research assistant in the 1950s, came to life in his speech and then in schemas and figures in chalk as he theorised about them.
