Over the last decade, Green and other researchers built advanced statistical frameworks to tackle the calculation. In 2024, a separate team used these tools to create a continuous three-dimensional map of the density of the ISM’s dust inside a sphere more than 4,000 light-years wide around our sun.

The dust map showed researchers where interstellar dust was concentrated, revealing the dense clouds scattered through the space between stars. But it couldn’t tell them how hot or cold that dust was. To understand the full range of ISM temperatures, researchers would need a second map to reveal how it gets heated. That map would come from an unlikely source.
