US unveils sanctions on ICC in move court condemns as 'assault on rule of law'

The UK joined Canada, Denmark, Germany, France, Italy, Japan and the Netherlands in reaffirming their "continued and unwavering support" for the ICC and said they "regret and strongly disagree with" the sanctions.

They also praised the court's staff for their work bringing "those accused of the most serious international crimes to justice", as well as supporting victims.

The ICC was founded in 2002 to prosecute war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide, and counts 125 countries as member states. Several major countries remain outside the ICC, including the US, Russia, China, India and Israel.

The Trump administration argues the ICC is run by what it calls globalists who threaten American sovereignty.
