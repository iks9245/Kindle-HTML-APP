Ward rejected the catastrophic pessimism of her fellow economists, who challenged existing growth models, but echoed their concerns about the planet as a fragile, vulnerable environment that needed to be preserved for future generations. Moreover, she was sympathetic to the claims of ‘developing countries to get their peoples out of a trap of poverty’ through economic growth based on agricultural and industrial innovation. Yet she did not think that these two aims – economic and environmental justice – were necessarily at odds. Optimistically, she suggested that economic wellbeing and planetary wellbeing could be reconciled if humanity acted collectively, morally and responsibly.
