The three leaders then inked the deal, seemingly ending more than a year of tension over the Arctic territory.

Trump had threatened to "take" the semi-autonomous Arctic territory over what he said were national security concerns, citing its strategic defence location and its mineral wealth.

His rhetoric prompted outcry from Greenlanders, Danes and European leaders who issued a joint statement saying his words "undermine transatlantic relations and risk a dangerous downward spiral".

The deal amends and updates a 1951 defence agreement between Denmark and the US.

The new pact says the US "shall be allowed to establish an additional Defence Area at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig" - referring to locations in Greenland's south and east.
