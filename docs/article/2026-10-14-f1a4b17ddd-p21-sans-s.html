Deskilling the body

Of course, human beings have always outsourced some agency into shared structures, be they laws, public health systems or institutions. This has largely been to our benefit. But today our technologies capture attention, predict behaviour, automate choice and monetise our impulses, while keeping our bodies anchored to the machines on which we increasingly rely. These coordinating systems are often extractive and indifferent to our flourishing. We might imagine we’re escaping the body, but the technologies that make this seem possible depend on mines and cheap labour and come at a planetary cost. And if our technologies weaken the feedback loop between our bodies and the world, they may weaken it in both directions. At its worst, we become less sensitive not only to what the world is doing to us, but to what our own actions are doing to the world.

The trees and grasses I brush past as I go on my walk are also hard at work learning from each changing day
