His time-lapse video of this microscopic dance, which leads our gallery below and was recently honored in Nikon’s 2026 Small World in Motion Competition, reveals the close partnership between the organelles: chloroplasts (red) spin sunlight into sugar, and mitochondria (green) convert sugar into energy.

The Nikon Small World competition, established in 1974, honors excellence in microscopy. The annual contests have charted not only the evolution of creative photomicrography but also the progress of imaging technology.

Hickey — and many of the other honorees featured here — relied on a technique called confocal laser scanning microscopy.

Unlike a standard microscope, which illuminates an entire sample at once and can produce blurry images, a confocal microscope focuses on one depth level at a time. Researchers first “label” a sample with fluorescent dyes that attach to specific structures within the cell. A laser then illuminates a thin layer of the sample, and the microscope detects the resulting fluorescence. Scanning line by line, the system builds a detailed image.
