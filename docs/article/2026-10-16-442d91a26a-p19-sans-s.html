KIERS: Well, these networks, they really can support us in tackling some of the biggest challenges facing humanity today. I mean, they are incredibly important in regulating our climate. And that’s a really new field. So some work that we published in 2023 found that these mycorrhizal networks are processing about 13 billion tons of CO2 every year.

So that’s like roughly a third of emissions from fossil fuels. So to say that another way, plants are taking up all of this carbon from our atmosphere and then allocating about thirteen billion tons of that to networks every year. So they really help regulate our climate. And once that carbon is down in the soil, the networks bring it down deep where it can stay and not be released so much.
