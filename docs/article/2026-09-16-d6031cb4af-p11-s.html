小中大切換為無襯線

Chasing la dolce vita

Descendants of Italian migrants generally express an intense pride in their heritage, and many continue to live by its sociocultural tenets in spite of decades, or more, of absence. It is as if time away from Italy, and geographical distance, only amplify a yearning for it. Stanley Tucci, to cite but one well-known example (the same could probably be said of Lady Gaga or Francis Ford Coppola) begins his TV food series Searching for Italy (2021-22) by stating in his broad American accent: ‘I’m Italian on both sides.’

Every summer, as per my own family’s tradition, members of this diaspora return to Italy. They enjoy the peninsula in the heat-filled months: sea, mountains, food, art and music, and its dolce vita. Where Italians living in Italy might imbue their identity with more intrinsically regional elements, the diaspora, in its dissemination around the globe, carries with it a more all-encompassing interpretation of the country. Their version of Italy, like the one I grew up with, is capable of brushing over fractures and complications, and the darker sides of the nation, articulated as it is by ideals that extoll the peninsula as a connected whole.
