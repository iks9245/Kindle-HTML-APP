The solution? A twist. Instead of matching one rim to the other point by point, in the same order, the twist allowed the researchers to reverse the order of preferences around one of the rims — making the cycles equivalent. “Gluing with the twist is the only valid option,” Livson said.

If you twist a strip of paper and join its edges, you get a surface with only one side, called a Möbius strip. But what happens when you twist a tube? You end up with something remarkable and strange: a Klein bottle.
