The ringdown allows us to work out the size of the resulting black hole as it’s related to its mass and spin

A black hole has an equally, if not far more, complicated history. Yet it reveals almost none of this. It doesn’t matter what kind of star formed it, whether it had planets, or life, or whether it merged once, twice or a hundred times. An entire planet like Earth might once have fallen in, and the black hole would show no trace.

Why should this be true? While the analogy of the ringing bell is powerful when describing the ringdown, and close to the underlying physics, it takes us only so far. Ultimately, the no-hair theorem, and the deeper loss that accompanies it, can be explained only by the unique properties of a black hole itself.
