小中大切換為無襯線

Life on a hair trigger

We asked each person we spoke with to tell us the story of his life, then to reconstruct minor confrontations, conflicts averted, and those that escalated into violence. We asked what they had been thinking and feeling as these events unfolded, returning to a single question: ‘What was going through your mind when that happened?’ Our aim was to access a layer of cognition beneath the behaviour, and to understand how the men made sense of their own actions and those of others.

Our conversations revealed something important about what violence teaches the mind. Lives spent amid violence meant the men had learned to anticipate danger in ordinary moments and to meet it by fighting back. The premise of READI’s work, and our research, is that because this way of seeing was learned, it could also be unlearned.
