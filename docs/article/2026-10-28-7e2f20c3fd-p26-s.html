小中大切換為無襯線

Love with nowhere to go

Recently, while in the Netherlands, I visited the Kröller-Müller Museum with my father, the same father whose future was negated by a stroke. I looked at him from a distance, the man who once built his life around cycling – to France, to Rome, to Spain’s Santiago de Compostela – now hunched behind his rollator. We stood together in front of Vincent van Gogh’s painting At Eternity’s Gate (1890): an old man, face buried in his fists, clearly hurting. A sorrowful man, looking at a sorrowful man, looking at a sorrowful man painted by a sorrowful man. Years before he painted it, when he first drew the same figure for a lithograph, Van Gogh wrote that there can be an ‘unutterably moving’ quality, ‘something precious, something noble’, in the expression of an old man like that.
