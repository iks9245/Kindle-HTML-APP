In 2007, Graham Fleming of the University of California, Berkeley tested this idea using a light-harvesting complex of pigments and proteins from a bacterium. He and his colleagues pumped the pigments and proteins with extremely fast pulses of laser light to generate excitons, then probed them with follow-up pulses. This revealed synchronized “beats” that, at the time, seemed like evidence that excitons were interfering with each other via quantum coherence. Other researchers, including Scholes, performed similar experiments and found similar beats — even in experiments conducted at room temperature.
