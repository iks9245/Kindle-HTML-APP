enclosure like my school soccer court,

and saw the Hudson River once a day.

And like this, addressing his young daughter while shaving:

Dearest, I cannot loiter here

in lather like a polar bear.

These two poems are from Life Studies (1959). If they rhyme – and many don’t – it is in a sneaky, cat-like way. The line-lengths vary; these poems are no longer in set, regular patterns. Harriet was born at the start of January 1957. At the end of that year, Lowell sent a handful of new poems to his old friend William Carlos Williams with a note: ‘At forty I’ve written my first unmeasured verse.’
