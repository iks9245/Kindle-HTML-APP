But recording electrical activity from outside the skull limits the fidelity of the information you can gather. Jacobs’ lab specializes in intracranial brain recordings. People with severe epilepsy may have had electrodes placed inside their brain to help locate the source of their seizures. With roughly 100 electrodes placed in a particular brain region, and with permission from the patient, Jacobs and his team are able to capture high-resolution data — across both space and time — as the participants perform various thinking tasks.
