So we had four different geographically separated groups, and they all used different algorithms. And then we all came together to the building where I am sitting right now, the Black Hole Initiative at Harvard in July of 2018, and we were in the basement of this building, and the four teams put up their images, and they were all rings.

That was a moment where there was a lot of crying and screaming. Uh, and, and then we all, and then we spent the next six months trying to make those rings go away. So we would train the algorithms on everything except for rings.

So they became very good at imaging double sources, or they became very good at imaging filled disks. And then we would set them loose on the data, and they would always come back to the ring. And that’s when we also knew in a more robust way that the ring image was real.
