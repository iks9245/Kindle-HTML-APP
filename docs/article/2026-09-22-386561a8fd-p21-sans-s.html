Second, democracy requires the willingness to be persuaded. Deliberation is possible only if citizens hold their political views as probabilia rather than as dogma, and the current political crisis is partly an epistemological one. Of course, there was never a golden age of reasonable citizens. But the measurements are unambiguous about the direction in which we have been moving: roll-call analyses of the US Congress show ideological overlap between the parties shrinking from the 1970s and effectively gone by the 2000s, while survey work by Pew and others documents a sharp rise since the 1990s in partisans holding consistently ideological positions and viewing the other side as a threat to the nation. Much of the change, moreover, is affective rather than programmatic: partisans have diverged on actual policy far less than they have come to dislike one another, which is exactly the sort of commitment that evidence cannot touch, and social media and the fake-news economy have accelerated it.

Developing a crystallised self that never updates is not integrity: it is rigidity dressed up as character
