And so what we’re doing now is working with all these data sets and all of these scientists all over the world. I think now there’s about 600 SPUN associates in, like, over 80 countries, and we’re working together to create something called the Underground Atlas. And that’s this digital tool that allows decision-makers to actually start quantifying and tracking and protecting hidden underground fungal communities. We’re asking, where’s the Amazon of the underground? Is it in the Amazon? Like, where are these hotspots of biodiversity, and are they being protected?

So I think it was in last year, in 2025, we had a big paper in Nature that showed that 90% of mycorrhizal biodiversity hotspots are outside protected areas. So only 10% of hotspots are actually inside protected areas currently. And so, we’re trying to get these maps to decision-makers and say like, “Listen, this is key biodiversity that’s been ignored for so long,” and it’s also a library of solutions to really important questions facing the world in terms of, like, how are we gonna restore degraded lands?
