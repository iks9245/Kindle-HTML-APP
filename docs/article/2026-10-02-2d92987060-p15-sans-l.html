Reason is more than a tool

The AI revolution has made this old dispute newly urgent by bringing the Humean view of reason back with a vengeance, this time in code. The route back to Hume was practical rather than philosophical. In the absence of any settled account of intelligence, much AI research has proceeded operationally: a system counts as intelligent insofar as it succeeds at a set task. But success is always success relative to some end and, in AI, that end is typically supplied by human engineers. A reinforcement-learning system learns to maximise cumulative reward. A chess engine searches for moves that increase its chances of winning. A navigation system finds the fastest route to a destination it did not choose. Across their enormous differences, these systems are judged by their ability to optimise externally programmed goals against standards of success they do not set.
