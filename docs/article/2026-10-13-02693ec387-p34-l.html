Is everything conscious?

A single thing with all properties and essential attributes fits the bill of both God and nature, hence Spinoza’s argument, that only one thing exists, is an argument for pantheism. But this can also be cast as an argument for panpsychism. The single existing substance is, according to Spinoza, both extended and thinking. Hence, thought is a fundamental and ubiquitous feature of reality. There is, however, scope for debate about whether the fundamentality and ubiquity of thought amounts to panpsychism since there is reason to believe that not all thought is conscious thought, in Spinoza’s system. But, at the very least, mentality is a fundamental property of matter, for Spinoza, and many are happy to equate this with consciousness and, thus, categorise Spinoza as a panpsychist. The US philosopher Martin Lin, for example, writes: ‘Spinoza is a panpsychist. For him, mentality is a pervasive and fundamental feature of the natural world.’
