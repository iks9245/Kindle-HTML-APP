Bohr’s contemporary Pascual Jordan spent several decades writing on Quantenbiologie, or quantum biology, arguing that life has a unique ability to amplify the strange indeterminism of the quantum world to macroscopic scales, and claiming this as the basis of human thought and free will. J.B.S. Haldane, a geneticist and evolutionary biologist, echoed Jordan in a 1934 paper arguing that the ability to scale up quantum indeterminacy was what made life special. (Jordan, who joined the Nazi Party and its paramilitary forces in 1933, damaged the credibility of quantum biology by attempting to link it to Nazism.)
