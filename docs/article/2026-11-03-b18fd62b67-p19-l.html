It took him an hour to walk me through the stages of the process. The system creates antiprotons and gradually bleeds off their energy. Some stages act on entire clumps of particles to slow them down, while others act within a clump to keep it from spreading out. One technique is to flood the chamber with electrons, which cool the antiprotons like so many ice cubes. Magnetic fields and an extreme vacuum — so extreme that no pressure gauge can measure it — ensure that the antiprotons don’t prematurely encounter ordinary protons, which would annihilate both. In 2021, CERN began operating the Extra Low Energy Antiproton ring, or ELENA, to do the final braking. Creating and slowing other electron surrogates such as muons is easier because they don’t pose the annihilation challenge.
