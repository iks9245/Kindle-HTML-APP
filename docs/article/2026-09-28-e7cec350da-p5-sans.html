The glowing pill under the city name shows it, for example "23 here now · 5 in Tokyo · 480 today · 12,345 all time": people with Lofi Cities open right now, how many of them are on your city, browsers that came by today, since midnight where you are, and every browser that has come by since the launch. When nobody else is on, it shows the rest ("480 here today · 12,345 all time"). The city picker (/) shows how many people are in each city right now. You can say hello to the people on your city too: pick a heart, note, star or moon with the Together button in the player (or G), then tap the scene to float it up from that spot on everyone's screen there, or release a paper lantern (L) that rises over the city for about a minute, in the same place for everyone, including people who arrive while it is in the air. The Chat button beside it (or Enter) lets you talk with the people on your city: short messages of up to 80 characters, without links, under a random two-word name such as "sleepy fox" that stays the same for your browser. There are no accounts: a random id in your browser's local storage (not a cookie) makes your tabs count as one person, the server forgets it about 25 hours after your last visit, and other people only ever see your two-word name. The all-time count is a single number: your browser notes once that it was counted, and the server keeps no list of who. Your page also tells it when your day began, so "today" starts at your midnight, and that is forgotten when you leave. Besides that it keeps the lanterns still in the air and each city's last 30 messages, for an hour at most. You can hide anyone's messages on your screen; when a few people hide the same person, their messages go for everyone and they can't chat for an hour.
