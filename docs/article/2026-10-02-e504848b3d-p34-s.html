So, for example, let’s move back down to a lower dimensional space. Let’s actually look at surfaces again. I gave two examples of surfaces before, which were the 2-sphere and the torus. It’s actually pretty hard the first time you learn about these objects to really verbalize what is the difference between them. When I give a talk, that’s a little bit more general audience, I’ll say something like, “Oh, well, the torus has a hole in it, and the sphere doesn’t have a hole.” But it’s pretty hard on average if I asked a student, “Okay, now tell me what a hole is.” Like, what does it mean that the torus has a hole? That’s a tricky question.

And so the way that I like to think about it is that, well, if I draw a loop on a 2-sphere, so think like the equator of the Earth, it will always divide the 2-sphere into two pieces. The equator divides the Earth into the Northern Hemisphere and the Southern Hemisphere, and you can’t go between them without crossing the equator.
