The ghetto on the lagoon

What made the Ghetto unusual was its name. Etymologically speaking, of course, the word geto had nothing to do with Jews. Derived from the verb gettare, meaning ‘to throw’ or ‘cast’, it was just the local name for a foundry (geto) – and, by extension, for the dump next door. But even after the Venetian government moved the foundry elsewhere, people kept on calling the area by its old name. And when the Jews moved in, the island soon lent its name to the institution of segregation, too – a bit like how, today, we can use ‘the White House’ to refer both to 1600 Pennsylvania Avenue and to the administration that’s based there.
