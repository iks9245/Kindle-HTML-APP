Briscoe and Rand’s dynamical-systems view provides some answers. It suggests that the landscape’s height reflects the stability of the cells’ states, with the least stable states on the highest peaks and the most stable in the lowest valleys. Time, meanwhile, is not literal time, but a kind of “pseudo-time” that reflects how the gene-expression profiles of all the cells change. In theory, all the cells can be ordered along a single pseudo-time axis so that the greater the change in a given cell’s state, the farther along the axis it is. But these changes won’t all be in sync, so there’s no ticking clock that applies to them all at any instant — hence, pseudo-time.
