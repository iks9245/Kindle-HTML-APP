小中大切換為襯線

Paternity is poetical

A man becomes a father and a poetic revolution follows. Not always, but often enough. Percy Shelley’s son William was born in January 1816. According to his biographer, also Richard Holmes, ‘after 1816: no other Romantic writer learned, and changed, and developed his art so swiftly.’ A century later, William Carlos Williams began what his biographer Paul Mariani describes as his ‘poem of initiation, his early crossing, his coming to some sort of terms at last with his own imperfect, vibrant world’ when his wife was pregnant. Their son was born in January 1914, and the poem was published that spring. Eighty per cent of the men who have won the Nobel Prize in Literature or the Pulitzer Prize for poetry were fathers. Celebrated philosophers, on the other hand, tend to be childless.
