In that moment, I was an elated farm ecologist – I sensed that we shared a respect for the soil and a sense of responsibility to hold this ground. His decision could limit if not reverse soil erosion and perhaps build thicker topsoil, higher fertility and greater yields in the long run. In the short run, too, holding this ground with hay will be more profitable than buying seed, fertiliser and herbicide to plant hybrid corn. David uses all of those tools on his farm, yet they don’t drive all his farming decisions. He farms with a forethought that gives a fuller accounting of costs that come both out-of-pocket and at the expense of the health of the land.
