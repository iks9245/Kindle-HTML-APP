Six times over the course of two weeks, Johnson donned two pairs of protective gloves. Over the course of an hour, she and several students “silked” black widow spiders, which had been knocked unconscious by carbon dioxide. With tweezers, the scientists gently pulled the strand of silk from the spider’s spinnerets — the abdominal spigots that fibers emerge from — and reeled it in by winding it around a vial with a spinning motor.
