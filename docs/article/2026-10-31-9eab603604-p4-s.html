In praise of the dilettante

But what if the scheme has it backwards? What if the dilettante’s perpetual movement from one thing to another is not a failure of nerve but a different and defensible way of being, one whose exploratory zeal turns out to be just what a hyper-specialised, over-optimised and all-too-serious world needs most? Then the dilettante would turn out to be not a capricious buffoon but a kind of modern anti-hero. That, I argue, is a dilettantism worth defending.

First, let’s consider how ‘dilettante’ relates to some of its conceptual cousins: the amateur, the dabbler, the jack-of-all-trades, etc. First, the dilettante is by nature an amateur, ie, a non-expert, a non-professional. But amateurism is a necessary though not sufficient condition of dilettantism. Anyone can be an amateur at any particular thing: woodworking, birdwatching, astronomy, etc. The dilettante’s amateurism is wide-ranging over several interests, and tends to orient around the production and/or appreciation of art and beauty. Like the jack-of-all-trades, the dilettante is a multi-dimensional amateur. But the dilettante’s amateurism is typically of the aesthetic variety.
