Deskilling the body

After all, the brain is itself a physical organ, continually shaped by what the body does and encounters. Once we decide that the mind is the real human and the rest of the body only hardware, it becomes easier to disregard the physical work, including the neurocognitive work of a biological brain, through which we move, find our way, put thoughts into words, and recognise our own needs. A machine may do some of that work faster, but each act it takes over is one that the body no longer performs and that the body, including the brain, no longer learns through. The irony is that a project motivated by the desire to overcome bodily fragility is helping to produce more fragile bodies.

On top of all this, we have little evidence that either thought or intelligence actually work like machines, and almost no indication that life is an abstract system that might emerge in silicon or mathematics. Instead, a substantial body of work, especially in systems biology, points to a quite different conclusion: that life and intelligence emerge from the unique qualities and organisation of our self-maintaining bodies, rather than from abstract models of information and algorithms.
