What sets grief apart is how deeply the loss reaches into the fabric of your life: grief begins where your identity is disrupted. What is lost in the case of grief is not just some concrete, historical object, but a cluster of (future) possibilities that are tightly woven into your sense of identity, life structure or experiential world. When they go, you don’t just lose something: you lose a version of yourself, your life is turned upside down, and you come to inhabit, in a real sense, a different world. This is why death is grief’s most extreme instance, rather than its only one. When someone we love dies, every possibility – not only our own possibilities with them, but their possibilities too – is wiped out.
