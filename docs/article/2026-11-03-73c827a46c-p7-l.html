While many people think of giraffes when they hear Lamarck’s name, few know that he coined the word ‘biology’, in 1802. He needed a name to designate a new science he thought should exist: a science of all living beings, and only living beings. At the time, there was no such field. There were broader sciences, such as natural history, which included inanimate things like rocks, rivers and mountains; and there were narrower sciences, such as botany or zoology, which covered subsets of the living world. But Lamarck thought there should be a science of life. At the heart of his new science, he developed the first theory of what we now call ‘evolution’, although he didn’t call it that. He simply referred to ‘change’.
