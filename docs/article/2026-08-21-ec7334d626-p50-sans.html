Dolphins and other lovers

The words in the recordings of mythology that my Andoque friends shared with me during long mambeadero sessions, which likely originated decades or even centuries ago, are spoken now with languid and defeated voices. Listening to them is like swimming in an ocean of unfathomable wisdom. But we are swimming against the current.

And all at once, I am gasping for air, drowning. Little did I know when I left the forest, that the Andoque perspective had taken root in my mind, reshaping the world around me, recasting even my own life in its terms.

I check my phone: it’s already time. I stop the recording, silence the voices from the mambeadero, open the dating app, and send a message that I’m on my way. Before I leave, I put on my cap – just in case – to cover the blowhole.
