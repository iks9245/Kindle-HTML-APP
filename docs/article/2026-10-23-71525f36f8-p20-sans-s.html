Other people

The porousness of babies to their caregivers, and especially their need for face-to-face connection is widely understood. The neuropsychologist Allan Schore calls a baby an ‘open dynamic system’. What our house is helping me see is how much humans remain ‘open systems’, and how much mind/self/soul (all translations of the word ‘psyche’) arises within and between us. We are not closed selves, semaphoring information to each other and making free rational choices. We are always makers of each other – the act of mutual witnessing, or its lack, moulding us like wet clay.

One way of thinking about our unconventional living situation, then, is that it offers a way of choosing who we want to be formed by, as if consciously curating the powerful gaze of others. Our bet is that the real costs, in control, convenience and comfort, the exposure of being seen and known ‘with masks slipped from faces’, are worth it. I believe that it is a demanding but effective method of becoming the kind of people the world needs now.
