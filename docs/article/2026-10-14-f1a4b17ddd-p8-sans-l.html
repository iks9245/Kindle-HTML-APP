By the middle of the 20th century, the comparison between organisms and machines had been recast in the language of information. During the cybernetics revolution of the 1950s, the US computer scientist Norbert Wiener envisaged a future in which ‘messages between man and machines, between machines and man, and between machine and machine, are destined to play an ever-increasing part’. Writing in The Human Use of Human Beings (1950), he warned against wholesale automation, while also noting that ‘we are not stuff that abides, but patterns that perpetuate themselves’. A person could be imagined as a pattern of information surviving in another form.
