How to be a (Roman) man

Given the military might of ancient Rome, it is surely unsurprising that Romans often associated masculinity with courage in battle. The word virtus, a term cognate with the Roman vir (meaning man as opposed to woman), clearly means ‘courage in war’ when it is first recorded in the mid-5th century BCE. Courage in battle could be taken as the foremost manifestation of Roman masculinity. The historian Sallust, writing in the 40s BCE, praises earlier generations of Romans for their pre-eminent manliness in war: ‘whenever their virtus repelled dangers, they rendered aid to their allies and friends.’ And yet, while Rome continued to boast ever more notable military successes in the middle and later republic (from the 3rd to the 1st century BCE), the battlefield was by no means the only arena in which Roman masculinity might be put to the test.
