Horrible, unmanly, indispensable

Dissidents in Brescia (a subject city of Venice) also obtained weapons: testimony from 1539 shows how one alleged firearm offender, Antonio Maria, was accused of saying several times that ‘he desired nothing more than that the Emperor and the King of France should make an accord against our Venetian lords, and that he hoped to come with them to sack Brescia.’ Antonio Maria was further accused of ‘continually keeping in his house exiled men of a bad sort, who go out killing on the streets’; he had in his house around 25 guns of different types. While we typically learn about these cases from regime records, by reading between the lines it becomes clear that at least in some notionally criminal cases there was also a political motivation for gun ownership.
