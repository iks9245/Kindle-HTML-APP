Eventually, I came across the proceedings from a symposium I had not known about: the Ethnopharmacologic Search for Psychoactive Drugs, held at the University of California, San Francisco, from 28 to 30 January 1967. The time and place stood out to me immediately. Two weeks earlier, a couple of miles away in Golden Gate Park, Timothy Leary had told the Human Be-In crowd to ‘turn on, tune in, drop out’, a phrase that would become shorthand for the moral panic that soon surrounded psychedelics and particularly LSD. The symposium also opened with a letter by Albert Hofmann, the Swiss chemist best known for synthesising LSD and who had also led the work identifying psilocybin and psilocin as the active compounds of Psilocybe mexicana. The symposium covered compounds from many psychoactive plants and cultures, including a lecture on nutmeg delivered by Alexander Shulgin – the San Francisco Bay Area biochemist who is remembered most infamously (and perhaps unkindly) for his rediscovery and popularisation of MDMA (aka Ecstasy).
