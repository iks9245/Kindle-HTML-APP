Is There Anything Left in the Dream of Cold Fusion?

“The whole system is collapsed down,” said Nancy Paul, a physicist at the Kastler Brossel Laboratory in Paris. She is exploring using even heavier negatively charged particles to shrink atoms still further. “It’s so rare in physics, especially nowadays, where you get something where nothing has been done.”

Shrinking atoms intensifies the electric field within them. According to Coulomb’s law, which is the electric counterpart of Newton’s law of gravity, the electric field strengthens as the inverse square of distance. For a kaonic or antiprotonic atom, then, it scales up a millionfold. That provides a stress test of a central pillar of modern physics, quantum electrodynamics (QED), or how electromagnetism behaves at the quantum level. “It is the strongest Coulomb field that you can create in the lab to probe QED,” Paul said.
