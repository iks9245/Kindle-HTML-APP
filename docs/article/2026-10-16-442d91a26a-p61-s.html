KIERS: I think fungi really challenge so much of what we think we know about the living world. They challenge our understanding of sex. They challenge our understanding of defining what is an individual. They challenge our understanding of how something so small can have such magnitudes of effects at the global level, even though it’s such a small microbe, it’s almost invisible. And I think that’s what makes it so interesting to work with is that every day when you’re working with mycorrhizal fungi, you’re challenged. They make it so you never know what’s going to happen in a given experiment. They keep you guessing. We call them divas sometimes, in the nicest way possible, because they keep us coming back, because they open up this idea of curiosity-driven science.
