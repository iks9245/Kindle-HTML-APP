小中大切換為無襯線

Where a land ethic blooms

Can a community of well-connected but individually small actors hope to break agriculture out of its lock-in trap? There is little room for optimism, but our prospects are not hopeless. As David Orr reminds us: ‘Hope is a verb with its sleeves rolled up.’ When Orr encouraged us to be hopeful in times like these, perhaps he was continuing a literary conversation with Wendell Berry, a Kentucky hill country farmer and writer. Berry stated that he had no reason to be optimistic about the prospects of people becoming reconnected with the ‘wealth, health, knowledge, and pleasure of their country’, but still he concluded that ‘hope is one of our duties … The health of nature is the primary ground of hope – if we can find the humility and wisdom to accept nature as our teacher.’
