These are ancient skills. READI’s contribution was not inventing them but building a place where men could practise them. These changes required certain conditions. READI’s wages gave the men enough stability to keep returning, and staff created a space safe enough for them to lower their guard, even around those participants they previously considered rivals. The pay check may have been what initially brought them in, but it was not how they ultimately understood the programme. In fact, 70 per cent described READI primarily as an opportunity for personal growth rather than just a job, and they spoke at length about how it reshaped their closest relationships, their daily habits, even their sense of who they were.
