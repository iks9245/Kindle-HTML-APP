By the turn of the century, Spanish arquebusiers – named for the long, wooden-stocked gun with which they were armed – were doing precisely that on the battlefields of Italy. As the conflicts of 1494-1559 rolled on, the rulers of the peninsula’s multiple states promoted shooting in the interests of civic defence and military preparedness and, as they did, their own use of firearms shifted too.
