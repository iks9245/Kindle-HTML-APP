What he announced at the Königsberg meeting the day before Hilbert’s radio address, no one had expected. Taking Principia Mathematica as the articulation of mathematics within logic, Gödel showed that there were true mathematical statements that cannot be proved using the resources of Principia Mathematica alone. Among them is the statement ‘Principia Mathematica is consistent.’ The trick at the heart of the proof is to encode statements about the system within the system itself – a move with the same vertiginous quality as Russell’s letter to Frege. But where Russell’s letter had killed a system, Gödel’s theorem killed a programme.

The one-time prophet of the Vienna Circle left nothing of its philosophy standing

His argument was so general that it applied to any reasonable system for arithmetic. Hilbert’s dream of an axiomatic system capturing all of mathematics – or even just arithmetic – was over.
