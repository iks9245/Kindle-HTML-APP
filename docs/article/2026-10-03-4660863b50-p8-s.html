The danger these men read into the world is real; recall that more than a third of them had been shot. The difficulty is that a mind shaped by real danger does not confine its lessons to dangerous situations. It brings them into ambiguous ones, where the reading is wrong often enough to produce the very danger it anticipates. The outer world and the inner one are not separate. A world of violence sustains itself not only through guns, poverty and other social ills, but also through the ways of seeing it produces. Our research asks where these patterns of seeing and responding come from, why they persist, and how they can change.

Again and again, three patterns surfaced that could make future violence more likely.
