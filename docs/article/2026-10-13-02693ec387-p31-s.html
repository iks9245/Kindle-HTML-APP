小中大切換為無襯線

Is everything conscious?

Perhaps it’s misguided to expect to render the world intelligible, and we ought instead to just enjoy the process of contemplating its messy unintelligible glory, of pushing our philosophical boulder up the mountain. One way of engaging with this contemplative project is in the development and exploration of systematic philosophical theories. A messy, unintelligible world doesn’t preclude the construction of highly orderly philosophical edifices. But it would be a mistake to think these capable, even in principle, of definitively dissolving life’s mysteries. Philosophical theories such as panpsychism are better thought of as providing new and enriching perspectives on these mysteries, and we should remain open to the possibility of their collapse and replacement.

By giving up the all-encompassing demand for intelligibility and, instead, conceiving of philosophy as providing different perspectives on our condition, we can better understand what it is to make progress in philosophy. Widespread philosophical disagreement should not be taken as evidence that philosophy is degenerate compared with, say, the natural sciences. Rather, we should celebrate the fact that opposing views such as dualism, physicalism and panpsychism exist to provide us with richly diverse perspectives on life’s most tantalising mysteries.
