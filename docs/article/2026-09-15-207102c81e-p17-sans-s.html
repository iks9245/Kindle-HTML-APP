小中大切換為襯線

The bombarding of childhood

Some critics may dismiss slow media as nostalgia: a longing for the children’s television of the previous generation, or a privilege available only to parents with time and large bookshelves and big houses with quiet rooms. But that is a notion we should refuse. The attention economy has trained us to treat a child’s pause as a problem, a wandering gaze as lost engagement, and an unfilled moment as wasted time. The opposite is true – slow time gives children the space to perceive, process, respond and make meaning – and it can be designed for in children’s media.

Like energy drinks, fast media promise more capacity than the child actually has: more attention is supposed to lead to better performance. Fast media feel like an extension of the child’s energy, but the bargain is unsustainable and unhealthy. What looks like support can become dependency, and what promises engagement and learning is actually compulsion.
