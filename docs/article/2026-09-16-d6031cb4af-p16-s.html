小中大切換為無襯線

Chasing la dolce vita

Increasingly, I’m hankering after Italy, wanting to pass on its rituals. I’ve made sure my children have Italian first names and a basic knowledge of Italy’s geography and history, and they seem to accept this as a given. I’ve fed them stories about eagles and ancestors, accounts of my childhood holidays in Emilia-Romagna and Tuscany, snippets of my time studying in Padua and Florence. I refer back to weddings and family gatherings we’ve all been to, seeding these memories in them, tincturing them with the aura of official history. I tell them about films they should watch one day, by Antonioni and Visconti, of the writings of Boccaccio and Dante. This visceral attachment to Italy is something I feel compelled to pass on. All the while, in parallel, I know I must value the family environment my wife and I have built in England – which is their home. It is an acknowledgement, too, of the sacrifices my ancestors made.
