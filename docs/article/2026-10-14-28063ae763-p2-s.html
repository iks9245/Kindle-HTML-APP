OpenAI personnel found the device Sept. 30. It was plugged into a USB port on the back of a television in an executive conference room and had been concealed in toilet paper, the complaint says. The company determined it was installed around Sept. 28, and its contents were encrypted.

Surveillance footage shows Alfozan near the room on multiple dates before the discovery, though he was not authorized to enter the room or attend meetings there, prosecutors said. Meetings in the room frequently involved confidential technical, commercial, and strategic information, including proprietary research, product plans, and government engagements, according to the complaint.

Before the device was installed, Alfozan used a productivity tool to ask about OpenAI’s building addresses, meeting rooms, room capacities, and calendars, as well as the locations of leadership and outside partner meetings, the complaint alleges.
