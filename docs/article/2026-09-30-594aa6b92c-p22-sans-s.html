Grade group 1 prostate cancer – the lowest-grade form, with no immediate potential to spread or cause death – is exceedingly common. Among men who die in their 70s or 80s of non-cancer causes, these cells are found on autopsy in about half; among men in their 30s, the figure is about 20 per cent. They are not clinically meaningful cancers and may be better understood as a feature of normal male ageing. William’s low-grade prostate cancer was detected because he underwent an appropriate mid-life PSA screening, but it had likely been present for years before and was still many years away from threatening his life.
