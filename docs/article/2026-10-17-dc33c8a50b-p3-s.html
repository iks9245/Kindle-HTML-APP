How to be a (Roman) man

For Roman men, anything labelled feminine was necessarily to be avoided, something to be ashamed of. But what exactly did it mean to act like a man? Ancient Rome in the 2nd and 1st centuries BCE saw the rise of a succession of military leaders who were promoted as masculine role models. The great general Marius won a series of victories in Spain, North Africa and on Italy’s northern frontier. For several years in a row, he was elected consul, one of Rome’s two chief executive officers. Unlike many other Roman politicians, he didn’t have an aristocratic background. One Roman writer imagines Marius fending off accusations that he was coarse in his manners:

elegance is becoming to women but toil to men. All virtuous men ought to have more fame than riches. Arms, not fine trappings, bring glory.

– from The War Against Jugurtha by Sallust
