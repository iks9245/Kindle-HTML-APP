小中大切換為襯線

Love with nowhere to go

But the Western tradition had it backwards. Suppressing, eradicating, denying or pathologising grief doesn’t protect the good life; it sabotages human flourishing by cutting us off from the things we love. More than that, it cuts us off from each other. Grief named and disclosed is grief that can be met, grief that can be placed between you and another person, where the two of you can look at it and hold it together. That shared vulnerability lets one person recognise another in their common humanity. Grief, as Weller reminds us, was never meant to be carried alone; it was meant to be held and processed by a whole village. Strip it of its name and you strip people of the possibility of being seen in their loss, of discovering that the wound they thought was theirs alone is in fact shared by those around them.
