小中大切換為襯線

Paternity is poetical

The two poems quoted here are taken from his collection Lord Weary’s Castle (1946), which was published when he was 29. Young Lowell loved rhyme. He later told an interviewer that he wrote his first poems unrhymed and then put the rhymes in after, like an obedient schoolboy doing his homework. Of the 42 poems in the collection, only one doesn’t rhyme completely and this has a complex system of repeated words at the line ends. Beneath the rhymes steps a cautious measure or rhythm. Try saying ‘I saw the spiders marching through the air’ aloud, or even tapping along: the 10 syllables and five balanced feet of iambic pentameter. Lowell followed this collection with a set of dramatic monologues in rhymed iambic pentameter, sometimes known as heroic couplets, published in 1951. He was taking form seriously.
