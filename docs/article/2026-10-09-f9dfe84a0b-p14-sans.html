But no sooner had Venice recovered its prosperity than the ‘need’ for such toleration began to wane and prejudice once again reared its ugly head. Disagreements sprang up. Threats were made. And, after briefly reviving the plan to create a separate Jewish quarter – albeit to segregate, rather than to welcome the community – the Venetian government decreed that, when the condotta expired in 1397, the Jews would have to leave.

With no other choice, the ebrei Tedeschi dolefully made their way back to Mestre. Although they were not forbidden to visit Venice, they would be allowed to stay only for 15 days at a time and were required to wear a yellow circle on their outer clothing at all times, so that they could be clearly identified – an obligation that had already been foisted on their coreligionists in Sicily, and which would subsequently be imposed in many other Italian cities.
