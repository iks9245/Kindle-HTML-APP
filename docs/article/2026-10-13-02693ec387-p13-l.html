Is everything conscious?

Contemporary philosophers who describe themselves as ‘Humean’, that is, as influenced by the Scottish Enlightenment philosopher David Hume, are explicit in their rejection of necessity and ultimate explanations. David Lewis, perhaps the most prominent Humean of the past century, maintained that ‘all there is to the world is a vast mosaic of local matters of particular fact, just one little thing and then another.’ That is to say, there are no reasons, and no necessity for what goes on in the world. In a similar spirit, the Dutch American philosopher Bas van Fraassen consistently challenges the need for a metaphysical explanatory structure. This manifests itself in the rejection of robust laws of nature that necessitate events in the Universe. Science, according to Van Fraassen, can get along just fine without deep explanations that make things necessary.
