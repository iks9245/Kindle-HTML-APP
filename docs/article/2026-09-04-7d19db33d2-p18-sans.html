These Victorian anxieties and competing impulses resonate today. At a Coldplay concert in Massachusetts in July 2025, the stadium camera landed on a man with his arms around a woman. The pair were Andy Byron, then chief executive of the data company Astronomer, and Kristin Cabot, its chief people officer. As soon as they realised that their embrace was being projected onto the jumbotron, Byron ducked out of sight and Cabot turned her face away. Coldplay’s Chris Martin joked from the stage that they were either having an affair or were simply very shy. The clip raced across social media, where online detectives identified the pair, investigated their private lives and transformed a few seconds of visible panic into a global morality play about an alleged affair. Both executives subsequently resigned. Yet the fascination lay not simply in what may or may not have happened between them, but in the spectacle of a carefully managed public surface suddenly collapsing. Under today’s norms, public displays of distress threaten to expose the precarious choreography sustaining the fiction that everything is under control.
