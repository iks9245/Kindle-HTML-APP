Reason is more than a tool

This view is often treated as too dark and cynical. That is unfair to Hume. For him, passions were not merely appetites or primitive urges. They included sympathy, benevolence, moral feeling, concern for others, love of tranquility, even a kind of longing for freedom from our more violent desires. To Hume’s further credit, his view has strong intuitive appeal. He was right that reason often serves passion, and right to give feeling, sociability and temperament their due. His mistake was to conclude that reason can do nothing more. By making passion so rich and reason so poor, Hume left us with a lopsided picture of human nature: ennobling about what the passions can be and accomplish, but implausibly restrictive about reason. Without reason’s power to project, assess and revise ends, we may know how to reach a destination, but not whether it is one worth choosing.
