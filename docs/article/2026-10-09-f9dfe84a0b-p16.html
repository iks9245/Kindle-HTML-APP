Naturally, the Venetian authorities tried to clamp down on such infractions. In 1402, they decreed that, after the 15 days were up, Jews could not return to the city for another four months. But there were so many exceptions that it was virtually impossible to enforce. Therefore, even though the Venetian government had no intention of revoking the Jews’ expulsion, it had to reconcile itself to the fact that a Jewish presence of sorts was inevitable – even desirable. As such, its approach shifted from expulsion to containment. Efforts were made to enforce the wearing of the yellow circle, to prohibit acts of worship, and to prevent ‘sexual pollution’. By the 1420s, however, there were just as many Jews in Venice as ever.
