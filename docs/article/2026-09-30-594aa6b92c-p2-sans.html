William came to me for a third opinion. He was 53 and healthy, with a wife and two teenage children, a busy job, and a typically hectic work-and-family schedule. He’d taken time to do the right thing and had visited his primary care provider for a routine health exam and blood tests, his first in many years. A few weeks later, he’d found himself in an unfamiliar urologist’s office, at the edge of an abyss, ears ringing with what may be three of the most dreaded words in medicine: ‘You have cancer.’ This diagnosis portends pain, suffering and death: in the United States alone, cancer kills more than 600,000 people a year, enough lives to empty a city.
