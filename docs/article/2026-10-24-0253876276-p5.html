The history of malaria research has paralleled advances in microscopy, from the development of simple light microscopes like the one Laveran used to the cutting-edge cryo-electron techniques of Ho’s lab. This is because the cells the microbes invade are extremely tiny — and the parasites are even smaller. At about 8 micrometers across, red blood cells are among the smallest cells in the human body, and all species of Plasmodium are a fraction of that size during the invasion stage.
