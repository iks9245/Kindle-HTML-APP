Reason is more than a tool

Part of what makes this model so attractive is that it is clean and measurable. It promises to turn the mystery of intelligence into a technical problem humans can solve: specify the objective, improve performance, reduce error, maximise reward. It offers a picture of mind that can be graphed, benchmarked, monetised and scaled. In a culture already devoted to metrics, this makes for a snug – and profitable – fit.

It is also seductive because viewing human intelligence in this way relieves us of a burden. If intelligence is really nothing more than optimisation, then the hardest, most uncomfortable questions can be ignored, downplayed or displaced. We no longer have to ask what is worth wanting, only how to get what we already want. We no longer have to ask what human beings are or should become, only how to model their behaviour and optimise their goals.
