There are broadly two perspectives on mathematics. According to one, we invent it as we construct it: we introduce concepts, choose axioms, and develop theories. According to the other, mathematics exists independently of these activities, and our task is to discover it. These perspectives need not disagree about every aspect of mathematical practice. We plainly develop formalism and arguments. The deeper question is whether the truths we come to understand owe their existence to these acts, or whether our constructions enable us to recognize something that does not depend on us.
