DOELEMAN: There was really a sense that black holes existed or strong evidence for them. And of course, there are two flavors of black holes. There are stellar-mass black holes which form during star death. So when a star goes supernova, it will explode the outer shells off, and then the interior part will collapse and if the star is massive enough, it will turn into a black hole, and that black hole could be tens of solar masses.

But then there are these behemoths, these supermassive black holes that live in the centers of galaxies. And while there can be many stellar-mass black holes in a galaxy, there’s probably just one, maybe two, supermassive black holes at the core of any given galaxy.

And for the stellar-mass black holes, people had seen these X-ray binaries, and they were seeing very fast fluctuations in X-rays and even in the radio from these stellar-sized sources. And everything fit with this idea that there was a black hole eating another star that goes an X-ray binary.
