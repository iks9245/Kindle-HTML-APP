The seal used its hind limbs as a paddle when swimming. They hadn’t become a fin because the seal also used them for seizing prey

As evidence of animals’ power of creation and self-transformation, Lamarck cited many examples in which the increased use of a body part had apparently developed and shaped it: the webbed feet of water birds from pushing against the water; the hooked talons of eagles from clasping branches; shorebirds’ long, skinny legs and necks. Lamarck reckoned that shorebirds dislike the water yet have to wade out to catch their prey. Sinking into the mud, they stretch their legs in distaste, straining to keep their body above water, and craning their necks. This behaviour over many generations resulted in stilt-like legs and snaky necks.
