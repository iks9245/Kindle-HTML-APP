These unruly genetic fragments are known as transposable elements, or transposons for short. Often called jumping genes for their ability to relocate in a genome, they may seem pathological — indeed, many have viral origins — or perhaps little more than junk. But transposons are increasingly understood to be a key feature of many genetic tool kits. Their connections to the evolution of everything from moths to wombs, and even to the fundamental biological processes that turn genes off and on, suggest that the relationship between host genome and transposon is best understood as a deep coevolutionary entanglement.
