Terrel, 29, had endured a childhood of compounding losses. His mother was incarcerated, his little sister killed in a house fire, and he was shot – all before finishing the eighth grade. ‘I was an angry-ass little kid,’ he recalled. ‘I was angry because my momma was gone … My little sister dead … Everything I got is gone, so I’m angry.’ Terrel came to read his anger and the violent behaviour it spawned as a verdict on himself. Beneath the easy smile others saw, he felt he was ‘just covering up [what’s inside] with that smile’: ‘I been fucking up the community for years. How the fuck am I good?’
