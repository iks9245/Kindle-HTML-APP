The bombarding of childhood

The child’s attention is kept in motion by a manipulative rhythm that continuously restarts attention with the next tune, next bright image, next comic beat

Conversations between characters are another way in which higher-ranked shows convey new concepts with appropriate pace. When introduced with context, a new word or idea might not be immediately clear, but the child can notice and infer. Over time, the child arrives at understanding, and this slow, even arduous process helps the concept stick. For example, in one episode of Daniel Tiger’s Neighbourhood (2012-) – based on Mister Rogers’ Neighborhood – Daniel is nervous about trying the swing. The scene allows that feeling to be acknowledged, sung through, and gradually transformed into confidence, with support from Daniel’s friend Jodi and her grandmother. Similarly, in the Sesame Street-based game app Elmo Loves ABCs, a letter is traced, sounded, repeated, connected to words, and encountered through touch and play.
