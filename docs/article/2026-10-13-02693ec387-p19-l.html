Is everything conscious?

However closely we look at the physical matter that makes up conscious beings, we get no closer to understanding how consciousness arises. Leibniz encouraged us to imagine increasing the size of the human brain to the size of a mill so that we can walk around inside it and observe all its working parts in meticulous detail. If we did this, at no point would we observe conscious experience. Conscious experience, by its very nature, is private, first-personal. Since scientific methods can yield knowledge only of the objective, third-person-accessible facts regarding matter in motion, there is an essential mystery concerning the origin of consciousness. The question of why some physical states, but not others, are conscious seems unanswerable.
