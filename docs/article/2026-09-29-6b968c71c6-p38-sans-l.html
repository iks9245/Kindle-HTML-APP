小中大切換為襯線

Paternity is poetical

‘Parenting involves heightened emotional and cognitive demands,’ observes the study led by Vaccaro: ‘further emphasising the brain’s role in integrating diverse experiences into a coherent sense of purpose.’ Here is T S Eliot, who was not a father, defining the poet’s task in 1921: ‘When a poet’s mind is perfectly equipped for its work, it is constantly amalgamating disparate experience.’ The coincidence of language here points to an idea that we have often overlooked: that fatherhood and artistic creation are not antagonistic but congenial; that becoming a father might, quite simply, improve a new father’s poems.
