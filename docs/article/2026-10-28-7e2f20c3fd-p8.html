小中大切換為無襯線

Love with nowhere to go

Death is visible and final: it has a body, a date, a medical certificate. The other losses are blurrier: they have no physical object, no clear beginning or end, no tangible proof they happened. We also have an infrastructure for death: funerals, condolence cards, flowers, casseroles, bereavement leave – a whole social script. Perhaps the infrastructure defines what counts: if there is no social and cultural scaffolding to digest the experience, it must not be real grief.

The prime reason for the single-minded focus on death loss, however, must be that bereavement is simply the most striking instance of a broader emotional phenomenon that comes in degrees. But restricting grief to bereavement is like restricting pain to cluster headaches, anxiety to panic attacks, sadness to depression, or pleasure to ecstasy – it is taking the most extreme instantiation of something and making it the essence of that thing.
