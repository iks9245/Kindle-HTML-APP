To answer this question, the new work builds on a model that Yuliy Baryshnikov, now a professor at the University of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign, proposed in 1993. First, consider a single voter’s ballot in an election with three candidates. There are six possible ways to rank the options. Each one can be represented by a triangle:

Mark Belan/Quanta Magazine

Each corner of this triangle represents one of the comparisons the ballot implies: Alice over Bob, Bob over Carol, and Alice over Carol.

A different ballot listing Alice first, Carol second, and Bob third agrees on two of those comparisons, since both ballots put Alice above her rivals. Their triangles therefore share two corners and can be joined along the edge connecting them.
