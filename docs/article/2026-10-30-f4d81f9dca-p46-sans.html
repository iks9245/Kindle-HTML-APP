How Do You Take a Picture of a Black Hole?

DOELEMAN: You know, it all makes sense when you look back on it. Of course, Sagittarius A* was the preferred candidate. We’re all in love with our own supermassive black hole in the center of the Milky Way galaxy.

But there’s a problem with Sagittarius A* as much as we love it, because it’s only 4 million solar masses, the time it takes matter to orbit Sagittarius A* is only about half an hour. Now, what that means is that during a night of observing, the thing you’re trying to take an image of is changing in its appearance. Things are orbiting around it, it’s burbling, it’s turbulent, and it looks different from one moment to the next. In aggregate, averaged, you would see this ring, but from moment to moment it would change.
