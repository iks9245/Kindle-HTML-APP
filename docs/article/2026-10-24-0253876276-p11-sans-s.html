小中大切換為襯線

Malaria Parasite Caught in the Act of Breaking and Entering

The parasite itself undergoes a radical change inside the cell. It transforms from a roundish merozoite into an amoebalike trophozoite. It grows several cytoplasm “arms” and flattens out, said Christiaan van Ooij, a molecular parasitologist at Keele University in the United Kingdom. This also helps it evade the immune system. The trophozoite shape is more flexible than that of the spherical merozoite, and the immune system clocks supple cells as healthy and stiff cells as dead.

The parasite then replicates asexually for about 48 hours, dividing inside the red blood cell until it’s so full of Plasmodium that it bursts. Like house flippers, the parasites move on to other blood cells. Within days, millions could be lurking in the bloodstream.

In this way, malaria can quickly overwhelm a human host. It is all enabled by the moving junction that allows the parasites to set up shop without tipping off the authorities. The structure of the moving junction could help researchers intervene, with drugs to block the parasite from invading a host blood cell in the first place.
