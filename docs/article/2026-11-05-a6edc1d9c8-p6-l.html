But Trump will instead face a motivated Democratic majority in Congress that is eager to investigate the administration, block his judicial nominees and stymie the rest of his agenda.

The midterms have ushered in an uncertain new phase in Trump's second term and may force Republicans to start grappling with their post-Trump future.

"I don't think his influence is slipping," said Steve Scheffler, the Republican National Committeeman in Iowa, speaking on the eve of the election. Still, he said, when it came to the midterms, "there were things you wish he said differently".
