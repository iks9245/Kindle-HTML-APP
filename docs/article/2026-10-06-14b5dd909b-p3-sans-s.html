France braces for national day of school protests after injuries and mass arrests

On Sunday, French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu announced a series of measures to "address the difficulties faced by secondary school students and education staff", promising "visible changes" in schools.

However, he also said: "We will make no concessions to violence. We stand by students and staff who have been assaulted, as well as police officers and gendarmes who have been injured."

The unrest has not only seen schools blockaded, but also rubbish bins set on fire. Education Minister Édouard Geffray said 24 schools had been "burned and ransacked" since the start of the protests.

Violent clashes between students and the police have led to some serious injuries throughout the course of the protests so far.

On Monday, a 15-year-old boy's hand was blown off during a confrontation near a school in the northern city of Lens. The student was transferred to a nearby hospital and his injury is not life-threatening, according to officials.
