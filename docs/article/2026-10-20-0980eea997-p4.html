Militias were not new but, by the 1490s, large-scale foreign military intervention on the Italian peninsula was giving a new impetus to their development, as was the growing importance of infantry in the military context. In Friuli, where Venetian lands bordered Ottoman territory, the militia were using handguns from the 1490s, and city guards across the Italian peninsula were soon provided with guns too. The growth of militias was the single most important factor in the proliferation of firearms in 16th-century Italy. This is too early a period for the kind of autobiographical writing that would tell us what these men made of the process, but there was certainly ample encouragement to become familiar with firearms and acquire shooting skills.
