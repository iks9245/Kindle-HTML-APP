The situation is heartbreaking. Part of what is so wonderful about mathematics is its open and honest collaboration, and its independence from political or corporate concerns. That’s not to say that there haven’t been other problems with the culture of mathematics — inequity and lack of access, among other things — but what’s special about that culture is at great risk without a signal change in the field’s incentive structure.

“The existing equilibrium has broken,” said Daniel Litt, a mathematician at the University of Toronto. “We’ll have to find a new one.”
