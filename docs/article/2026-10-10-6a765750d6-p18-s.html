In the early 1970s, Ward offered an original and optimistic response to two contemporaneous concerns about growth. Thinkers in the Global South worried that there was not enough economic growth to generate wealth and prosperity in postcolonial countries. Without robust economic growth, these new states could not guarantee welfare services, healthcare and education to their citizens. Economic stagnation could threaten political stability and peace, always a significant concern during the Cold War. At the same time, Western thinkers warned that the relentless pursuit of economic growth in consumerist societies was destroying the natural environment. In 1972, the Club of Rome published its report, The Limits to Growth, endorsing plans of economic equilibrium rather than growth as a strategy for planetary ecological wellbeing.
