LEVIN: With these four-dimensional spaces and your intuition faltering, what are the kinds of questions that you’re asking? What are the hypotheses that you’re really pursuing?

MILLER: So the thing that I study most commonly is the study of knotted surfaces. So in dimension three, the analogous object would be a knot. Knot — k-n-o-t. So a knot is a loop. If I took a piece of string… Wait, I actually, I have a string. If I took a piece of string, and I actually tangled it up, but then I glued the ends together to make one closed loop, then I would call that a knot.

LEVIN: Right.
