"I'm not naïve. I know there are a lot of more militant voices from young generations," he said. "I don't know if Eisenkot will be better than Golan in terms of security, but I think we have bigger problems: economics, social problems, education, health. We didn't get new neighbours on October 7 - it was a major event, but didn't change how we see ourselves."

Threats to security versus threats to democracy - these are old political tramlines for Israeli voters, deepened by the 7 October attacks, and by four years of the most right-wing government in Israel's history.

But on some of the biggest international concerns, the differences between government and opposition are not as wide as it might seem.

Gadi Eisenkot has pledged to end Hamas rule of Gaza within 18 months, either through a US-led peace plan, or through "military and operational capabilities".
