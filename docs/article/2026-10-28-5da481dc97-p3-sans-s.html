Norway and Finland leaders hold rare call with Putin

Finland and Norway, which border Russia, initiated the 90-minute call - held from Helsinki - to "discuss Russia's war of aggression against Ukraine and the diplomatic efforts to end the war and promote peace and stability in Europe", the leaders' offices said.

Støre added: "The war comes with enormous human and material costs... The suffering and destruction will impact generations."

The Kremlin said Putin told the leaders that "a just and lasting peace" would only be possible if Russia's interests were considered.

Putin also reaffirmed to the Nordic leaders what he views as the "root causes" of the conflict - his frequent shorthand for Ukraine's desire to exist as a sovereign state.

"A fair long-term peace settlement is possible only if Russia's legal interests are taken into account fully and unconditionally", Putin said.

All three parties reportedly agreed to continue their direct phone communication.
