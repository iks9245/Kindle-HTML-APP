Other people

Isolation allows us to maintain all kinds of illusions about ourselves and about others. In contrast, remaining tethered to a shared reality requires us to really see others, not in the refracted fun-house mirror of social media, but in regular, embodied ways. We triangulate the world via other people, taking readings from a range of perspectives to build a more accurate picture of things. My image of this process is of humans echolocating like bats, where the objects sounding back are the voices and faces of other people. René Descartes might have thought he needed to withdraw from society completely to find truth but, respectfully, he was wrong. Turning in, pulling away disorientates us. The social theorist Sarah Stein Lubrano argues that the more time we spend alone, the more prone we are to delusion. Communities like ours are often laughed at, written off as naive, but if our minds do indeed require a thick web of strong relational connections in order to grasp reality, then we weirdos are possibly learning to see the world more clearly.
