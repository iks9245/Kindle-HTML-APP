小中大切換為襯線

Paternity is poetical

‘Frost at Midnight’ is a poem about caring, in both senses of the word: caring for and caring about. As such, it is a surprising poem about domestic labour, which has invited comment from scholars. ‘What is truly radical about “Frost at Midnight” is Coleridge’s self-representation as a father in the traditional maternal posture of watching over a sleeping baby,’ notes Jonathan Bate. What Bate calls ‘the traditional maternal posture’ is what Hector calls ‘your own handiwork / at loom and spindle’. But perhaps it is not so radical to picture a poet who is equally involved in the work of parenting. Perhaps it is precisely fatherhood that made Coleridge a poet.

What makes ‘Frost at Midnight’ significant in Coleridge’s career is that it represents the crest of the much longer wave of his stylistic development. It is not just a great poem – it is the poem within which he becomes a great poet, and his growth as a poet can be tracked neatly against his experience of becoming a father.
