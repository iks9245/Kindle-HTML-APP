DOELEMAN: It’s a slow burn in a certain way. So, the first thing you see is just a graph, a two-dimensional graph. And you see on the Y-axis, it’s the amount of energy the two telescopes that are widely separated are seeing. On the X-axis, it’s the distance between the telescopes. And what happens is that if you see a ring, you’d see a characteristic bounce in this graph, and that’s what we saw. So, we plotted up the data, and without making an image, and we saw this characteristic bounce, and we realized there’s something really special about these data.

I think there’s a picture of me at a fancy dinner. I’m wearing a tie and one of my post-docs is showing me the data for the first time, and there’s a picture of me going like, “What am I, am I looking at?” And I came back to my office that night, and I went through the calculations based on where these bounces were, and I realized it’s exactly the size it’s supposed to be, right?
