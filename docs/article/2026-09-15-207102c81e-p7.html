The bombarding of childhood

In this sense, pace is not a minor stylistic feature of children’s media. It is one of the ways media imagine the child. Is the child a consumer to be retained, a nervous system to be stimulated, a vessel to be filled with educational content – or a person whose attention is still forming, and who therefore needs time to arrive?

People who worry about children and screens tend to worry about violent content, advertising, or whether a phonics app calling itself ‘educational’ truly supports phonics. These are reasonable worries and I share them. But spend five minutes watching what many young children watch, and you will see how fast, loud and bright everything on the screen has become. It does not matter whether the child is watching on a smartphone in the car or on a television at home. The screen has inherited the adult logic of media design.
