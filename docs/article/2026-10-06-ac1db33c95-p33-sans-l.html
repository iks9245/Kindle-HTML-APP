小中大切換為襯線

A life in episodes

Readers are no gentler with my professional life than with my private one. Now and then, they puncture the version of myself I have just offered. In one post, I recounted an episode from the final year of my PhD studies, when I attended the Swedish annual meeting for historians of science. The morning’s keynote, by the most powerful professor in the field just back from a sabbatical at Berkeley, had struck me as a long and self-satisfied catalogue of the distinguished colleagues he had met and the dinners he had attended. My irritation grew with the square of the elapsed minutes. When the floor opened, I put up my hand and asked, with deliberate weight on the adjective and no softening preamble, whether he had got any intellectual benefit from his trip as well. There was a silence, then scattered tittering. The chairman immediately declared coffee break, and I felt like a king for a few minutes amid the other doctoral students’ admiration (‘How did you dare?’), until the keynote speaker’s own student looked at me with a dry smile and said: ‘There you’ve evidently destroyed your career in Swedish history of science.’ He was right; I took my future ambitions to Denmark.
