Dark matter has long been the favored theory for cosmologists, as MOND “could never really explain everything,” Ghag said. He explained that MOND suffered a significant blow in 2015 when the U.S.-based Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory, or LIGO, detected gravitational waves — ripples in the fabric of the universe — for the first time. Scientists found that these waves move at the speed of light in all directions over huge distances, which implies that gravity works in a consistent, unmodified way throughout the cosmos.
