At one point, he writes that what’s been lost, in the democracies now regressing, is ‘a conflict of opinions that remains recognisable as a dispute about which position is backed by the better reasons.’ That’s as close as he ever came, in anything he wrote for me directly, to stating my own premise in his own words. He names the same loss again later in the foreword, describing people who retreat into whichever group already agrees with them and lose their grip not just on the public sphere’s inclusiveness but on the idea of a shared standard of true and false. What he doesn’t do here, any more than he did in 1999, is follow that loss when it concerns a moral claim once the discourse behind it comes apart. And that is what I tried to explore in my philosophical work since my dissertation.
