DOELEMAN: Yeah. The way this works is that when you’re so close to the black hole, the paths of light, which normally would travel in a straight line, is forced to travel along the curved spacetime. And so it’s really tracing the metric of spacetime. It’s tracing the gravitational well that it finds itself in.

So when light leaves hot gas that’s swirling around the galactic center, let’s say, it starts off, but then instead of going in some straight line, it’ll curve around and come to you. So all the light from behind the black hole is being lensed around to form this ring.

And it’s the size of this ring that is given by Einstein’s equations. And you get these crazy factors like two the square root of 27. G is Newton’s gravitational constant, M is the mass of the black hole, c is the speed of light. But you realize that the diameter of this ring basically just controlled by the mass of the black hole.
