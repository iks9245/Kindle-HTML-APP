King did not arrive at that response alone. For months, he had practised slowing down and questioning his first reaction with other men. READI did not make the danger around him less real, nor did it ask him to abandon the vigilance that had sometimes kept him alive. Rather, it helped him more clearly discern the moments it threatened to reproduce the very violence he feared.

This is where I part with Wallace. He was right that we can choose how to read a situation, but he underestimated what such a choice requires. For the men we interviewed, seeing differently was not just a private act of will performed alone in traffic. It was something they practised repeatedly with others, under conditions of safety, enough time and skilled support.
