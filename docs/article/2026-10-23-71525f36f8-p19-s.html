Dan Siegel, the clinical psychiatrist and founder of interpersonal neurobiology, says that our earliest relationships ‘shape the very neural structures that create representations of experience and allow a coherent view of the world: Interpersonal experiences directly influence how we mentally construct reality.’ The connections formed by the face-dancing we all instinctively do with babies help create the conditions that enable them to experience what is real. More than this, these gazes are where their sense of self comes from. Siegel says that, beyond these earliest experiences of connection, relationships shape, throughout our lives, ‘how we feel, how we think, how we remember things, how we tell the story of who we are, the sense of self … what gives you a sense of purpose.’ We become who we become because of who we gaze on, and who gazes back at us.
