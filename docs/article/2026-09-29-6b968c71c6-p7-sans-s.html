Paternity is poetical

‘Kubla Khan’, ‘Christabel’, ‘The Rime of the Ancient Mariner’ were all begun or completed within 18 months of his son’s birth

Because Coleridge narrated his moods, and wrote letters that turned into poems, he permits us to reconstruct a detailed timeline of his changing feelings towards his son. Later, it will be an unhappy story, and Hartley will live an unhappy life, but at first it did not seem to be going that way. By December 1796, Coleridge was planning to move his family to a thatched cottage in the village of Nether Stowey in Somerset. He wrote to a friend with enthusiasm: ‘I mean to work very hard as Cook, Butler, Scullion, Shoe-cleaner, occasional Nurse.’ He turned out to love nursing. By the start of February, he was writing to another friend: ‘You would smile to see my eye rolling up to the ceiling in a Lyric fury, and on my knee a Diaper pinned, to warm.’ This moment – of writing while nursing, of diapers and poetic inspiration – is the genesis of ‘Frost at Midnight’.
