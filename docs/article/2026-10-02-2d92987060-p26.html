Reason is more than a tool

Kant is again decisive here, for he saw that the human capacity to set and assess ends is itself fragile. Whether our ends are ultimately up to us depends partly on whether we understand them as open to rational assessment. This is the paradox at the heart of human freedom. Belief in our agency is often a condition of making that agency real. If we think of desires and preferences as brute facts that determine us from without – as psychological weather, market signals, biological programming, algorithmic prediction – then they are more likely to rule us in just that way. By contrast, if we see them as candidates for endorsement or refusal, as an expression of our agency, then that very agency expands.
