小中大切換為無襯線

Deskilling the body

And yet we turn to them because they have been designed either to ease the friction of the hard work our bodies must put in to develop skills or because they promise to make us more productive. More insidiously, they do so by modelling the appearance or behaviours of living beings. A recent analysis by the psychologist Clara Colombatto, the philosopher Jonathan Birch and the neuroscientist Stephen Fleming found that exposure to these systems leads to an increase in attributions of mental capacities to them. Another study, by the behaviour scientist Yaoxi Shi and others, found that regular five-minute conversations with an AI on personal issues resulted in a small but meaningful decrease in preference for seeking human support.

The danger is that the systems now coordinating us don’t answer to biological needs. They don’t get tired or hungry or lonely. Yet they increasingly regulate the behaviour of beings who do. In short, we are losing our own agency to parasites: systems that simulate agency while feeding on ours. They can be designed to direct us without ever needing to understand what a human body is and what an individual needs for their betterment.
