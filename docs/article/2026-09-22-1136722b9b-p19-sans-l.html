This drive for coexistence may undergird fundamental aspects of how genomes regulate themselves. The disruptive nature of transposons may have required organisms to evolve new methods of shutting off their activity. Epigenetic control — the means by which an organism can dial the expression of its genetic repertoire up or down, or shut off the expression of some genes entirely — may have evolved first to bring transposons to heel.
