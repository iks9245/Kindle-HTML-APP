He was probably right

That grading is where the Academic parts company with the other great sceptical school of antiquity, Pyrrhonism, which counselled suspending judgment on most issues and otherwise acting according to how things appear to be while following local customs. There are three differences between the two approaches: first, the Academic assents, in a weakened but real sense: they approve the claim, argue for it, and carry the responsibility if it proves wrong, whereas the Pyrrhonist withholds approval and merely goes along. Second, the Academic ranks: appearances are sorted by how much testing they have survived, and some genuinely turn out to be better than others, while to the Pyrrhonist appearances arrive with no comparative weight attached – one is just as good (or bad) as another. Third, the two schools want different things: the Pyrrhonist pursues ataraxia, meaning tranquility, and suspension of judgment is his means to it. The Academic is still after truth; they simply doubt they will ever be able to certify that they have reached it. One is a therapy for anxiety, the other is a research programme conducted by a fallible animal that nevertheless needs to act in the world.
