The PSR is so alluring because it would seem that the very activity of enquiry – whether philosophical, scientific, or the first ‘Why?’ question of a young child – is premised on there being an explanation, a reason, for everything. To endorse the PSR is to adopt an attitude of optimism that the world and our place in it are intelligible and infused with reason and meaning. If anything were unexplainable, there’d be a grave risk that human enquiry is ultimately futile. The falsity of the PSR would constitute a fundamental mismatch between our inquisitive nature and the world in which we find ourselves; it would imply that the world is unintelligible.
