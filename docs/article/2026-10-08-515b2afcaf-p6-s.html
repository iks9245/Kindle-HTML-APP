Israelis mourn 7 October attack victims three years after deadly Hamas raid

The prime minister has rejected any personal responsibility and has refused to establish a state inquiry led by Israel's Supreme Court, arguing recently that the country's judiciary would be biased against him.

Einav Zangauker became an outspoken critic of the Netanyahu government while her son, Matan, was in captivity in Gaza. He was released last October as part of the ceasefire agreement.

"They're trying to make us forget what happened on 7 October," she told Army Radio. "We won't forget, we will demand the truth."

Memorial events were organised in other countries around the world, including in Germany, where the Jewish students' union held a vigil at Berlin's Brandenburg Gate.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said the Hamas-led attack marked a "profound rupture".

"We commemorate the victims, we share their pain," he said, adding that antisemitism must be confronted with "even greater resolve".
