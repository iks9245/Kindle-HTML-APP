DOELEMAN: Well, so we’re lucky in the sense that we have one, Sagittarius A*, that we felt for sure we could image. And then there was another one, M87, which is in the constellation Virgo. That black hole is about six billion times what our sun weighs, but it’s fifty-five million light years away. So it’s about two thousand times farther away, but it’s also about two thousand times more massive. And the event horizon scales with mass, so its angular size is about the same as the supermassive black hole in the center of the Milky Way galaxy.
