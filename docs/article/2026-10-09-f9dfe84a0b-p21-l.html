Several towns had already banned Jews from lending money, or expelled them outright

The first reliably attested monte di pietà was established in Perugia in April 1462. Over the subsequent decades, dozens more followed across Italy and, in most cases, they worked pretty much exactly as the friars had hoped. Once a ‘bank of compassion’ was operating, Italian towns generally didn’t waste much time before ordering the local Jews to leave.
