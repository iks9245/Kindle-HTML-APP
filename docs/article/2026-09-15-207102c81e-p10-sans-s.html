And parents are rightly worried: a four-year-old does not come to media with adult reserves of self-regulation. She is still learning how to stay with a feeling, how to wait for a story to unfold, how to connect one event to another, how to notice that a character’s sadness matters before the joke wipes it away. If a programme moves too quickly, it may encourage a child to expect information to arrive in rapid, constantly shifting bursts, making slower, more sustained forms of attention harder to tolerate. Stopping this vicious cycle starts by asking what exactly makes children’s media fast or slow.

A child can look intensely engaged while being given very little time to think

Don’t get me wrong, I am not saying that slow is always good or better than fast media. Some lively children’s media can be excellent; some slow media can be dull, inert or badly made. The point is that good children’s media give a child time to complete an act of attention. Bad media keep interrupting that act before it can become a memory or play.
