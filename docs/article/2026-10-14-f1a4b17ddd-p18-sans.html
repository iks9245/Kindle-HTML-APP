So, what happens if you rely on an app to find your way? GPS can make navigation easier and less energetically costly. Yet studies of large-scale navigation have also demonstrated that physically moving through an environment produces better spatial knowledge than viewing the same journey on a 2D source. When we routinely follow GPS directions, an external source supplies information we would otherwise work out through our own sensing and movement. One longitudinal study that followed 13 GPS users over three years found that those users had a steeper subsequent decline in their spatial memory as mediated by the hippocampus, compared with control groups who didn’t use GPS. This suggests that, when we practise wayfinding less often, our ability to notice where we are and readjust may dwindle.
