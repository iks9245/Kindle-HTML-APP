How Spiders Spin ‘Silk Dope’ Into Nature’s Toughest Material

Spiders are incredible natural factories. They can spin up to seven types of silks, and the same spider can often spin several of them. Aciniform silk wraps up insects for later snacking. Rubbery flagelliform silk reinforces the capture spiral to absorb shock. Dragline silk anchors webs and spiders to walls. Each type of silk has different properties depending on the species and related to how its molecules are assembled. And silks of the same type can have slightly different properties depending on the species.

In 1907, scientists discovered that spider silk is made of long, modular proteins, later named spidroins, which are in the same group of fiber proteins as collagen and keratin. More than half a century later, X-rays started to reveal the fiber’s underlying structure. The silk is made of flat sheets of protein, known as beta sheets, that are embedded in a less ordered, non-crystalline matrix.
