小中大切換為襯線

A life in episodes

Archival documents and memories are the obvious source materials for anyone writing their autobiography – and gathering them had so far been exactly the solitary labour the genre is known for: the lone author behind drawn curtains, exhuming papers and interrogating memory. But few authors go further and seek out the people they have encountered throughout their lives. As an experienced oral historian – both for the history of ecology and, later, for the Jerne biography – I had already spent hundreds of hours interviewing others about their lives. So it was a natural step to turn the same method on myself, and supplement the archival records and my own memories by contacting people from my past – childhood and adolescent friends, schoolmates, teachers, former colleagues, and even former girlfriends – in order to flesh out and contrast my self-image with their perspectives. Some declined; others had little more than banalities to offer (‘you were tall and thin’), but many were surprisingly candid. Over the years, I have spoken with several hundred acquaintances and colleagues, and have renewed a couple of lost connections – though not romantic ones. These conversations were the first breach in the traditional solitude of the genre.
