A New Map of ‘Empty’ Space Challenges a Tranquil Picture of the Galaxy

As gas and dust float through space, they pull more gas and dust together through gravity, eventually forming a cloud. These clouds tend to cool down, because fast-moving molecules will collide with other molecules and lose energy. Their temperature stays at a bitter 100 kelvins, or about minus 173 degrees Celsius.

But sometimes the gas and dust are too hot to collapse into a cold cloud; instead, they form a diffuse one. A diffuse cloud is much more exposed to nearby stars, which deliver new energy in the form of ultraviolet radiation. This process keeps the cloud at a steady temperature near 8,000 kelvins.

Scientists thought that atoms in the interstellar medium would transition to either the cold or warm state almost instantly, leaving basically no unstable, intermediate ISM behind.
