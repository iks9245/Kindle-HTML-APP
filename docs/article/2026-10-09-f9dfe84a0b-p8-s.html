The ghetto on the lagoon

Towards the middle of the 14th century, however, a new wave of Jewish migrants began arriving from Germany. These ebrei Tedeschi (German Jews) were not visitors, pausing in the middle of a journey, but refugees. Having been forced to flee their native land by the waves of persecution unleashed by the Black Death, they came to Venice in search of safety – and in the hope of sharing in its commercial might. They first settled in Mestre, just across the lagoon from the city. And they quickly flourished. Some became labourers, others merchants, tailors or jewel-cutters. There were even a few doctors. But it was as money-lenders – or, more properly, pawnbrokers – that they found their niche.
