Reason is more than a tool

David Hume, the cool-eyed Scottish anatomist of human nature, gave the most famous answer on one side: ‘Reason is, and ought only to be the slave of the passions,’ he wrote in A Treatise of Human Nature (1739). It can ‘never pretend to any other office than to serve and obey them.’ Reason does not command. It defers. It does not tell us what to want or what might be worth wanting. Instead, it tells us how to get what we already want. It calculates relations, weighs evidence, discovers causes and effects, draws inferences, corrects mistakes about means. But it cannot judge or generate ends. If I desire revenge, reason can help me sharpen the knife. If I desire peace, it may recommend compromise. Passion sets the destination. Reason merely shows the way there.
