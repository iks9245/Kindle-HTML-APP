Reason is more than a tool

This is one of the reasons why Pope Leo XIV’s encyclical Magnifica Humanitas (2025) is interesting even for readers far outside Catholic social teaching. Its idiom is theological, but its central anxiety overlaps with Kantian and Frankfurt School worries about the technocratic paradigm. When efficiency, control and profit become ‘the standard by which everything is judged’, they begin ‘to dictate what matters and what can be discarded.’ The encyclical warns that making efficiency the ultimate measure of value tempts human beings to see themselves, and to treat others, as mere means rather than as persons in the Kantian sense – that is, as bearers of dignity. Hence the pope’s striking counterclaim: ‘The quality of a civilisation is measured not by the power of its means, but by the care it is able to offer.’
