AI Proved the Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis. What Happens Now?

OpenAI announced two different versions of their quasi-Riemann hypothesis proof. The first version, dated September 30, 2026, claimed to show that all nontrivial zeros must be between 1/8 and 7/8, and was formally verified using the Lean programming language. The second version, which was completed almost a week later (and is not yet formally verified), claimed to prove a weaker result: that the zeros must have a real part between 1/12 and 11/12. But while the first paper is 199 pages long, the second weighs in at a comparatively modest 49 pages.
