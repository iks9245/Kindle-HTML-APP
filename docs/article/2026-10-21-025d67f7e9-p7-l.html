In the trolley problem, the philosophers Philippa Foot and Judith Jarvis Thomson imagined a runaway trolley hurtling toward five people tied to the track. You are standing on a bridge above, next to another bystander. The only way to stop the trolley and save the five is to push the bystander off the bridge. If you do, his body will halt the trolley, but he will die as a result. Many people feel, immediately and forcefully, that pushing him is wrong, even if it saves five lives. But why?
