Several hundred people are understood to have decided to stay in the war-torn city, despite widespread devastation and the lack of gas, electricity and running water.

Some of the older generation did not want to leave, one relative told the BBC.

Lubinets said on Friday morning that those who decided to stay "will receive all the necessary humanitarian assistance".

He and his Russian counterpart Yana Lantratova travelled to Oleshky together on Friday, setting off from Armiansk, in the Russian-occupied Crimean peninsula.
