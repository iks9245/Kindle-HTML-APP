Research looking at why students choose or reject physics has found that it’s seen as abstract, difficult, and disconnected from real life – a perception that’s remained steady, even as enrolment has recovered. Meanwhile, newer fields – computer science, behavioural economics, even data journalism – have felt more immediate, more applicable. They speak the language of now.

What’s strange is that this perception problem has happened at the same historical moment in which physics has begun popping up in other surprising places.
