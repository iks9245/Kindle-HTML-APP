In 1977, Chris McKee and Jeremiah Ostriker added a third phase to the picture: At supernova shock fronts, gas could get even hotter, transforming into a scorching plasma at a temperature of half a million kelvins. Yet even in this expanded framework, the atomic gas outside blast zones was assumed to achieve a calm, pressure-balanced existence.

But it was hard to test the theory. Cold atomic hydrogen emits no visible light. The easiest way to measure it is to look for a rare atomic shift: The electron and proton inside a hydrogen atom will sometimes switch between two possible states. This transition emits a faint radio wave at a wavelength of 21 centimeters.
