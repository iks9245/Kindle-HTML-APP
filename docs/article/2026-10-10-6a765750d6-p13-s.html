When the astronauts spin through more than a dozen sunrises and sunsets in a single day and night; when the whole globe lies below them with California one minute and Japan the next; when, as they return from space, they feel spontaneously, with the first Soviet spaceman: ‘How beautiful it is, our Earth’; it is inconceivable that no modification of consciousness or imagination occurs, no sense that quarrels are meaningless before the majestic yet vulnerable reality of a single planet carrying a single human species through infinite space.

Ward borrowed the metaphor ‘Spaceship Earth’ from the US inventor and scientist Buckminster Fuller, who used it in 1963 to describe the planet’s fragility. He argued that Earth was a capsule with limited resources on a ‘totally precarious’ vast space voyage, whose success depends on the rational actions of every single individual.
