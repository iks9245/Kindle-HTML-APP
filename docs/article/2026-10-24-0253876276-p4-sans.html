Now, Ho’s lab has imaged the moving junction right at the moment of invasion, in unprecedented detail. Her team found that when the Plasmodium parasite installs molecular gaskets in the host cell, it radically remodels the cell membrane to slip through without a trace. This knowledge could help inform the next generation of malaria treatments and influence the drug-discovery process for other parasitic diseases, such as cyclosporiasis and toxoplasmosis.

“It’s a totally mind-blowing paper,” said Jake Baum, a parasitologist at the University of New South Wales, who was not involved in the research. “At the end of the day, this is not molecular biology. This is biophysics.”
