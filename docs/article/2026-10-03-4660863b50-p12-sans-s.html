Third, our interviews revealed that participants often read violent behaviour as proof of who someone was, rather than how that person had responded to a situation. Psychologists call this the fundamental attribution error, or our tendency to explain behaviour through features of a person’s character rather than circumstance. But the men turned this error most mercilessly on themselves.

Accepting violence as part of who they were made their future involvement in violence more likely
