In the centuries after the development of microscopy, modern pathology evolved to allow researchers and physicians to see the hidden face of the adversary: the minute cells devolved from their proper roles in the broader organism and hellbent on reproduction and migration. By the late 19th century, improved microscopes allowed early pathologists to categorise cancers – carcinomas – based not just on their organs of origin but also on the appearance of the cells themselves, laying the foundation for modern grading and staging.

The unintended consequence of screening has been over-diagnosis – the detection of lesions that would never threaten survival
