Is everything conscious?

Distinct things are individuated by their properties. But this relation of individuation is what philosophers call a dependence relation. For example, the fact that this is an essay on panpsychism, intelligibility and the aim of philosophy depends on the fact that there are words arranged in the appropriate way. This relation of dependence induces an ordering of fundamentality: the words are more fundamental than the essay because the latter depends on the former.

But, for Spinoza, if things depend on properties because properties individuate things, then it follows that properties are more fundamental than things. This result, however, is not acceptable. Things – or ‘substances’, as Spinoza would say – are supposed to be absolutely fundamental. What it is to be a substance, a distinct thing that exists, is to be independent of everything else, completely self-sustaining. It seems odd to say that properties occupy the fundamental level. Is the redness of an apple more fundamental than the apple itself? Surely not, since the apple, the thing, is required to exist before redness can be instantiated. So, we must find another way to explain why there are multiple distinct things. Doing so in terms of properties won’t cut it.
