Triplet of ‘Ghostly’ Galaxies Hints at What Dark Matter Is — and Isn’t

It’s reminiscent of the Bullet Cluster, two massive collections of galaxies 3.8 billion light-years away that also collided eons ago. This destruction derby, which was recently observed by the James Webb Space Telescope, has a messy distribution of mass that only makes sense if dark matter and ordinary matter got torn asunder during the collision.

Although this kind of segregation can be seen with greater clarity in DF2, DF4, and DF9, not everyone agrees on how the separation took place.

Kinwah Wu, a theoretical astrophysicist at University College London, noted that the triplet does not exhibit any of the fluid, wispy features that one would expect to see after a galactic collision. He suggests that, in this case, DF2, DF4, and DF9 were passing through a large group of massive galaxies. This outsize rabble then stripped the triplet of all their dark matter during their ill-fated voyage.
