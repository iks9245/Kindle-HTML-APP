There is another reason. The community we live in offers mutual witnessing, mutual recognition, increased capacity and care, and economies of scale, but this alone is not enough to hold us when it all feels too much. When people come to our home wanting advice about setting up something similar, we always tell them: community for the sake of community doesn’t work. Communes might be highly effective sites of self-formation, but the ones designed primarily to do that will likely collapse. My experience is that our community (and other lasting ones) hold together by turning attention beyond ourselves. The ability to locate the project in a vision of the good – political, ecological, philosophical or other – is an essential foundation.
