Chasing la dolce vita

In 1940, when Mussolini declared war on Britain and France, all the ‘enemy aliens’ living in Britain had to be imprisoned, or as Winston Churchill put it: ‘Collar the lot!’ Marco, along with thousands of other Italians, and Germans, was rounded up and interned on the Isle of Man. As Rachel Pistol writes in ‘Enemy Aliens’ (2024), many Italian shops and businesses across the country were broken into and smashed by mobs. Marco was lucky not to be placed on the SS Arandora Star, a prisoner-of-war ship heading to Canada. More than 800 of these internees, including 442 Italians, died when the boat was torpedoed off the coast of Ireland. Just under a quarter were from different corners of Emilia-Romagna – some of them friends of Marco. The Italian Hospital scraped through the war and Marco returned to assist them. Researching this essay, I discovered that the building was broken into in 1947 and my great-grandmother, who was with her husband that day, died while confronting the intruders.
