STROGATZ: I certainly grew up in reading pop accounts of quantum physics, hearing a lot about the observer, certainly a lot about the collapse of the wave function. I’m not so sure it’s totally been put to bed, though. What you’re talking about is what used to be called the Copenhagen interpretation, right? From Niels Bohr and his school.

LEVIN: Right.

STROGATZ: The original attempt to not exactly make sense, but give some kind of philosophical account of what’s going on with this measurement problem. It’s out there still.

LEVIN: It’s still an issue. I think the Copenhagen interpretation and the many worlds interpretation are still out there. I think decoherent histories is maybe an alternative to both. So in the Copenhagen interpretation, something causes the superposition to collapse and a definite state to be assumed by the superposed particle, let’s say its location.
