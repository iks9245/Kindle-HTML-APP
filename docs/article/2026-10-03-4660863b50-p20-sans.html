We tend to imagine that we take in the world and then respond to what we find. In fact, the mind guides what we see, filling gaps with expectations built from experience. The journalist Walter Lippmann in 1922 called these expectations ‘the pictures in our heads’ and explained that it is the pictures we respond to, not some objective environmental stimuli. Psychologists and sociologists call these shortcuts frames and scripts. Frames answer, in a fraction of a second, the question under every social encounter: What is going on here? Scripts suggest what to do next. Together they help us move through our days without deliberating over each interaction. Frames and scripts are tools that make daily life possible.
