小中大切換為無襯線

The bombarding of childhood

Children’s media are usually judged by their content and quantity: how many minutes watched and whether it was educational, age-appropriate, diverse, prosocial, commercial or safe. These questions matter and, with my colleagues, I have studied them through experiments and contributed to guidelines, regulations, or toolkits for designers. But we have been missing a bigger design feature that may be just as consequential: the time a medium gives a child to make meaning. A programme or app can contain good lessons and still move too quickly for those lessons to settle. For instance, in the US animated educational series Ask the StoryBots (2016-20), the subject matter may be genuinely rich – why the sky is blue, how eyes work, or where planets come from – but the explanation unfolds through rapid jokes, songs, visual shifts and sudden changes in style. Before a child has time to sit with one image or idea, the scene has moved on to the next, with a burst of information.
