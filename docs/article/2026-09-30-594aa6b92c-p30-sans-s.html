Don’t use the ‘C-word’

When I perform biopsies, I try to forewarn patients of the language they may see on the pathology report before we talk again, but there are limits to how much I can explain in advance. This problem is all the worse because we cancer specialists inhabit a borderland our vocabulary has not yet evolved to govern. The binary language of ‘normal’ vs ‘cancer’ suited the long epoch in which disease announced itself clinically but is badly strained in an era in which screening tests and incidental scans identify biological possibility long before clinical destiny. Pathologists describe what they see through the microscope, but a diagnosis is more than a description. When a word rises off a pathology report, whether into my exam room or the patient’s living room, it becomes prognosis, identity, fear, crisis.
