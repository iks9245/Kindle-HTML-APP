Even creating these atoms inverts the intuitions of particle physics. CERN’s flagship instrument, the Large Hadron Collider, revs up particles almost to light speed to initiate those high-energy interactions. Paul’s team instead relies on a lesser-known machine called the Antiproton Decelerator that slows particles to a crawl, so that they become able to infiltrate atoms instead of blasting them apart. In some ways, it’s a harder job. “Acceleration is easier than deceleration,” said Lars Jørgensen, an experimentalist at CERN who helped to develop the decelerator.
