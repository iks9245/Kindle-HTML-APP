STROGATZ: Well, when you spoke about some of your lab experiments, I got the feeling that you might have been criticized by colleagues at some point for providing idealized conditions in the lab that allowed you to see certain patterns.

KIERS: Of course, of course.

STROGATZ: Ah, you’re already getting the thrust of my question.

KIERS: Oh, yes. No, but I take it as an invitation, because these systems, I’m the first to admit they’re incredibly simplified. The other day, we had a plate that was contaminated in the sense that there was bacteria growing all over it, and we looked across the network and there were these like… they looked like little happy jelly beans, just like moving along. They call that the fungal highway. Bacteria use the meniscus of the network to move incredibly fast, and there’s just beautiful work coming out of Japan on how bacteria use the network.
