小中大切換為襯線

The bombarding of childhood

When learning is crowded with content that keeps jumping ahead – a crisis appears, a mission begins, a spaceship launches, a door opens – the child’s attention is restarted as each problem is introduced and immediately solved. Such media may gesture at teamwork or perseverance, but the speed of the format leaves little time for those lessons to sink in. In the animated TV series Paw Patrol (2013-), for instance, a rescue is clear and reassuring: someone is in trouble, pups assemble, each uses a skill, and order is restored. Yet the movement from problem to rescue to resolution is often so efficient that the child is swept along by urgency rather than invited to dwell on responsibility or care for the other. Similarly, in the app-based game Hot Wheels Unlimited, building tracks and racing cars may suggest creativity and problem-solving, but the dominant rhythm is acceleration: boost, race, crash, win, unlock, repeat.
