Where a land ethic blooms

If we are to transform agriculture for better outcomes, we need a similar roll-out of networked and diverse people and organisations who collectively act as a keystone species. But we cannot simply wait for the bison of federal policy reform to come trampling through the industrial agriculture complex. We must act lest the bison of more sustainable agriculture stumble, allowing the most ruthless forces in industrial agriculture to continue unchecked – to the degradation of soil, extinction of wild plants and animals, and hollowing-out of communities. To restore agriculture to a more diverse and resilient community, each of us must express our individual land ethic through the food markets and by giving voice to our values in the public sphere. There are deep congruencies between sustainable agriculture and conservation, and we cannot afford to lose the incremental but crucial gains that pioneers have made over the past few decades.
