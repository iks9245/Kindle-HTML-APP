小中大切換為無襯線

Love with nowhere to go

The narrowing of grief to bereavement is more than a conceptual mistake. It does real damage. What the writer and grief counsellor Stephen Levine called ‘unattended sorrow’ doesn’t dissolve but goes underground, hiding in the shadows, later resurfacing as unexpected symptoms. I have been there. A mechanical tightness in the chest and a hard belly, like wearing a harness to brace against life. Harrowing dreams. Attempts to numb what is too painful to feel alone. And, worst of all, cynicism and a slow erosion of trust. Not just in other people, but in your own feelings, your own intuitions about the depth, sustainability and future of any new connection. When no one around you recognises your grief, you begin to wonder whether you were wrong to feel it at all.
