Barbara Ward’s vision

Ward advocated for institutional innovation on a planetary scale and considered the UN as a proto-world government. Yet she was acutely aware of the limits of international institutions and, by the late 1970s, lamented the UN’s inability to translate environmental commitments into effective action. The challenge was not just to create adequate institutions, but also to give them the power to act. In this sense, Ward suspected that the problem was not the institution’s design but the ‘loyalties’ of its members, which were based on ideology and morality: ‘The planet is not yet a centre of rational loyalty for all of mankind.’ Instead of institutional change, she sought to transform the world’s civil society by raising public consciousness of questions of political and economic justice. A new planetary order should be based not on top-down institutions but on a bottom-up planetary community that ‘our environmental interdependence’ could generate.
