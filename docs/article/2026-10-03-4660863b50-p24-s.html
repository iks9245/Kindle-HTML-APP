Second, they learned to see more than one possible response to feeling threatened. Kobe described his old reflex as follows: ‘If you said something, I was going to touch you or tap you or get on your ass.’ Now, Kobe said: ‘I think before I act.’ Recalling a role-play in which he was bumped outside a store, he laid out what he could now see: ‘I could whoop one of they ass and one of them could shoot me… or I could just walk away. Or I could ask him, did he mean to do it?’ Three options emerged where there had previously been one.

To help them remember they had options in the moment, the men developed mantras simple enough to recall under pressure. Keith, 24, learned to: ‘Take 10 deep breaths, pace back and forth, and you might see things differently.’ Tune, 30, used: ‘It only takes three seconds to ruin your life.’ Larry, in a fight with his son’s mother, told himself: ‘It’s just words.’
