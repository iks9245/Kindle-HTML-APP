BigHat Biosciences: lead optimization. BigHat Biosciences pairs AI with an automated lab to improve potential drugs (or “leads”), which must bind their target tightly, remain stable through manufacturing and storage, and avoid clumping together (among other criteria). BigHat’s scientists and an Anthropic researcher worked with Claude to improve these three properties in two leads, both antibody fragments that could become parts of future drugs: one designed to target cancer cells, and one designed to keep a drug in the body longer. Claude, connected to BigHat’s lab data system, Reccy, proposed changes to each molecule, and BigHat’s lab made and tested about 1,010 variants in about 10 weeks. Partway through, the team suspected the standard heat-stability and clumping metrics were misleading. Claude and the researcher reanalyzed the raw data, then proposed different metrics and a protocol change to test them. BigHat’s scientists refined and ran it, and the main predictions held. Compared with the originals, the best versions bound more than 10x more tightly, the cancer-targeting lead stayed in solution 18°C higher, and the lead for longer-lasting drugs clumped more than 100x more slowly.
