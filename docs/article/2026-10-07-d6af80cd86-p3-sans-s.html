Her friend, Sacha, said the ceilings in Marseille's schools were leaking and conditions were generally rather poor, "so we can't really work a lot of time". "We will keep doing this until good measures are taken," he added.

In parliament, the prime minister accused the radical-left France Unbowed (LFI) party of seeking to "sow chaos" ahead of presidential elections in seven months' time.

President Emmanuel Macron is nearing the end of his second and final term in office and LFI leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon is currently runner-up in the opinion polls behind Marine Le Pen of the hard-right National Rally.

Macron was due to chair a meeting with ministers at the Élysée Palace on Tuesday evening to assess the situation.

Education Minister Édouard Geffray said that from the start he had appealed for dialogue rather than school blockades and complained that "ultra-violent elements infiltrated the high-school student movements".
