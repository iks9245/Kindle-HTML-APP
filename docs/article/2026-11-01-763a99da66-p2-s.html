小中大切換為無襯線

Ukrainian evacuees reunite with family after years trapped in Russia-occupied city

Their cries and their tears captured the sheer relief of three years of desperate worry, fearing they would never see him again.

Their mother Zhanna clasped Mykhailo's face and stroked his forehead again and again, as if checking he was the same man she had left behind. He promised her, finally, they will now get married.

Tetiana and Volodymyr, an older couple carrying their cat, told the BBC they no longer have a home, after it was burned down in the first year of the war. They have been living in the abandoned house of a neighbour.

They said they instantly took the decision to leave but feared their journey could be disrupted. Now they have to work out where to go next and how to rebuild their lives.

Ukrainian military positions lie just 5km (3.1 miles) from Oleshky, but evacuees first had to travel through Russian-occupied territory to southern Russia, where they flew to Belarus, before finally being driven across Ukraine's northern border on Saturday.
