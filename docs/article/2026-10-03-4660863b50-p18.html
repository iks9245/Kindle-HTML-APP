Life on a hair trigger

While the stakes of these cognitive patterns are exceptional, the mental machinery underpinning them is not. Say you’re driving and another car cuts you off. Before you’ve even thought about it, your mind has supplied a reason: This driver is reckless and has no regard for anyone else on the road. The interpretation arrives in a split second, built more out of your irritation than anything you know about the driver. Maybe she’s racing to a hospital for an emergency. The writer David Foster Wallace described such a scene in his commencement speech, ‘This Is Water’ (2005). His point was hopeful: we are free to choose how we read such a moment. The automatic, egocentric perspective is never the only one available.
