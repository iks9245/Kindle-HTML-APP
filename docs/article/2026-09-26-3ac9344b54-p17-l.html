Several people had been pushing him toward this change. The US philosopher Richard Rorty pushed from the side from which Habermas was now backing away: for Rorty, there was no need for truth to answer to anything beyond what an audience could be talked into agreeing on, and he’d been making that case to Habermas for years. Lafont, my advisor, who’d arranged the hotel meeting, pushed from the other side, drawing on the US philosopher Hilary Putnam, who had spent much of his career arguing against exactly the kind of view that made truth depend entirely on human agreement. Lafont had argued since 1994 that Habermas’s own philosophical tradition, in its turn toward language, had drifted into a position where it couldn’t say the world holds firm no matter how we talk about it. When Habermas changed his mind, he moved toward Lafont’s position, not Rorty’s.
