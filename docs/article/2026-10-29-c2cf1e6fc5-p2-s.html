Bloodied, dazed and angry - how passengers' rail journey turned to disaster in Spain

Some of the passengers were asleep, some had already started their working day on laptops - and others scrolled through their apps on their phones.

The journey was interrupted with an announcement that there was congestion on the tracks. This lasted a few minutes - for some, a bit longer for others.

Tangui Binier, 22, was in the Iryo train, the front carriage, number eight. His father was in number seven.

Their train was one of six to have been ordered to stop at 08:30. A few minutes later, "the train started speeding", Binier told the BBC, "then it came to a sudden halt".

Baffled, he looked out of the window and saw "wires on the left side come down, and then there being just a lot of smoke behind us."

Spanish journalist Roberto Gómez was dozing on the high-speed Renfe train which had stopped more than 25km (16 miles) away from Binier's.
