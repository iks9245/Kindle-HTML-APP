- They foreclose on their own members, over debts that start small Florida law gives every association a lien on each home for unpaid assessments and the right to foreclose it — section 720.3085, Florida Statutes for homeowners' associations, section 718.116, Florida Statutes for condominiums. The debt that starts it can be a few hundred dollars; by the time a suit is filed it carries interest, late charges, collection costs and the association's attorney's fees, all secured by the lien. 10,296 foreclosure suits on file were brought by a Florida association against one of its own members, from 2,695 communities — 2.6% of those indexed. The lender's foreclosures that merely name an association number 1,629: the association is the foreclosing party more often than the bank is. The peak was 2009, with 875 suits (about 2 a day); 2025, the last full year, had 599, and 667 have been filed so far in 2026. Each suit is a household that may be forced to sell, a home that may go to auction for the debt and the fees, and a sale that takes place under a deadline instead of on the market.
