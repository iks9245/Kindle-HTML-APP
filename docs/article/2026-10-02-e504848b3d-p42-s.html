It’s important that they live inside of three-dimensional space because when I study the surface, I’m allowed to continuously deform it, but it has to stay inside of three-dimensional space. So I could have two different tori that are both tori, but I can’t actually turn one into the other inside of three-dimensional space.

A simpler thing is just even knots, knotted loops. If I take a loop that’s really a circle, just a nice round circle, we call that the unknot. If I take a loop and actually tie it into some complicated thing, it won’t be the unknot. And they’re still both circles, but they’re not the same knot. I can’t actually turn one into the other without passing it through itself. Seifert surfaces are the same thing.

But for Seifert surfaces, I do want them to have boundary. They should have an edge, and the edge will be a knot, a knotted loop.
