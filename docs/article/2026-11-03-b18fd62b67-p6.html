I’m all for open-mindedness, but Price’s argument troubled me. There must be a thousand other anomalous phenomena that are as likely to be real as cold fusion and, if they are, to transform the world. Why focus on this one? The only reason we are talking about cold fusion at all is that we were lied to in 1989. The initial mistruth created a kind of psychological anchoring effect, causing us to entertain a possibility that we otherwise wouldn’t have. It is as if I said I saw Bigfoot in my backyard, then confessed I hadn’t, but people started holding annual cryptozoological conferences nearby anyway. I found it jarring that a deception could conjure an entire academic research program. How is this any different from the antivax movement, which also started with data that turned out to be faked and should have just packed up once that became known?
