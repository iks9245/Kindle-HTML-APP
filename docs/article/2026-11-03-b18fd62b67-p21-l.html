The atom eventually stabilizes again, but not for long. In an antiprotonic atom, the antiproton reaches such a tight orbit that it touches the nucleus and annihilates. In other types of exotic atoms, the electron substitute quickly decays. Muons last a mere 2 microseconds, and other species are even more fleeting. But during these truncated lifespans, exotic atoms can even form short-lived molecules.
