Where a land ethic blooms

In the language of microeconomics, the tall grasses have the lowest costs for construction and operations. As a result, these ships in the prairie sea can cast vast nets of roots below the surface to capture water and nitrogen, and set huge sails of green leaves to catch the sun to propel photosynthesis. These photons and molecules are finite resources that plants cannot share and for which they must compete. The highly efficient tallgrass fleets should rule the prairies. Paradoxically, the tallgrass prairies are blessed with an abundance of smaller, less efficient wildflowers and legumes, prospering from sub-boreal Manitoba in Canada to the subtropical coastal plains of Louisiana and Texas. As with the mystery of decay and renewal that abounds in the soil, microbes dwell at the heart of this paradox.

In the cycles of evolution, the efficiency and dominance of the tall grasses built a palace of greenery
