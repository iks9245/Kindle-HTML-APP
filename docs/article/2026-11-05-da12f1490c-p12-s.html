While anomaly detection isn’t as dramatic as the models’ evocative descriptions of injected concepts, it’s still striking in its own right. When training a model, researchers never tamper with its neural activity — so there’s no reason to expect it to develop the ability to detect this kind of tampering. Yet that’s what seems to have happened.

“The fact that it can notice the anomaly is insane,” Lederman said. “That is a mind-bending fact. It is wild, and it does seem robust to our various controls.”

Around the same time, Lindsey and his colleagues at Anthropic conducted a follow-up study focused on mapping out how this anomaly detection actually works, and they reached a similar conclusion. Other researchers have continued to explore alternative “deflationary” explanations using smaller models, but it remains to be seen whether they can account for all the results of Lindsey’s experiments.
