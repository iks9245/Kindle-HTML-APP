How intuitions work

We all have beliefs about the world, and we care whether they are true. We want to get things right. But we can’t step outside our own minds to check a belief against the world; the best we can do is to hold it for good reasons – for considerations that, from where we stand, speak in favour of its truth. That is what justification is.

Now suppose that beliefs could only ever be justified by other beliefs. Then, as the ancient Sceptics observed, no belief could ever be justified at all. Each belief would derive its support from further beliefs, which would themselves require further support, and so on without end. To foil this regress, we have three options: embrace an infinite chain (infinitism), allow the chain to loop back on itself (coherentism), or hold that some beliefs are justified independently of others (foundationalism). It is this final option, the view that there is a starting point or foundation for justification, that I will develop. But to do so, we must spend a little time explaining how a belief can be justified by something other than another belief.
