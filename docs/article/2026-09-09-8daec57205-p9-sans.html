Our eloquent hands

The hand has a history of natural magic and occult knowledges, but it is also just anatomy, and so it appears in a plainer anatomical canon too. Yet that tradition is enchanted in its own way. One of the 19th century’s most eminent anatomists, Charles Bell, wrote The Hand (1833), a medical text on God’s design. And not long after, humanity’s deep past was being re-told through the human hand. In The Descent of Man (1871), Charles Darwin recounted that, one day, standing upright, the hand of the primate called Homo sapiens was free to grasp and so to evolve differently, uniquely. It is no coincidence that it was in the 1860s that the term ‘haptic’ arose, from the Greek haptikós, not just to touch, but to grasp or perceive. The hand of this new species of primate was set free for matters beyond locomotion, ultimately giving humankind ‘universal dominion’ over all other creatures, as Darwin wrote, quoting Bell. What a revolutionary idea! Friedrich Engels certainly thought so, as he wrangled the human hand into his dialectics of nature, his search for the material drivers of history. Bell’s anatomical textbook had shown the human hand grasping that all-important tool.
