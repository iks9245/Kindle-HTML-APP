But the moving junction may prove to be too much of a moving target. “Some people have always argued that invasion is a really bad drug target because it’s a very short process,” van Ooij said. Any drug with the ability to stop invasion would have a roughly 60-second window in which to do so, which means it would need to remain stable in the body for a long time or act extremely quickly, or both.

Ho’s team already has their sights set on another target. They plan to use similar cryo-electron microscopy techniques to solve the structure of other protein complexes involved in malaria infection, such as those adhesive proteins that keep infected blood cells from journeying to the spleen.
