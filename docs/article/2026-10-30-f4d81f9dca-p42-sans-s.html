So like X-ray jets coming out when a black hole’s cannibalizing a neighboring star. So these are star-sized black holes, not supermassive black holes. But it’s very indirect. You’re quite far out when you’re seeing those jets. You’re collecting X-rays. The jets are huge. You’re nowhere near honing into that shadowy region, which is the defining character of a black hole.

And so what they’re achieving by getting in so close is testing strong field gravity, a gravity where it’s even stronger than what you see when you’re looking at a star being cannibalized, surprisingly.

And so, that’s why people look at it as tests of general relativity because general relativity predicts the event horizon. That’s the prediction. That’s the golden ticket, you know? And indeed, that’s what they’re seeing. They’re seeing something a little bit bigger, a little bit outside because the light is orbiting a little bit further out, but so close that it is really an incredibly strong test of the success of that prediction.
