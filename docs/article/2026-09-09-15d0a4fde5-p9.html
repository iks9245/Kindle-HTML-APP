Continental divide

Oliveira’s convictions kept him and two later companions at their task, plodding north, year after year. At every opportunity, he spoke with energy about the dreams surrounding the construction of a highway connecting the continents, of an imminent hemispheric fraternity, and his remarks were published approvingly in papers in every nation he passed through. At his arrival in Washington, however, US officials insisted that what he claimed to have done was impossible. Most were considerate enough not to say it to his face, and he had the honour of meeting President Franklin Roosevelt and speaking to a large audience at the Pan-American Union building. But almost nobody believed Oliveira’s claims. Engineers from the US Bureau of Public Roads had struggled to walk segments of the route, let alone motor along it. And they rejected his maps, which they described as not inaccurate but belated. The Americans had already projected the official route through spaces their surveyors had not yet dared to go.
