But the damage runs deeper than that. When we cannot grieve a loss, we lose something else along with it: the chance to register what mattered to us. Grief is how we come to know what we loved in the presence of its absence. Or as the psychotherapist and grief worker Francis Weller puts it in The Wild Edge of Sorrow (2015):

Grief and love are sisters, woven together from the beginning. Their kinship reminds us that there is no love that does not contain loss and no loss that is not a reminder of the love we carry for what we once held close.
