However, what the ebrei Tedeschi really wanted was to live in Venice itself. But the Venetians were having none of it. Although they were happy enough to borrow money from Jewish bankers when it suited them, they still regarded the same bankers with undisguised hostility. In the Venetians’ eyes, Jews posed a clear and present danger to the Christian faith. If they were allowed to take up residence in the city, the thinking went, they could easily ‘infect’ unsuspecting believers – and thereby undermine the health of Christian society as a whole. There was no question of them being allowed in.
